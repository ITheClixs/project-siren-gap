\section{Identifiability and a ceiling on weight access}\label{sec:ident}

Let $\Thgen$ be the parameters with all $w_i \neq 0$, all $u_i \neq 0$, and no pair
$w_j = \pm w_i$.

\begin{theorem}[Generic identifiability, $L=1$]\label{thm:po2}
Let $\theta,\theta' \in \Thgen$ with widths $n, n'$ and $f_\theta = f_{\theta'}$ on some open
$U \subseteq \R^m$. Then $n = n'$ and $\theta' = g\theta$ for a \emph{unique} $g \in \Dinf \wr S_n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch; App.~\ref{app:po2} gives it in full]
$f_\theta - f_{\theta'}$ is entire and vanishes on an open set, hence everywhere. As tempered
distributions,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{f_\theta} \;=\; \beta\,\delta_0 \;+\; \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{u_i}{2\mathrm i}
\Big( e^{\mathrm i b_i}\,\delta_{w_i} \;-\; e^{-\mathrm i b_i}\,\delta_{-w_i} \Big),
\label{eq:atoms}
\end{equation}
an atomic measure whose $2n$ support points $\{\pm w_i\}$ are distinct and nonzero on $\Thgen$ and
whose coefficients are nonzero. Equality of transforms forces equal supports and coefficients: the
$\delta_0$ terms give $\beta=\beta'$, the punctured supports biject, and each matched pair solves to
either $(u',b') = (u,\, b+2\pi k)$ or $(-u,\, b+\pi+2\pi k)$, which is the $\rho/\tau$ family, or to
their $\sigma$-image.
\end{proof}

The parallel-frequency exclusion is not technical convenience: when $w_j = \pm w_i$ the two atoms in
\eqref{eq:atoms} share a support point and add as phasors, so separate recovery is genuinely
impossible. The result is \emph{non-asymptotic}: the only hypothesis is membership in $\Thgen$, an
explicit and measurable condition.

Theorem~\ref{thm:po2} is the sine-network entry in a classical line
\citep{hechtnielsen1990algebraic,sussmann1992uniqueness,fefferman1994reconstructing}, of which our
Fourier-atom argument is the periodic analogue. What is new is the \emph{phase} component: those
groups are finite, while ours contains an infinite translation subgroup $\pi\Z$ acting on the bias.
Because $\sin$ is $2\pi$-periodic the bias direction closes into a loop, and it is that loop which
makes canonicalization globally obstructed rather than obstructed only at ties.

\begin{conjecture}[Deep case]\label{conj:deep}
For $L\ge2$, off a proper analytic subset of parameter space, $f_\theta = f_{\theta'}$ implies
$\theta' = g\theta$ for some $g\in G$.
\end{conjecture}

Our attempt reduces it, via Jacobi--Anger, to uniqueness of a CP decomposition in a Bessel basis
and stalls on two lemmas. A pre-registered empirical attack (Appendix~\ref{app:s4e}) finds the
conjecture survives with positive evidence at width two and no counterexample, but that
identifiability has no empirical content at production width, because the basin of the true orbit
collapses with width.

\begin{proposition}[No continuous canonicalization]\label{prop:po5}
There is no continuous $\kappa : \Thgen \to \Thgen$ with $\kappa(\theta) \in \Orb(\theta)$ and
$\kappa(g\theta) = \kappa(\theta)$ for all $g \in \langle\sigma,\rho,\tau\rangle$. One hidden neuron
and $m=c=1$ suffice.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take $\theta_t = (1,t,1)$, $t\in[0,2\pi]$, and write $\kappa(\theta_t) = g_{d_t,j_t}\theta_t$.
Continuity of the first coordinate forces $d_t \equiv d$; of the third, the parity of $j_t$ is
constant; then $j_t$ is a continuous integer-valued function, hence constant. But
$\theta_{2\pi} = \tau_1\theta_0$, so invariance requires equal bias coordinates while
\eqref{eq:normalform} gives a difference of $(-1)^d 2\pi \neq 0$.
\end{proof}

This is the sine instance of the obstruction of \citet{dym2024impossibility}, and stronger in an
operationally relevant way: for the usual sorting canonicalizer the discontinuity appears at
sorting-key ties, while the periodic bias direction produces a loop obstruction already for a single
neuron.

\begin{proposition}[Completeness is function access]\label{prop:po6}
Assume the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:po2}. If $I : \Thgen \to X$ is $G$-invariant and
\emph{complete}, then $I = \bar I \circ R$ factors through the realization map
$R:\theta \mapsto f_\theta$, with $\bar I$ injective on $R(\Thgen)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:ceiling}
A complete-invariant weight-space perceiver receives \emph{exactly} the information of a
function-space perceiver. Any advantage of weight access must therefore be computational, or must
come from deliberately \emph{incomplete} invariants, or from non-pointwise processing.
\end{corollary}

Corollary~\ref{cor:ceiling} is a ceiling on the enterprise, not a refutation of it, and it is what
makes the FLOPs-matched comparison of \S\ref{sec:function} the appropriate test. It is derived
under the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:po2}, proved for $L=1$ only, so at $L=2$, where all our
corpora live, it is conditional on Conjecture~\ref{conj:deep}; \S\ref{sec:function} is therefore an
empirical result at $L=2$ rather than a consequence of the proved theorem.
