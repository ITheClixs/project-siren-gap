\section{The orbit-only intervention}\label{sec:orbit}

Take a corpus with \emph{no} initialization nuisance, \texttt{P-shared-det}, every INR fitted from
the same $\theta_0$. Hold each fitted network and its realized function fixed. Draw an independent
group element per INR and apply it:
\begin{equation}
\theta_i \;\longmapsto\; g_i\theta_i, \qquad g_i \sim \mu_B \ \text{i.i.d.}
\label{eq:orbit-intervention}
\end{equation}
Because the same networks and the same functions appear on both sides, and we verify the residual
functional gap is at machine precision, any degradation is caused by the group action and by nothing
else. Write $\Delta_{\mathrm{sym}}$ for that degradation. There is no uniform measure on the
infinite group, so $\mu_B$ is a family: windings $j \sim \mathrm{Unif}\{-B,\dots,B\}$, signs
$d \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)$, permutations uniform on $S_n$.

\paragraph{Group scatter reproduces nearly the whole gap.} Randomizing $g$ over the untouched
corpus takes the decoder from $94.36\%$ to $15.3\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:orbit}, Appendix~\ref{app:orbit}): $\Delta_{\mathrm{sym}} = 79.07$ at $B=0$ and
$79.09$ at $B=10$, flat in the winding bound, against an $80.4$-point observed gap on the same
dataset. But the two are not the same intervention, and the recoveries separate them. $\calign$
returns $86\%$ of $\Delta_{\mathrm{sym}}$ against $63\%$ of the observed gap; the invariant encoding
$72\%$ against $27\%$. Every treatment does markedly better against synthetic scatter than against
independent fits, which is the first direct evidence that the observed gap contains something the
group does not.

\paragraph{A residual survives, and it is a property of the reader.} W11b, a $G$-invariant reader
whose invariance we \emph{measure} rather than assume, scores $84.81\%$ on the untouched corpus and
$85.39\%$ on the same corpus after randomization, a difference of $0.59$ points that is seed noise.
The same reader scores $56.24\%$ on \texttt{P-random}. So a reader empirically blind to
$\Dinf \wr S_n$ still loses $28.6$ points between the two corpora, and that loss cannot be group
scatter.

That number is not a property of the corpora. Re-running the triple with W12, the phasor-graded
reader of \S\ref{sec:reader}, collapses it: $95.46\%$ on \texttt{P-shared-det} against $87.64\%$ on
\texttt{P-random}, a loss of $7.8$ points rather than $28.6$. We therefore report $7.8$ points as
\emph{the shared-versus-random difference of the strongest invariant reader we have built}, and
nothing more. It is not an upper bound on a latent non-symmetry share, because no such quantity is
defined here and no result says a stronger invariant reader must shrink the difference; an
unconstrained infimum over invariant readers would in any case be vacuous, since a constant
classifier achieves zero. The earlier reading, on which $28.6$ points measured what is not symmetry,
is withdrawn. What survives is qualitative: the loss is real, since the intervals do not overlap, so
it is at least $6.7$ points, and it is not group scatter. Against the $5.4$-point fit-quality
movement the function side sees between the same corpora (\S\ref{sec:function}), the margin is
$2.4$ points rather than the $23$ the weaker reader suggested.

\paragraph{Sign, permutation and winding.} With the permutation fixed to the identity,
$\Delta_{\mathrm{sym}}$ is still $62.9$ points at $B=0$ and $64.1$ at $B=10$, against $79.1$ for the
full group. These are \emph{nested} interventions rather than a factorial design, so they give
increments in one order: sign flips alone cost about $63$ points, adding windings up to $|j|=10$
costs about one more, and adding the relabelling of $32$ neurons costs about $15$ more. Two
registered rows are badly missed by this, and in the reverse direction to what we predicted.

The consequence cuts both ways. The $\Dinf$ component dominates the permutation component by four
to one, which is the empirical case for treating the sine group as more than $S_n$, but
\emph{within} $\Dinf$ almost all of it is the reflection generator $\sigma$, which monomial-matrix
frameworks already cover \citep{tran2024monomial}. The affine phase component, the piece outside
every linear classification and the one this paper's group-theoretic contribution turns on, costs
about one accuracy point at $B=10$. It is mathematically necessary for the identifiability statement
and empirically minor as a source of scatter, and we make those claims separately.
