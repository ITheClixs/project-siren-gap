\begin{abstract}
A classifier reading the raw weights of independently fitted implicit neural representations (INRs)
performs near chance, while the same reader on weights fitted from a shared initialization nearly
matches a pixel model; that gap is routinely attributed to parameter symmetry, and it has not been
measured. We characterize the group generated by the function-preserving transformations of sine
networks, showing that each hidden neuron carries an infinite dihedral action $\Dinf$ whose phase
component is affine and therefore lies outside the monomial-matrix actions previous classifications
cover, and we prove that at one hidden layer $\Dinf \wr S_n$ exhausts generic functional
equivalence. Exact orbit-valued reframing recovers $63\%$, $66\%$ and $32\%$ of the gap, but that is
relative to the reader and its training algorithm rather than a causal share, so we intervene
directly: randomizing the group while holding every network and its realized function fixed costs
$79$ of the $80$ points, which makes group scatter sufficient to reproduce almost the whole
degradation without identifying what fraction of the natural gap symmetry mediates. Acting on the
characterization, bias phasors reduce the winding to a parity and leave a finite grading a network
preserves layer by layer, giving a reader on raw parameters that we prove exactly invariant and
that is competitive rather than leading against published numbers for three standard benchmarks.
Querying the same INR at $64$ learned coordinates is nonetheless more accurate than every
weight-space pipeline we built and about $100\times$ cheaper, and all fits use constant-step Adam,
which our own sweep shows does not reach stationarity.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Impact Statement}

This paper studies how much of a measured degradation in weight-space classifiers is attributable to
parameter symmetry. The work is methodological and its experiments use standard public image
datasets converted into implicit neural representations, so we see no societal consequences that
require specific highlighting here beyond those common to machine learning research generally. One
practical implication is worth stating: several published results in this area are measured on
corpora whose construction makes the task easier than the realistic setting, and a reader that
appears to work may be exploiting that artifact rather than the structure it claims.
