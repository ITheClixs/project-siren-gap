\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:w12}}\label{app:w12}

\begin{proof}
Induct on the layers, with the hypothesis that every block satisfies the covariance
$f^{(\chi)} \mapsto \chi(g) P_g f^{(\chi)}$. \emph{(i)} A linear map applied along the feature
axis commutes with $P_g$ and with the scalar $\chi(g)$; a bias would add a constant to a block whose
sign flips, which is why bias is permitted only where $\chi \equiv 1$. \emph{(ii)} Products are
taken node-wise, so they commute with $P_g$, and characters are multiplicative,
$\chi_1(g)\chi_2(g) = (\chi_1\chi_2)(g)$, which is exactly the routing rule. \emph{(iii)} For odd
$\sigma$ and $\chi(g) = \pm1$, $\sigma(\chi(g) z) = \chi(g)\,\sigma(z)$, so covariance survives;
on $\chi = (0,0)$ the scalar is $+1$ and any $\sigma$ is admissible. \emph{(iv)} $W^2$ transforms
with character $(1,1)$ on its layer-1 index and $(1,0)$ on its layer-2 index and is permuted on both,
so each admitted channel sums a product whose total character matches its destination, and the sum
over the contracted index absorbs the permutation. \emph{(v)} Mean and max over the node axis are
$P_g$-invariant, and on $\chi = (0,0)$ the scalar is $+1$, so the pooled vector is fixed. The head
is then an arbitrary function of an invariant. Finally the winding enters the features only through
$\cos b$, $\sin b$, $\cos 2b$, $\sin 2b$, on which $b \mapsto b + \pi j$ acts by $(-1)^j$ or
trivially, so $j$ is absorbed into the characters and the argument is uniform in $|j|$.
\end{proof}

It is a statement about the architecture in exact arithmetic, so it is agnostic to the trained
weights but not to floating point. The audit covers the latter, and puts the relative logit movement
at $3\times10^{-6}$ on $512$ fitted networks under windings to $|j| = 40$. It also requires the
feature standardization to be scale-only: dividing each channel by a constant commutes with the sign
action, while subtracting a mean would not, so the implementation never centres a non-neutral
channel. In our implementation the odd nonlinearity is $\tanh$ and the neutral one is GELU.

\section{The phasor and grading ablation, in full}\label{app:square}

\paragraph{Effect of removing the layer grading.} Applying S7's argument to W12,
the matched control removes the grading and keeps everything else: same skeleton, same block shapes,
same phasor features, capacity re-solved by the same rule (width $122$, $1{,}888{,}212$ parameters).
It is genuinely not invariant, its logits moving $0.25$ relative under the group on fitted INRs
against W12's $3\times10^{-6}$, and it still reaches $s(\mathrm{W12u}) = \mathbf{0.858}$. The
grading is therefore worth $+0.059$ of W12's $0.917$, and the control on its own already beats
$\calign$'s $0.628$ and W11b's $0.526$.

The reading we take from that is not the one we expected. Both readers receive the same
exactly-invariant neutral block from the shared feature map ($\|w_i\|^2$, $\cos 2b_i$,
$\|u_k\|^2$ and the Gram diagonals), which moves by $3\times10^{-8}$ under $\Dinf$. What the grading
adds is the \emph{guarantee} that nothing outside that block reaches the head, and on this corpus
that guarantee is worth about six points of the eighty-point gap. The advantage does not belong to
enforcing equivariance in the layers. That is still the group characterization paying off, since the character
table is what says which coordinates those are, but it is a different mechanism from the one we
would have named, and the control is the only reason we can tell.

\paragraph{Effect of removing the phasor coordinates.} W12u varies the
grading and keeps the coordinates. The converse arm, W12b, keeps the graded skeleton, the block
structure and the edge coupling exactly, re-solves capacity by the same rule (width $186$,
$1{,}873{,}782$ parameters, within $0.06\%$ of W12), and changes only the feature map: the bias
enters \emph{raw} instead of phasor-lifted. It reaches $s(\mathrm{W12b}) = \mathbf{0.602}$. The
$0.265 \to 0.917$ step from a permutation-equivariant reader on raw weights therefore has three
identifiable parts. Filling the fourth cell of the square makes this a factorial design rather than
two one-at-a-time comparisons: W12ub, the same skeleton carrying neither the lift nor the grading,
reaches $s(\mathrm{W12ub}) = \mathbf{0.557}$.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
 & raw bias & phasor bias \\
\midrule
ungraded skeleton & $0.557$ & $0.858$ \\
graded skeleton   & $0.602$ & $0.917$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The phasor lift is worth $+0.301$ without the grading and $+0.315$ with it; the grading is
worth $+0.046$ on the raw bias and $+0.059$ on the phasor. The interaction is $\mathbf{+0.013}$, so
inside this skeleton the two ingredients are close to additive:
\[
\begin{aligned}
0.265 &\;\xrightarrow{\;+0.291\ \text{skeleton}\;}\; 0.557\\
&\;\xrightarrow{\;+0.301\ \text{phasor lift}\;}\; 0.858\\
&\;\xrightarrow{\;+0.059\ \text{grading}\;}\; 0.917 .
\end{aligned}
\]
Because W12ub carries neither ingredient, the first step is also the cleanest estimate this design
offers of what the message-passing skeleton alone buys, It is smaller than the $+0.337$ obtained by differencing
along the graded row alone, which folds the grading into the skeleton's share.

Both controls were registered before running, with their reading rules fixed, and between them they
score $5/5$ intervals. S10 committed that at $s \ge 0.75$ we would withdraw the claim that the
coordinates carry the win, at $s \le 0.55$ keep it, and in between report a split attribution
without picking the closer side; $0.602$ fell in between, so we do not claim the coordinates carry
it. S11 committed that an interaction outside $\pm0.25$ would retire the additive language
altogether; at $+0.013$ it does not. Within this skeleton, representing the bias in
symmetry-adapted coordinates and reading it with a graded message-passing architecture are worth
about the same, and the layer-level grading is worth little next to either.

Two scope conditions, both registered before the arm was run. First, W12b is handicapped by
construction: under the grading only the neutral block reaches the head, so a \emph{non}-character
feature such as a raw bias reaches it only through the even products the bilinear rounds form. The
arm therefore bounds the architecture's contribution \emph{within the graded skeleton}, not in
general. Second, it is not invariant, and the audit shows the failure growing with the winding exactly as
the construction predicts: relative logit moves of $6.2$, $1.7\times10^2$ and $2.4\times10^4$ at
$|j| \le 3, 10, 40$, against W12's $3\times10^{-6}$. A raw bias grows linearly in $\pi j$ where its
phasor does not. Its \emph{feature-level} neutral block is exactly
fixed under $\Dinf$ ($0.00$), so the invariance is lost precisely where the theory says it must be:
in the bilinear rounds, which route a non-character quantity into the neutral block that the head
reads.

\paragraph{Replacing the encoding's pooling helps substantially.} W10 was limited to $0.269$;
keeping its invariants and replacing sorted eigenvalue spectra with learned equivariant pooling
gives $0.526$, against $\calign$'s $0.628$. We do \emph{not} decompose that movement into a
``pooling'' part and an ``incompleteness'' part, because W10 and W11b differ in more than the
pooling: reader architecture, parameter count ($0.99$M against $1.85$M), relational capacity and
optimisation geometry all change together. The supported statement is the weaker one, that replacing
fixed spectral pooling with a learned equivariant reader over the same invariant objects
substantially improves accuracy, and the factorial ablation that would license a split
(matched-capacity MLP on the same objects; richer fixed invariant statistics; the same learned
pooling over non-invariant control objects) is left as future work. What the result does support is
a practical suggestion: a $G$-invariant equivariant reader needs no template and no assignment
problem, and lands within $0.10$ of alignment. W11b is therefore this paper's $\Dinf$-aware reader,
and the comparison W11a versus W11b is the one that isolates $G$-awareness at fixed reader family: $0.265$ against $0.526$. Its limitation is structural rather than architectural: it is invariant
because its \emph{input} already is, so it inherits whatever the invariant family discards, and a
reader that quotients $\Dinf$ on the parameters themselves (\S\ref{app:limits}) is the construction
we did not build.

All five registered intervals hit and all three probability calls resolved as registered, including
the one that would have withdrawn the paper's practical claim had it failed.

\section{The full decomposition ladder}\label{app:ladder}
% =====================================================================================

The ladder applies different \emph{feature maps} to the same underlying corpora and decodes each
with one frozen apparatus: a matched MLP $[D \to 1024 \to 512 \to 256 \to 10]$, GELU, dropout
$0.1$, AdamW $10^{-3}$, cosine schedule, early stopping on a held-out INR validation split. Only
the feature map changes.

\begin{center}\small
\footnotesize\begin{tabular}{@{}llp{4.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
rung & corpus & feature map \\
\midrule
P0 & -- & real pixels (the reference task) \\
P1 & shared-det & oracle render of the fitted INR (information-preservation control) \\
W1 & shared-det & raw flattened weights, \emph{no} nuisance (the raw-weight reference, not a ceiling over readers) \\
W2 & shared-stoch & raw weights, optimization noise only \\
W3 & random & raw weights, the realistic setting \\
W4 & random & $\csort$: exact canonicalization by neuron sort, template-free \\
W5 & random & $\calign$: exact canonicalization by sort $+$ sign/phase alignment to $\theta_0$ \\
W6 & random & raw weights $+$ bounded group augmentation at train time \\
W7 & random-K & $K$-marginalization: all 8 fitted views as training rows \\
W8 & random & canonicalize, \emph{then} augment inside the canonical frame \\
W9 & random & frame averaging over $R=64$ group elements \\
W10 & random & the exact $L{=}2$ invariants of Proposition~\ref{prop:deep} \\
X1 & both & train on W1 features, evaluate on W3 features, and the reverse \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The quantity of interest is the \textbf{algorithm-relative recoverable fraction}
\begin{equation}
f(\mathrm{W}k) \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{W}k - \mathrm{W3}}{\mathrm{W1} - \mathrm{W3}} \;\in\; \R,
\label{eq:f}
\end{equation}
the share of the shared-versus-random \emph{accuracy} gap that feature map $k$ recovers for this
learning algorithm. Absolute accuracies inherit the task ceiling; $f$ does not, which makes it the
object that can be compared across datasets.

\eqref{eq:f} is well defined only while the reader and its training procedure are held fixed, which
they are for every rung of this ladder: one frozen MLP, one schedule, one seed policy, and only the
feature map changing. Sections \ref{sec:reader} onward compare \emph{architectures}, so the same
symbol would mix numerator and denominator from different learning algorithms. There we write
\begin{equation}
s(A) \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{acc}_A(\texttt{P-random}) - \mathrm{W3}}{\mathrm{W1} - \mathrm{W3}}
\label{eq:s}
\end{equation}
and call it a \textbf{reference-normalized score}: the accuracy of architecture $A$ on the
independently initialized corpus, placed on the scale set by the frozen MLP's two raw-weight
anchors. It is a common yardstick, not a recovered share, and unlike $f$ it is not bounded by one,
as \S\ref{sec:orbit} shows when a reader beats W1 on the shared corpus. It is \emph{not} a causal
share of the gap either, for
reasons set out in \S\ref{sec:results}; negative values occur (W8, W9) and simply mean the map is
worse than raw weights.

\paragraph{Pre-registration.} Every rung definition, hypothesis, point prediction with an $80\%$
interval, seed count, exclusion rule and falsification condition was frozen in a committed,
hash-stamped document before any cell was computed, following the pre-registration practice
argued for in \citep{munafo2017manifesto,nosek2018preregistration}. Seed counts were sized from a
\emph{measured} paired-difference SD ($0.210$ points for rungs whose decoder input is a fixed
matrix, $0.721$ for rungs that redraw it each step), giving $n=5$ and $n=15$ respectively;
at $n=5$ the second class would have had TOST power $0.20$. Rung comparisons use paired-by-seed
$t$ and Wilcoxon tests with Holm correction \citep{holm1979simple}, equivalence via TOST
\citep{schuirmann1987tost}, and a label-shuffle control at W1, W3 and W5
\citep{zhang2017rethinking}. All registered predictions are scored, and deviations and exploratory
analyses are identified as such wherever they appear; the full ledger and its calibration are in
App.~\ref{app:calibration}.

The registrations are \emph{sequential}, not one joint confirmatory experiment, and the sequence
matters for how they should be read: the grayscale arm was registered first and its outcomes
informed the CIFAR-10 predictions, which in turn informed the mechanism and identifiability arms;
two identifiability rows were informed by an under-powered pilot and are flagged pilot-informed in
the ledger. Sample sizes are also structured. The $1.8$M fitted INRs are not $1.8$M independent
replications: the units of uncertainty are datasets, images, fit realizations and decoder seeds,
and the paired tests here resolve only the last of those.

\section{Is the invariant encoding's gain about invariance?}\label{app:control}\label{sec:control}
% =====================================================================================

W10 is both a nonlinear feature map and a $G$-invariant one, so its accuracy attributes nothing to
symmetry on its own; a map that is equally nonlinear and not invariant might do as well. S7 supplies
the control the ladder was missing. Rung \textbf{W10c} emits, coordinate for coordinate, the same
monomials in $(w,u)$ at the same trigonometric orders as W10, pooled by the same eigenvalue spectra
under the same $\|w\|^2$ sort key, at the same dimension, decoded by the same frozen apparatus. Only
the parity class of each trigonometric factor changes: $\sin b_i \sin b_l \to \cos b_i \cos b_l$
against the Gram, $\cos b_i w_i \to \sin b_i w_i$, $\sin 2b_i w_i \to \cos 2b_i w_i$, and similarly
per neuron and in layer two. The three matrices remain symmetric and still transform as
$M \mapsto PMP^\top$, so W10c is \emph{still exactly permutation-invariant} and is broken only in
$\Dinf$; the test suite asserts both halves, at a relative move above $10^{-2}$ under the full group
and below $10^{-5}$ under permutations alone. The difference between the two rungs is therefore
attributable to the $\Dinf$ component specifically.

\input{tables/control_table}

\paragraph{Most of the encoding's CIFAR-10 gain is invariance; a substantial minority is not.}
$f(\mathrm{W10c}) = 0.125$ on MNIST against W10's $0.269$, and $0.216$ on CIFAR-10 against
$0.534$. All three predictions hit ($0.14\,[0.02,0.28]$, $0.22\,[0.05,0.42]$ and, for
the difference the review actually asked for, $0.31\,[0.11,0.48]$ against an observed $0.318$. The
pre-committed falsifier ($f(\mathrm{W10c}) \ge f(\mathrm{W10}) - 0.05$ on CIFAR-10, which would
have voided every symmetry reading of the CIFAR-10 encoding result) did not fire.

So of W10's $0.534$ on CIFAR-10, $0.318$ is attributable to quotienting $\Dinf$ and $0.216$ is
what the same nonlinearity buys without it. On MNIST the split is $0.144$ against $0.125$. The
encoding's advantage over an exact reframing on natural images is therefore a genuine symmetry
effect and not a feature-engineering artifact, but it is not \emph{purely} a symmetry effect
either, and the second registered probability call is the one that says so.

\paragraph{Contribution of the nonlinearity, and the W10--W4 comparison.}
P-S7-B put $0.55$ on $f(\mathrm{W10c}) \le f(\mathrm{W4})$ on CIFAR-10, predicting that breaking $\Dinf$ invariance would cost more than the nonlinear pooling buys. It resolves false: $0.216$ against
$\csort$'s $0.108$. A feature map with the same nonlinearity and \emph{no} $\Dinf$ invariance
already doubles template-free canonicalization. The registration fixed in advance that this must be
stated wherever W10 is compared with W4, so: the W10-versus-W4 comparison is not a clean
symmetry-handling comparison, because the two differ in nonlinearity as well. The clean comparison
is W10 against W10c, and it is the one we now quote.

\section{The crossover: alignment and invariance trade places}\label{app:crossover}\label{sec:crossover}
% =====================================================================================

The two exact methods move in \emph{opposite} directions as the signal gets harder:
\begin{center}\small
\resizebox{\columnwidth}{!}{%
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
& MNIST & FashionMNIST & CIFAR-10 \\
\midrule
$f(\mathrm{W5})$, alignment to a fixed reference & 0.628 & 0.664 & \textbf{0.325} \\
$f(\mathrm{W10})$, exact $L{=}2$ invariants      & 0.269 & 0.428 & \textbf{0.534} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
}
\end{center}

Alignment halves; the invariant encoding nearly doubles, and on CIFAR-10 it \emph{overtakes}
alignment. This is not a post-hoc observation about which method happened to win. The
pre-registration for the CIFAR arm carried an explicit probability call, $P = 0.60$ that
$f(\mathrm{W10})_{\text{CIFAR}} > f(\mathrm{W10})_{\text{MNIST}} = 0.269$, justified by the
encoding's algebra rather than by extrapolating the two observed values: with $c = 3$ output
channels each neuron's outgoing vector $u_i \in \R^3$ carries strictly more $\Dinf$-visible
structure than the $c = 1$ case, so the invariants built from it have more to see (and the
encoding grows from $D = 320$ to $D = 384$). That call resolved correctly. The companion call, that the grayscale ordering $f(\mathrm{W5}) > f(\mathrm{W10})$ would persist, $P = 0.65$, resolved incorrectly, and the crossover is the reason.

\paragraph{Ablation of the registered channel mechanism.} P-C1-B attributed W10's rise to
the three output channels. We can test that \emph{within} the CIFAR corpus, without new fitting,
by changing only what the encoder is allowed to read (exploratory, not registered):

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llrr@{}}
\toprule
arm & what the encoder reads & $D$ & $f(\mathrm{W10})$ \\
\midrule
full        & all three output channels                & 384 & 0.534 \\
truncated   & output channel 0 only                    & 320 & 0.457 \\
averaged    & the three channels' mean                 & 320 & 0.425 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Truncating to one channel, which restores exactly the grayscale encoding dimension, costs only $0.077$. Against the $0.265$ rise from MNIST's $0.269$ to CIFAR's $0.534$, the channel count
therefore explains about $29\%$; the other $71\%$ survives at $D = 320$ and is a property of the
corpus, not of $c$: the mechanism we predicted operates, but it is far smaller than we expected. (The \emph{averaged} arm is
the useful control: it has the same dimension as \emph{truncated} and strictly more of the
network's information, yet does \emph{worse}, so the effect is not ``more feature dimensions'' and
not ``more information'': channel-averaging cancels the per-channel sign structure that
$(\sin b_i)u_i$ exists to carry.)

\paragraph{Fit length as a candidate explanation.} The obvious confound is fit length: the
CIFAR corpora were frozen at $1000$ steps against $300$ for the grayscale ones, and a fit that
travels further from $\theta_0$ should be harder to align back to it. We measured the travel
directly on the shared-initialization corpora, with no new fitting. It is
\emph{indistinguishable} across datasets: median relative travel
$\|\theta_T - \theta_0\| / \|\theta_0\|$ of $0.186$ (MNIST), $0.191$ (FashionMNIST) and $0.187$
(CIFAR-10), with median layer-1 direction cosine to the initialization of $0.998$, $0.999$ and
$0.999$ respectively, and CIFAR-10 is if anything the \emph{least} moved. The extra $700$ steps did
not translate into extra displacement, so the drop in $f(\mathrm{W5})$ is not a fit-length
artifact and the laziness that makes alignment work at all is equally present everywhere.

\paragraph{It is not the output-channel count either: a conjecture of ours, tested and withdrawn.} An earlier draft of this paper conjectured a mechanism: $\calign$ matches neurons by
correlating their layer-1 \emph{activations}, a statistic blind to the outgoing structure, which is exactly the part that grows with $c$, and exactly what W10's Gram coupling reads. That predicts
alignment should recover once $c=1$. We registered it as a falsifiable prediction
(\texttt{S1-gray}, probability calls at $0.35$ and $0.45$) and built the corpus that tests it:
\textbf{luminance CIFAR-10}, the identical images at the identical geometry, architecture and fit
budget, with the output-channel count changed from three to one.

\input{tables/gray_table}

The conjecture is \textbf{wrong}, and the registration fixed in advance that both calls resolving
against it would withdraw rather than qualify it. At $c=1$ the recovery fraction is $f(\mathrm{W5}) = 0.324$, \emph{identical} to three decimals to the $0.324$ measured at $c=3$ on the same images, and the crossover does not reverse ($f(\mathrm{W10}) = 0.493$, still above W5). Luminance CIFAR behaves like
RGB CIFAR, not like the grayscale corpora. Both probability calls resolved false.

The arm also removes one alternative explanation. Dropping channels makes the fit
over-parameterised, taking median render PSNR from $40$ to $59.8$\,dB, so this corpus is fitted
\emph{much more accurately} than MNIST at $39.2$\,dB and still aligns far worse. Higher fidelity
does not buy alignment recovery.

\paragraph{Remaining candidate explanation.} Three candidate causes for the cross-dataset behaviour have now been
tested and eliminated: fit length (parameter travel is identical across corpora), output-channel
count (this arm), and render fidelity (this arm, in the helpful direction). What remains is image statistics, natural scenes versus centred strokes and silhouettes. We do not offer a replacement
mechanism: the one we did offer was well motivated by the algebra and false, and a second
explanation fitted to the same evidence would carry no more weight than the first.

\paragraph{Practical implication for method choice.} On low-complexity grayscale corpora, choosing a good orbit
representative is the dominant lever and an exact invariant front-end is a poor second. On natural
images the ranking reverses, at either channel count. A weight-space method that fixes one strategy
will therefore look much better on some benchmarks than others for reasons that have nothing to do
with its reader architecture, and, as this arm shows, nothing to do with the obvious surface
properties of the corpus either.

\section{Mechanism: the fit map does not leave its initialization}\label{app:mechanism}
% =====================================================================================

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig2_mechanism.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Why the initialization dominates.} (a) The profiled loss of the one-neuron
microcosm, certified against quadrature to $5.6\times10^{-16}$; its zero set is exactly the
$\Dinf$ orbit, and it carries 19 spurious minima besides. (b) The fraction of initializations
reaching the global orbit is non-monotone in the initialization range, peaking at range
$\approx\omega$, and this replicates across Nelder--Mead, Adam and plain gradient descent.
(c) At the setting our corpora are actually fitted with, the median parameter travel is
$\approx0.24$ \emph{independently of the initialization range}: the fit stays in its
initialization's neighbourhood.}
\label{fig:mechanism}
\end{figure*}

Why should the initialization matter so much more than the trajectory? We answer in a microcosm
where everything is computable (Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}): one neuron, $g(t) = u\sin(wt+b)+c$ fitted to
$y(t) = A\sin(\omega t + \varphi) + c_0$ on $[-1,1]$. Profiling out the linear parameters
$(u,c)$ gives a closed form $\mathcal{L}^*(w,b)$ whose Gram entries are elementary; we certify it
against numerical quadrature to $5.6\times10^{-16}$. Its zero set is \emph{exactly} the $\Dinf$
orbit of the realizing parameters, and it has $19$ additional spurious local minima.

Two findings follow. First, \textbf{basin capture is non-monotone} in the initialization range,
with a sweet spot at range $\approx \omega$, and this replicates across optimizer classes
(global-capture fractions $0.00/0.20/0.56/0.31$ for converged Adam, $0.00/0.18/0.58/0.33$ for
plain gradient descent and $0.00/0.26/0.62/0.32$ for Nelder--Mead on the profiled surface, at
ranges $2/5/10/20$). Second, \textbf{at the setting our corpora are actually fitted with}
(Adam, $10^{-3}$, $300$ steps) \emph{every} initialization ends unconverged, with endpoint
gradient norm $\approx 0.5$--$0.7$ and median displacement $|\Delta w| \approx 0.24$
\emph{independently of the initialization range}: the fit stays in its initialization's
neighbourhood, so the final weights are close to the initialization plus a signal-dependent
perturbation. A shared initialization then means a shared frame, and independent initializations
mean independently scattered ones, which is what the null W1$-$W2 rung reports.

We describe this as small displacement and not as a lazy or NTK regime
\citep{jacot2018ntk,chizat2019lazy}, because displacement alone does not establish one: that would
need the linearization error, the kernel drift, or the linearized model's own fit, none of which we
measure (\S\ref{app:limits}). The claim here is the weaker and sufficient one, that the fit map's output is dominated by its initialization argument at this budget.

\begin{remark}[A sub-claim that was an artifact]
Our first census reported ``$100\%$ degenerate-ridge capture at range $2$''. Re-running under
gradient methods rather than Nelder--Mead on the profiled surface shows ridge capture is $0.00$:
those runs are still \emph{descending}, not sitting in a $w\approx0$ basin. An
\texttt{unconverged} class had to be added; without it we would have made a false claim about the
landscape. The headline non-monotonicity survives; the sub-claim does not.
\end{remark}

\section{Does the recoverable fraction survive convergence?}\label{app:convergence}
% =====================================================================================

Every ladder cell in this paper is measured on fits that end \emph{unconverged}. The corpora at
$300$ steps end with a median relative endpoint gradient norm of $5.3\times10^{-3}$, and the
microcosm at the same budget ends with $\|\nabla\|\approx0.5$--$0.7$. If the recoverable fraction is
a property of that regime rather than of independently fitted INRs generally, the paper's scope is
much narrower than its claims. S8 tests this directly.

Same images, same architecture, same optimizer (Adam $10^{-3}$, full batch, deterministic), same
seed policy; only the step budget changes, over $\{300, 1000, 3000, 10000\}$, on both protocols. The
corpus is reduced to $10$k/$2$k/$2$k so the largest budget is affordable, which makes the
$300$-step arm at \emph{that} size the internal control, so comparisons are within the sweep, never
against the full-corpus ladder whose W1 is decoded from $55$k rows.

Render fidelity cannot settle the question, because an MNIST INR here has $1185$ parameters against
$784$ targets and can interpolate the sampled grid while sitting far from a stationary point: a
pilot puts $300$ steps at $37.3$\,dB and $1000$ at $63.6$\,dB, with both far from stationarity. We
therefore added a stationarity measure to the fitter, the relative endpoint gradient norm
$\|\nabla_\theta \mathcal L_i(\theta)\|/\|\theta\|$ of each INR's own full-batch loss, and report it alongside PSNR and relative parameter travel at every budget.

\input{tables/sweep_table}

\paragraph{Stationarity was not reached, so the convergence question stays open.} This is the outcome S8 §4 pre-committed to reporting if P-S8-C failed, and P-S8-C failed.
The registration asked for a $10\times$ fall in the median relative endpoint gradient norm between
$300$ and $10000$ steps as evidence that the sweep moves toward stationarity rather than merely
fitting harder. The observed ratio is $1.17$ on \texttt{P-random} and $0.91$ on
\texttt{P-shared-det}: no fall at all, and on the shared corpus a slight \emph{rise}. We therefore
do not read the accuracy numbers at $10000$ steps as measurements ``after convergence'', and the
question that review asked, whether the recoverable fraction survives convergence, remains
open. What follows are statements about the \emph{budget}, which is what this sweep varied.

\paragraph{Why stationarity is not reached: the optimizer.} Fit
quality is not monotone in budget: median render PSNR rises $27$\,dB from $300$ to $1000$ steps and
then \emph{falls} back $6$\,dB by $10000$, on both protocols, while the endpoint gradient norm falls
$26\times$ and then climbs back to $0.86$ of its $300$-step value on \texttt{P-random} and to
$1.10$ of it on \texttt{P-shared-det}. The fitter is constant-learning-rate Adam
with no schedule (\S\ref{sec:bench}), and Adam's step size is set by a ratio of moments, so it
does not shrink as the gradient does: past the end of descent the iterate stops converging and
starts diffusing in a band whose width is set by the learning rate. The $1000$-step corpus catches
the end of descent; the larger budgets sample the band. Adding steps under this fitter therefore
cannot produce stationarity at any budget, and the repair is a decaying schedule or an explicit
per-INR stationarity stopping rule, neither of which we ran.

This mechanism, and the three scoring consequences it implies, were registered in addendum~02 after
the $300$, $1000$ and $3000$ arms were decoded and before any $10000$-step cell existed: P-S8-C
resolving false at $0.72$, H-S8-6 missing high at $0.70$, H-S8-7 missing low at $0.75$, and the two
$f(\mathrm{W5})$ intervals hitting at $0.85$ and $0.88$. All five resolved as called, at a mean
Brier score of $0.054$. A sixth call, on median PSNR, was struck out
before scoring: the corpus generator prints per-shard PSNR into the same log the decode writes to,
so that quantity had been seen while the job was being monitored. The declaration is in the
addendum.

\paragraph{Conclusions the budget sweep does support.} Three things, none of which requires convergence.
$f(\calign)$ declines monotonically across a $33\times$ budget, $0.502$, $0.489$, $0.470$,
$0.459$, for a total of $\mathbf{-0.043}$: far smaller than the $-0.15$ we registered, and
comfortably inside its interval. Per the pre-commitment for that branch, the decline is reported as a real
budget dependence with its magnitude, and every ladder number in this paper is accordingly labelled
as measured at the frozen $300$-step configuration. The invariant encoding is flatter still
($f(\mathrm{W10})$: $0.249$, $0.253$, $0.258$, $0.252$), consistent with its not referencing
$\theta_0$. The falsifier did not fire and is not close. It would have required $f(\calign) < 0.15$ at the
largest budget, making the recoverable fraction a property of the early-stopped regime and
rescoping every ladder claim.

The third is the most informative, and it explains why the first two are so flat. \emph{Relative
parameter travel from $\theta_0$ saturates almost immediately}: $0.186$, $0.194$, $0.194$, $0.197$
across the whole sweep. Alignment to $\theta_0$ keeps working not because the fits are
under-trained but because they never leave $\theta_0$'s neighbourhood, at any budget this fitter
can reach. That measures directly the lazy regime the mechanism section infers from displacement
(\S\ref{app:mechanism}), and it is stronger than the registration anticipated. It remains a
statement about where $33\times$ more Adam steps take a fit, not about where a converged fit would
sit. S8 scores $\mathbf{6/8}$ intervals, and both misses are the stationarity diagnostics
themselves (H-S8-6 high, H-S8-7 low): the same finding, read through the registration.

\section{Does identifiability have empirical content at depth two?}\label{app:s4e}
% =====================================================================================

Everything above rests on a theorem proved at $L=1$ while every experiment is $L=2$. The
pre-registered attack on that gap (S4e) asks the question empirically: if two two-layer sine
networks realise nearly the same function, are their parameters nearly related by an element of
$G$?

\paragraph{The alignment instrument and its control.} A large residual after
$\calign$ proves nothing, because $\calign$ is a heuristic choice of representative. We therefore
minimise over the group directly. Given the other layers fixed, the optimum for one layer is
\emph{exact}: the per-neuron $\Dinf$ cost
$\|(-1)^dw_i - w^*_t\|^2 + ((-1)^db_i + \pi j - b^*_t)^2 + \|(-1)^{d+j}u_i - u^*_t\|^2$
depends on $j$ only through its parity, so four $(d,\text{parity})$ cases give the exact minimum
over the whole infinite group, and the permutation is then a Hungarian assignment on those
per-pair minima. Layers are swept by coordinate descent from several restarts.

The control is planted recovery: take $\theta$, apply a known $g$, and demand the search return
machine zero. It does, at every width, to $\approx4\times10^{-8}$ relative. This control earned its place: the first confirmatory launch \emph{failed} it (coordinate descent stalls on about
$10\%$ of width-2 pairs), which tripped the registration's own void condition, and that run was
discarded rather than reported. Restarts fixed it.

\input{tables/s4e_table}

\paragraph{Local recovery is well conditioned; the basin is not.} Perturbing a network by a
relative $\varepsilon$ and measuring both residuals gives $\kappa = R_\theta/R_f$, the local
condition number of the inverse map. It is \emph{small} and \emph{decreases} with width
($0.042 \to 0.0055$ from $n=2$ to $n=32$), so the forward map $\theta\mapsto f$ is strongly
expansive and local recovery is easy, the opposite of what the Bessel--Vandermonde
ill-conditioning in our own proof memo might suggest, and a distinction worth keeping: that
determinant governs the \emph{global} recovery system, not the local Jacobian. What collapses with
width instead is the \emph{basin}. Started a distance $\varepsilon=10^{-5}$ from the true orbit,
$78$--$91\%$ of runs return to it at $n\le8$, $44\%$ at $n=16$, and \textbf{none} at $n=32$, where
the optimiser instead walks from $R_\theta = 10^{-5}$ out to $1.3\times10^{-1}$ while the function
barely improves. That is not a training-budget artifact: a control at five times the step count
leaves it unchanged ($0\%$ at $n=32$, identical $R_f$ floor; open markers in
Fig.~\ref{fig:s4e}b).

\paragraph{Independent restarts witness the orbit only at the smallest width.} At $n=2$, one of
$128$ students converged to $R_f = 5.9\times10^{-8}$ and, after optimal alignment, agreed with its
teacher to $R_\theta = 1.2\times10^{-7}$, which is float32 relative epsilon, a maximum per-coordinate
relative disagreement of $2.8\times10^{-6}$, and $2.6\times10^{-7}$ of the unrelated-network scale
at that width. That is recovery to six or seven significant figures, and it is direct positive
evidence for Conjecture~\ref{conj:deep}. At every larger width no restart came close: the best
functional residual jumps to $10^{-2}$ and the orbit residual sits in the band occupied by
\emph{entirely unrelated} networks (Fig.~\ref{fig:s4e}a). Combined with the warm-start arm, which
returns $78$--$91\%$ of the time at those same widths when started \emph{inside} the basin, the
reading is specific: the basin does not become shallower with width, its \emph{volume} collapses
relative to what random initialisation samples.

\paragraph{The registered criterion fired, and it was wrong to.} Read literally, that $n=2$ student
satisfies the pre-registered falsification criterion: $R_f < 10^{-5}$ and
$R_\theta = 1.2\times10^{-7} > 20\kappa R_f = 5.0\times10^{-8}$. It is a false positive, and the
criterion is at fault in two independent ways. It is \emph{ratio-only with no absolute floor}, so
as $R_f$ approaches machine epsilon the threshold falls below the smallest residual a float32
aligner can represent and \emph{any} exact recovery fires it. And $\kappa$ is the \emph{wrong null}
for an optimiser residual: it was measured on random perturbation directions, whereas a minimiser's
residual lies in the flattest directions of the loss, exactly where $R_f$ is least sensitive to $R_\theta$, so $R_\theta/R_f > \kappa$ is expected for any converged minimiser ($2.10$ observed
against $\kappa = 0.042$). We report the firing, the defect, and a post-hoc amendment adding an
absolute floor $R_\theta > 10^{-3}$, marked as post-hoc with the frozen text unaltered, and score
the registered probability call against the criterion \emph{as written}.

\paragraph{The production arm.} At production width, two independent fits \emph{of the same image}
sit at $R_\theta = 0.279$ after the registered alignment procedure. Two fits of \emph{different}
images sit at $0.280$. The difference is $-0.001$. The alignment is exact for one layer conditional
on the other, but alternating between layers is not guaranteed to reach the global optimum over the
product group, and planted recovery certifies the solver only on planted transformations. These are
therefore the smallest distances the registered solver found, and they bound the true orbit
distances from above. Read that way, a same-image pair is not measurably closer than an unrelated
pair under this procedure, which is the S1 ladder's W1-versus-W3 gap seen from parameter space
rather than from a decoder.

\paragraph{What this licenses.} Conjecture~\ref{conj:deep} survives, with one width's worth of
direct positive evidence and no counterexample. But identifiability at $L=2$ has \emph{no empirical
content at production width}: the configuration that would witness it is not reachable, and the
optimiser will leave the true orbit even when placed on it. What this supports is that the $L=1$ theorem is the guarantee we have, and that the $L=2$ corpora
this paper decodes are governed by basin structure rather than by identifiability.

\paragraph{Weight-space architectures.} DWSNets \citep{navon2023dws}, NFN/NFT
\citep{zhou2023nfn,zhou2023nft}, graph metanetworks \citep{lim2024gmn,kofinas2024graph},
universal neural functionals \citep{zhou2024unf} and ScaleGMN \citep{kalogeropoulos2024scalegmn}
build permutation equivariance, and sometimes sign or scaling equivariance, into the reader.
\citet{tran2024monomial} classify monomial-matrix symmetries and prove maximality among linear
actions, which is the closest statement to Theorem~\ref{thm:po1} in the literature; what it cannot
reach is the affine phase component, and what it does not attempt is the empirical question of how
much of the difficulty the group accounts for.

\paragraph{Symmetry, alignment and canonicalization.} Git Re-Basin \citep{ainsworth2023rebasin}
and the linear-mode-connectivity line \citep{entezari2022lmc} align networks by permutation for
merging; our $\calign$ generalizes the assignment step to $\Dinf \wr S_n$ using the Hungarian
algorithm \citep{kuhn1955hungarian}. Learned canonicalization \citep{kaba2023canonicalization} and
frame averaging \citep{puny2022frame} are the standard principled alternatives; W9 is frame
averaging measured in this setting, and it recovers nothing. \citet{dym2024impossibility} give the
general impossibility of continuous canonicalization, of which Proposition~\ref{prop:po5} is the
sine instance. \citet{lim2024empirical} remove symmetries by \emph{architecture}; we remove them
by canonicalization on a fixed architecture, which is the complementary intervention and the one
that leaves the function untouched.

\paragraph{Concurrent 2026 work.} Three preprints bear directly on results here; our engagement
with them is through the close-read memos released with this code.
\citet{wsn2026expressivity} prove approximation-theoretic universality for permutation-invariant
weight-space networks. Proposition~\ref{prop:po6} is
\emph{not} a competing universality theorem: it is a quotient factorization, and all of its
content comes from Theorem~\ref{thm:po2}, without which it says nothing about sine networks;
\citet{quasiequiv2026} supply the maximal-symmetry-group vocabulary in which
Theorem~\ref{thm:po2} is stated; and \citet{chiba2026observable} also read symmetry off weights through Gram-type observables, and
argue that what is visible from weights is an \emph{observable} symmetry set fixed by the
parameters, the positional encoding and the readout rather than the true group. Their setting is
MLPs on 2-D signed distance functions with positional encodings, not sine networks, and they make
no invariance claim of the kind in Proposition~\ref{prop:deep}; the shared ingredient is the use of
a Gram to cancel a layer's action, which is standard. Finally, \citet{clustered2026} report that in
a meta-learned regime the
bias column is a causal readout route while weight-space geometry does not predict reader accuracy, a dissociation that runs counter to the mechanism of \S\ref{app:mechanism} and was named in
our pre-registrations.

\paragraph{INR corpora and the shared-initialization question.} inr2vec \citep{deluigi2023inr2vec}
relies on shared-initialization corpora; \citet{papa2024howtotrain} intervene on fit
hyperparameters and observe downstream deltas across a large benchmark. Their shared-versus-random
comparison is one rung-pair of our ladder (W1 vs W3), and we replicate it seed-for-seed as a
pre-registered anchor before decomposing it. \citet{shamsian2024augmentations} treat the nuisance
by augmentation; W6 measures that treatment against an exact alternative and finds it recovers
$5\%$ where reframing recovers $63\%$. Approaches that avoid the nuisance by construction, namely functa \citep{dupont2022functa} and hypernetwork or meta-learned regimes, change the data-generating process, and are reference points rather than rungs.

\section{Limitations and open problems, in full}\label{app:limits}
% =====================================================================================

\textbf{W11a is a member of the equivariant family, not its strongest member.} Our
permutation-equivariant reader is two rounds of bipartite message passing with mean/max pooling,
capacity-matched to the frozen decoder. Published architectures in this family are richer: NFN's
equivariant linear layers realise many more parameter-sharing patterns between weight-space
tensors than message passing does, and graph metanetworks operate on the full computation graph
with positional encodings and per-edge features we do not use. So a result in which W11a falls
short of an exact change of frame bounds \emph{this} construction at \emph{this} capacity on
\emph{one} corpus; it is not a proof that equivariance cannot close the gap, and we do not state it
as one. What it does establish is that the obvious equivariant construction, given the same
parameter budget as the decoder it replaces, does not substitute for choosing a good orbit representative, and that the phase component of $G$, which none of these architectures covers
for sine networks, is a plausible reason.

\textbf{W12's gain is now attributed, but only within one skeleton.} Both controls have been run
(\S\ref{sec:reader}): the layer grading is worth $+0.059$, the phasor coordinates $+0.315$ and the
graded message-passing architecture $+0.337$ of the $0.265 \to 0.917$ step. The square is now complete, and its interaction of $+0.013$ is what licenses reading the
parts additively. What it still does not license is a general statement about coordinates versus
architecture: every cell shares one message-passing skeleton, so $+0.291$ is what \emph{this}
skeleton buys over a permutation-equivariant reader rather than a claim about architectures at
large, and the graded row remains handicapped in the sense that a non-character feature reaches the
head only through even products. The numbers also come from one protocol, and from MNIST for the
$2\times2$ specifically.

\textbf{The $\Dinf$-aware reader is built at $L=2$ only, and by one route.} W12
(\S\ref{sec:reader}) quotients the group on the raw parameters, and it is the strongest weight-space result in this paper, but its grading is derived for two hidden layers, and extending it needs
one $\Z_2\times\Z_2$ per layer with the coupling matrices graded on both sides, which we have not
written down for $L\ge3$. A second route we did not take reads the shallow network as the atomic
measure of \eqref{eq:atoms}, whose atoms at $\pm w_i$ with coefficient proportional to
$u_i e^{\mathrm i b_i}$ quotient the phase shift automatically and turn orientation reversal into
complex conjugation; that is likely the cleaner object and it is not obviously equivalent to the
graded construction. W12 is now run on four corpora and on both MNIST protocols, and the ordering
holds on all of them, so the ordering is not an artifact of the corpus the reader was designed on. What remains dataset-bound is the $2\times2$ attribution, which was run on MNIST only.

\textbf{The non-symmetry residual is bounded above and never measured.} The orbit intervention
establishes that some accuracy loss survives removing the group, but its size is whatever the best
invariant reader we happen to have built leaves behind: $28.6$ points with W11b, $7.8$ with W12
(\S\ref{sec:orbit}). We do not know how these figures relate to any corpus-level non-symmetry
quantity. They are not bounds on one: no such estimand is defined here, and no result says a
stronger invariant reader must leave a smaller difference. At $7.8$ points the margin over the
$5.4$-point fit-quality movement visible from the function side is $2.4$ points. What would settle
it is not a better reader but a corpus pair matched on fit quality, namely refits of the \emph{same} functions from independent initializations held to the same render PSNR, which we did not build.

\textbf{Signal complexity remains confounded with image statistics.} CIFAR-10 differed from the
grayscale corpora in three ways: image statistics, output-channel count and fit budget. Two are now ruled out, fit budget by the travel measurement and channel count by the luminance arm, both in \S\ref{app:crossover}, which leaves image statistics as the surviving candidate and no
experiment here that isolates it. A $c=1$ natural-image corpus at the grayscale fit budget, or a
synthetic corpus that interpolates image statistics at fixed everything else, is what would.

\textbf{The mechanism is evidenced by displacement, not by linearization.} \S\ref{app:mechanism}
argues the fits stay in their initialization's neighbourhood, and measures relative parameter travel
and direction cosine. Those are not the standard diagnostics of a lazy or NTK regime: we do not
measure Jacobian or kernel drift, the error of the first-order linearization, or the accuracy of the
corresponding linearized model. The production fits are also not at a stationary point, since the microcosm at the corpus budget ends with $\|\nabla\|\approx0.5$--$0.7$, so what we have shown may
be specific to high-fidelity but early-stopped fits. The step-budget half of that experiment is now
run (\S\ref{app:convergence}) and answers less than it was meant to: across a $33\times$ budget the
recoverable fraction moves $-0.043$ and travel saturates at $0.19$, but the endpoint gradient norm
does not fall, so the sweep never reaches stationarity.

We then attempted the other half and \emph{failed to complete it}, which we report because the
attempt bounds how hard the remaining gap is. A separate pre-registration (S12) specified a
cosine-decayed schedule, a per-INR stationarity stop at a relative gradient norm of $10^{-4}$,
three independently seeded replications of both protocols, and a validity gate to be evaluated on
the corpora \emph{before} anything was decoded. The fitter behaved as intended: median render PSNR rose from $58$\,dB to $68$--$71$\,dB and the median relative gradient norm fell roughly
$50\times$ against the $10000$-step arm, to $1.0$--$1.4\times10^{-4}$ on five of six corpora, and the gate still failed, because the registered threshold was $10^{-4}$ and the corpora sat just
above it, with one corpus at $7.3\times10^{-4}$ and one replication also breaking the
render-matching condition. Per the registration nothing was decoded, and no number from those
corpora appears in this paper.

A second attempt doubled the step cap to $12000$ at the same threshold, staged so that one
replication would decide whether the other two were fitted. It came out \emph{worse}: the median
relative gradient norm rose to $3.7$ and $3.8\times10^{-4}$ on the two protocols, from $1.19$ and
$1.31\times10^{-4}$ at half the budget, while median render PSNR was unchanged to the decimal at
$69.3$ and $70.2$\,dB. Twice the budget therefore bought fits that sit further from a stationary
point and reconstruct the image exactly as well. We stopped there, as the addendum required, rather
than searching for a third schedule.

So the honest position is narrower than ``not attempted'' and weaker than ``settled''. A fitter
that anneals its step size gets within a factor of about $1.3$ of a bar that a constant-step fitter
misses by $60\times$, but the remainder is \emph{not} a matter of budget: doubling it moved the
fits away from stationarity. Three schedules now agree that what obstructs convergence here is the
optimizer's geometry on these objectives rather than the number of steps, which is the same
conclusion \S\ref{app:convergence} reaches from the constant-step side. Every empirical claim here remains scoped to non-stationary
fits, and the experiment that would remove that scope is specified, implemented and released,
which is the most we can currently say for it.

\textbf{Capacity is matched by rule, not across a frontier.} The decoder template is frozen, but
input dimension changes across feature maps, so parameter counts do too: W10's MLP is $0.99$M
against $1.85$--$1.87$M for W11b and W5. W11a and W11b are sized to the decoder within $1.5\%$; W10
is not, and the comparisons involving it should be read with that in mind. A capacity and
training-compute frontier, rather than single matched points, is the stronger design.

\textbf{Identifiability is proved only at $L=1$, and every experiment here is $L=2$.} This remains
the program's weakest theoretical link: Theorem~\ref{thm:po2}'s guarantee and our corpora do not
meet. S4e (\S\ref{app:s4e}) closed the empirical half of the question, since the conjecture survives with positive evidence at $n=2$ and no counterexample, but not the proof: the deep case still
reduces to a Bessel--CP tensor decomposition with two open lemmas. S4e's own contribution to the
limitation is that it shows the question cannot be settled empirically at production width, so the
remaining route is analytic. \textbf{Depth $\ge 3$ invariants} need a Gram per successive layer and the
parity bookkeeping compounds; whether a finite family stays separating is unknown.
\textbf{Eigenvalue pooling in W10 is deliberately lossy}, and the $0.269$-versus-$0.628$ span against $\calign$ bounds what it discards without identifying it. \textbf{The reader is a
plain MLP with no permutation structure}, so W3 is floor-level partly by construction; this is the
intended reading of the rung, and X1 plus the canonicalization rungs are what separate ``the
reader is weak'' from ``the representation is scrambled''. \textbf{Genericity holds marginally}:
minimal parallel angles of $3\times10^{-4}$\,rad mean orbits pass close to the stratum where
identifiability genuinely fails. \textbf{All results are single-device (Apple MPS)}; a CUDA
replication of one headline table is owed. \textbf{Widths are 32--64}; the Hungarian assignment is
$O(n^3)$ per layer and an amortized or Sinkhorn path is required before this scales to width 1024.

\textbf{The empirical domain is narrow.} Three image datasets, one architecture family, one width,
one depth, one initialization distribution, $10$-way classification. Nothing here speaks to audio,
to 3-D occupancy or radiance fields, to deeper or wider SIRENs, or to targets that are properties of
the training run rather than of the represented signal, and that last category is where the case
for weight access is strongest, because it is the one place Corollary~\ref{cor:ceiling} does not
reach.

\textbf{The uncertainty analysis resolves one level of five.} Comparisons use $n=5$ paired decoder
seeds, which is adequate for the decoder-seed level and nothing else. Images, fit realizations,
protocols and datasets are the other levels of variation, and the $1.8$M fitted INRs do not make
them independent replications; a hierarchical resampling that reports the variance decomposition
across those levels would be the right analysis and is not what we ran.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:po2}}\label{app:po2}
% =====================================================================================

Throughout, $\theta = (W,b,u,\beta)$ denotes a one-hidden-layer sine network of width $n$,
\begin{equation}
f_\theta(x) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{n} u_i \sin\!\big(\langle w_i, x\rangle + b_i\big) \;+\; \beta,
\qquad x \in \R^m,
\label{eq:app-l1}
\end{equation}
with $w_i \in \R^m$, $b_i,\beta \in \R$ and $u_i \in \R^c$. For readability we take $c=1$ in the
proof. The vector-output case does not simply follow coordinatewise, because a coordinatewise
argument would appear to permit a different sign on each output coordinate, so we record the step
that rules this out. Matching atoms at a shared support point gives $u'\,e^{\mathrm i b'} =
u\,e^{\mathrm i b}$ as an identity between vectors in $\C^c$ with $u, u' \in \R^c \setminus
\{0\}$. Choose any index $k$ with $u_k \ne 0$; then $u'_k e^{\mathrm i b'} = u_k e^{\mathrm i b}$
with $u_k, u'_k$ real forces $e^{\mathrm i (b - b')} \in \R$, hence $e^{\mathrm i (b-b')} = \pm 1$
and $b' = b + \pi j$ for an integer $j$. That single value of $j$ then determines
$u' = (-1)^{j} u$ simultaneously in \emph{every} coordinate, so one common sign is enforced across
the whole output vector, which is what the normal form $g_{d,j}$ asserts. Recall
$\Thgen = \{\theta : w_i \neq 0,\ u_i \neq 0\ \forall i,\ w_j \neq \pm w_i\ \forall j \neq i\}$ and
the normal form $g_{d,j}(w,b,u) = ((-1)^d w,\ (-1)^d b + \pi j,\ (-1)^{d+j}u)$.

\begin{lemma}[Analytic continuation]\label{lem:app-analytic}
If $f_\theta = f_{\theta'}$ on a nonempty open $U \subseteq \R^m$, then $f_\theta = f_{\theta'}$
on all of $\R^m$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each summand of \eqref{eq:app-l1} extends to an entire function of $z \in \C^m$, so
$h = f_\theta - f_{\theta'}$ is entire, in particular real-analytic on the connected set $\R^m$. A
real-analytic function on a connected open set vanishing on a nonempty open subset vanishes
identically: the set where all derivatives vanish is open by the local power-series expansion,
closed by continuity of the derivatives, and nonempty.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fourier atoms]\label{lem:app-atoms}
$f_\theta$ is a tempered distribution and, with the convention
$\mathcal F[e^{\mathrm i\langle w,\cdot\rangle}] = (2\pi)^m \delta_w$ and the factor $(2\pi)^m$
suppressed,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{f_\theta} \;=\; \beta\,\delta_0 \;+\; \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{u_i}{2\mathrm i}
\Big( e^{\mathrm i b_i}\,\delta_{w_i} \;-\; e^{-\mathrm i b_i}\,\delta_{-w_i} \Big).
\label{eq:app-atoms}
\end{equation}
For $\theta \in \Thgen$ the $2n+1$ points $\{0\} \cup \{\pm w_i\}_{i \le n}$ are pairwise distinct
and every coefficient in \eqref{eq:app-atoms} except possibly $\beta$ is nonzero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$f_\theta$ is bounded, hence tempered. Write
$\sin(\langle w,x\rangle + b) = \frac{1}{2\mathrm i}(e^{\mathrm i b}e^{\mathrm i\langle w,x\rangle}
- e^{-\mathrm i b}e^{-\mathrm i\langle w,x\rangle})$ and apply the transform termwise, which is
legitimate because the sum is finite. Distinctness: $w_i \neq 0$ gives $w_i \neq -w_i$ and
$\pm w_i \neq 0$; $w_j \neq \pm w_i$ for $j\neq i$ gives distinctness across neurons. The
coefficient of $\delta_{\pm w_i}$ has modulus $|u_i|/2 \neq 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:po2}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:app-analytic}, $f_\theta = f_{\theta'}$ on $\R^m$, hence
$\widehat{f_\theta} = \widehat{f_{\theta'}}$ in $\mathcal S'(\R^m)$. Dirac masses at distinct
points are linearly independent in $\mathcal S'$, so two finite atomic distributions are equal iff
their supports and their coefficients agree.

\emph{(i) The constant.} Since $0$ lies in neither $\{\pm w_i\}$ nor $\{\pm w'_k\}$, the atom at
the origin is matched only by itself: $\beta = \beta'$.

\emph{(ii) Width and permutation.} The punctured supports agree:
$\{\pm w_i\}_{i\le n} = \{\pm w'_k\}_{k \le n'}$. The left side has exactly $2n$ elements and the
right exactly $2n'$, so $n = n'$. Both sets are closed under $v \mapsto -v$ and are partitioned
into $n$ unordered pairs $\{w_i,-w_i\}$, respectively $\{w'_k,-w'_k\}$; since the pairs are
disjoint, there is a unique bijection $\pi \in S_n$ with
$\{w'_{\pi(i)}, -w'_{\pi(i)}\} = \{w_i, -w_i\}$, i.e.\ $w'_{\pi(i)} = (-1)^{d_i} w_i$ for a unique
$d_i \in \{0,1\}$ (unique because $w_i \neq 0$).

\emph{(iii) Coefficients, case $d_i = 0$.} Matching the atoms at $w_i$ and at $-w_i$ gives
\[
\frac{u'_{\pi(i)}}{2\mathrm i}e^{\mathrm i b'_{\pi(i)}} = \frac{u_i}{2\mathrm i}e^{\mathrm i b_i},
\qquad
-\frac{u'_{\pi(i)}}{2\mathrm i}e^{-\mathrm i b'_{\pi(i)}} = -\frac{u_i}{2\mathrm i}e^{-\mathrm i b_i},
\]
the second being the complex conjugate of the first, so the content is
$u'_{\pi(i)} e^{\mathrm i b'_{\pi(i)}} = u_i e^{\mathrm i b_i}$ with both $u$'s real and nonzero.
Taking moduli gives $|u'_{\pi(i)}| = |u_i|$, so $u'_{\pi(i)} = \pm u_i$. If $u'_{\pi(i)} = u_i$
then $e^{\mathrm i b'_{\pi(i)}} = e^{\mathrm i b_i}$, i.e.\ $b'_{\pi(i)} = b_i + 2\pi k$; if
$u'_{\pi(i)} = -u_i$ then $e^{\mathrm i b'_{\pi(i)}} = e^{\mathrm i (b_i + \pi)}$, i.e.\
$b'_{\pi(i)} = b_i + \pi + 2\pi k$. These are exactly $g_{0,j}$ with $j = 2k$ and $j = 2k+1$
respectively, since $g_{0,j}(w,b,u) = (w,\, b + \pi j,\, (-1)^j u)$.

\emph{(iv) Coefficients, case $d_i = 1$.} Here $w'_{\pi(i)} = -w_i$, so the atom of $\theta'$ at
$w'_{\pi(i)}$ must match the atom of $\theta$ at $-w_i$:
$u'_{\pi(i)}e^{\mathrm i b'_{\pi(i)}} = -u_i e^{-\mathrm i b_i} = u_i e^{\mathrm i(\pi - b_i)}$.
Taking moduli again gives $u'_{\pi(i)} = \pm u_i$; if $u'_{\pi(i)} = u_i$ then
$b'_{\pi(i)} = -b_i + \pi + 2\pi k$, and if $u'_{\pi(i)} = -u_i$ then
$b'_{\pi(i)} = -b_i + 2\pi k$. These are exactly $g_{1,j}$ with $j$ odd and $j$ even, since
$g_{1,j}(w,b,u) = (-w,\, -b + \pi j,\, (-1)^{1+j}u)$.

Assembling the per-neuron elements with the permutation $\pi$ gives $g \in \Dinf \wr S_n$ with
$\theta' = g\theta$.

\emph{(v) Uniqueness.} It suffices that the stabilizer of any $\theta \in \Thgen$ is trivial, since
$g_1\theta = g_2\theta$ then forces $g_2^{-1}g_1 = \mathrm{id}$. Let $g = (\pi, (g_{d_i,j_i})_i)$
fix $\theta$. Reading the first-layer rows, $(-1)^{d_i} w_{\pi(i)} = w_i$ for every $i$. If
$\pi(i) \neq i$ this contradicts $w_{\pi(i)} \neq \pm w_i$, so $\pi = \mathrm{id}$; then
$(-1)^{d_i}w_i = w_i$ with $w_i \neq 0$ forces $d_i = 0$; then the bias reads
$b_i + \pi j_i = b_i$, so $j_i = 0$. Hence $g = \mathrm{id}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Where each hypothesis is used]
$w_i \neq 0$ is used for distinctness of $\pm w_i$, for uniqueness of $d_i$, and in (v);
$u_i \neq 0$ is used to know every atom is present, which is what makes the support carry the
width; and $w_j \neq \pm w_i$ is used for distinctness across neurons and in (v). On the parallel
stratum $w_j = \pm w_i$ the two atoms coincide and add as phasors,
$u_i\sin(z+b_i) + u_j\sin(z+b_j) = \operatorname{Im}\big((u_ie^{\mathrm i b_i}
+ u_je^{\mathrm i b_j})e^{\mathrm i z}\big)$, so only the complex sum is observable and the conclusion genuinely fails, so the exclusion is not an artifact of the proof.
\end{remark}

\section{The depth-two invariant encoding, in full}\label{app:w10}
% =====================================================================================

This appendix specifies rung W10 coordinate by coordinate, proves invariance of every emitted
coordinate, and states what is \emph{not} claimed.

\paragraph{Notation.} $L=2$, widths $n$ (layer 1) and $p$ (layer 2), input dimension $m$, output
dimension $c$: $W^1 \in \R^{n\times m}$, $b^1 \in \R^n$, $W^2 \in \R^{p\times n}$, $b^2 \in \R^p$,
$W^3 \in \R^{c\times p}$. Layer 1 acts on the rows of $W^1$, the entries of $b^1$ and the
\emph{columns} of $W^2$; layer 2 acts on the rows of $W^2$, the entries of $b^2$ and the columns of
$W^3$. Write $\varepsilon_i = (-1)^{d_i + j_i}$ for the sign a layer-1 neuron's outgoing column
picks up, and $\varepsilon'_k = (-1)^{e_k + l_k}$ for the layer-2 analogue. Let
$G = (W^2)^\top W^2 \in \R^{n \times n}$.

\paragraph{Transformation rules.} Under a layer-1 element,
\[
w^1_i \mapsto (-1)^{d_i} w^1_i, \quad
\sin b^1_i \mapsto \varepsilon_i \sin b^1_i, \quad
\cos b^1_i \mapsto (-1)^{j_i}\cos b^1_i, \quad
\sin 2b^1_i \mapsto (-1)^{d_i}\sin 2b^1_i,
\]
and $\cos 2b^1_i \mapsto \cos 2b^1_i$, $\|w^1_i\|^2 \mapsto \|w^1_i\|^2$. Under a layer-2 element
$W^2$ has its rows sign-flipped and permuted, so $G \mapsto G$; layer-2 phase shifts touch $b^2$
and $W^3$ only. Under layer 1, $G_{il} \mapsto \varepsilon_i\varepsilon_l G_{il}$ and
$G \mapsto PGP^\top$ for the layer-1 permutation matrix $P$.

\paragraph{The emitted vector.} With $\lambda^{\downarrow}(M) \in \R^n$ the eigenvalues of a
symmetric $M$ in decreasing order, and $\mathrm{sort}_{\kappa}$ the gather of a vector by the
descending order of the key $\kappa$, W10 emits the concatenation
\[
\underbrace{\lambda^{\downarrow}(A),\ \lambda^{\downarrow}(B),\ \lambda^{\downarrow}(C)}_{3n},\quad
\underbrace{\mathrm{sort}_{\kappa_1}\big(\|w^1_\bullet\|^2\big),\
\mathrm{sort}_{\kappa_1}\big(\cos 2b^1_\bullet\big),\
\mathrm{sort}_{\kappa_1}\big(G_{\bullet\bullet}\big)}_{3n},
\]
\[
\underbrace{\mathrm{sort}_{\kappa_2}\big(\|w^2_\bullet\|^2\big),\
\mathrm{sort}_{\kappa_2}\big(\cos 2b^2_\bullet\big),\
\mathrm{sort}_{\kappa_2}\big(\|u_\bullet\|^2\big),\
\mathrm{sort}_{\kappa_2}\big(\sin(b^2_\bullet)\,u_\bullet\big)}_{(3+c)p},
\]
where $A_{il} = (\sin b^1_i \sin b^1_l)G_{il}$,
$B_{il} = (\cos b^1_i w^1_i \cdot \cos b^1_l w^1_l)G_{il}$,
$C_{il} = (\sin 2b^1_i w^1_i \cdot \sin 2b^1_l w^1_l)$, $u_k = W^3[:,k] \in \R^c$,
$\kappa_1 = \|w^1_\bullet\|^2$ and $\kappa_2 = \|w^2_\bullet\|^2$. The total dimension is
$6n + (3+c)p$, which is $320$ at $n=p=32$, $c=1$ and $384$ at $c=3$.

\begin{proposition}[Invariance, coordinate by coordinate]
Every coordinate above is invariant under $G_{\mathrm{grp}} = (\Dinf \wr S_n)\times(\Dinf \wr S_p)$,
provided the sort keys $\kappa_1,\kappa_2$ have no ties.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{The matrices.} $A_{il} \mapsto (\varepsilon_i\sin b^1_i)(\varepsilon_l \sin b^1_l)\cdot
\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_l G_{il} = A_{il}$, using $\varepsilon_i^2 = 1$. For $B$, each factor
$\cos b^1_i w^1_i \mapsto (-1)^{j_i}(-1)^{d_i}\cos b^1_i w^1_i = \varepsilon_i \cos b^1_i w^1_i$,
so the inner product contributes $\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_l$, which cancels $G$'s. For $C$, each
factor $\sin 2b^1_i w^1_i \mapsto (-1)^{d_i}(-1)^{d_i}\sin 2b^1_i w^1_i$ is already invariant, and
no Gram coupling is needed. All three depend on $W^2$ only through $G$, which is layer-2 invariant.
Under the layer-1 permutation each transforms as $M \mapsto PMP^\top$, an orthogonal conjugation,
which leaves the spectrum unchanged; each is symmetric by construction, so
$\lambda^{\downarrow}$ is well defined and invariant.

\emph{The layer-1 order statistics.} $\|w^1_i\|^2$, $\cos 2b^1_i$ and $G_{ii}$ are individually
$\Dinf$-invariant per neuron ($\varepsilon_i^2 = 1$ for the last), and all four vectors, the three payloads and the key $\kappa_1$, are permuted by the \emph{same} $P$. Gathering by the
descending order of $\kappa_1$ therefore yields the same vector before and after.

\emph{Layer 2.} $\|w^2_k\|^2 = \sum_i (W^2_{ki})^2$ is invariant under layer-1 column signs
($\varepsilon_i^2=1$) and layer-1 permutation (it is a sum over $i$), and under layer-2 row signs
($(-1)^{2e_k}=1$); $\cos 2b^2_k$ is invariant; $\|u_k\|^2$ picks up $(\varepsilon'_k)^2 = 1$; and
$\sin(b^2_k)u_k \mapsto \varepsilon'_k\varepsilon'_k \sin(b^2_k)u_k$. All four are permuted by the
same layer-2 permutation as the key $\kappa_2$, so the gather is invariant.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is not claimed]\label{rem:w10-scope}
Three qualifications, all of which matter for how the W10 rows are read.

\emph{Invariant, not proven complete.} We prove every coordinate is constant on orbits. We do
\emph{not} claim the family separates distinct orbits, and the empirical shortfall against $\calign$ bounds what it discards without identifying it. In particular the eigenvalue pooling
is deliberately lossy: it retains a symmetric matrix only through its spectrum.

\emph{Stability.} The spectral block is $1$-Lipschitz in the spectral norm by Weyl's inequality,
$|\lambda_i(M) - \lambda_i(M')| \le \|M - M'\|_2$, so repeated eigenvalues are harmless here, since it is eigen\emph{vectors} that are unstable at a repeated eigenvalue, and we do not use them. The
order-statistic blocks are the fragile part: gathering three payloads by a shared key is
discontinuous where the key ties, i.e.\ where two neurons have equal $\|w^1_i\|^2$. This is the
sine-network instance of the obstruction of Proposition~\ref{prop:po5}, it has Lebesgue measure
zero, and the implementation uses a stable sort so the map is at least deterministic; the audit in
\S\ref{sec:bench} reports how close the production corpora come to it.

\emph{An encoding, not a reframing.} W10 returns features, not a parameter vector realizing the
same function, and its coordinates are nonlinear in the parameters. Its gain is therefore
separated from generic nonlinear feature engineering only by the matched control of
\S\ref{app:control}, not by its invariance.
\end{remark}

\section{Calibration of the program's own forecasts}\label{app:calibration}
% =====================================================================================

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig3_calibration.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Scoring our own predictions.} (a) Every registered interval, rescaled to its own
$[\text{lo80},\text{hi80}]$; blue is a hit, red a miss, arrows are off-scale. (b) Realized
coverage against nominal. The intervals are too \emph{narrow}, not too wide.}
\label{fig:calibration}
\end{figure*}

Because every prediction was registered with an interval, the program can be scored as a
forecaster \citep{gneiting2007scoring}. Through the two grayscale arms, realized coverage was
$9/14 = 64\%$ against a nominal $80\%$: the intervals were too narrow. Fig.~\ref{fig:calibration}
plots every registered interval against its outcome. More useful than the number is that the
failures fall into exactly two modes.

\emph{Mode 1, hedging a registered mechanism toward priors from a different setting.} Three of
the five early misses (the anchor gap, predicted $30\,[12,45]$, observed $80.4$; the CIFAR render
fidelity, predicted $27\,[22,32]$\,dB, observed $40.1$; the alignment recovery, predicted
$0.10\,[0.02,0.30]$, observed $0.628$) err in the same direction: our own mechanism predicted an
extreme and we hedged toward the middle because a neighbouring literature, working in a different
regime, reported milder effects. Where we trusted the mechanism instead, as with the $80.4$-point gap predicted to the decimal, the intervals hit.

\emph{Mode 2, registering a contrast that could not exist.} Two misses (optimization noise,
predicted $+2.0$; marginalization-beats-augmentation, predicted $+15$) assumed a nuisance was
present and then registered a difference between two ways of handling it. Both nuisances turned
out to be null, so neither contrast \emph{could} have appeared at any magnitude.

\paragraph{The CIFAR-10 arm was registered with those lessons applied, and is the cleaner test.}
Seventeen intervals were frozen against a corpus that did not yet have a single decoded cell,
after an explicit decision to register \emph{its own} magnitudes rather than inherit the MNIST ones, because scoring the FashionMNIST arm against MNIST magnitudes had already produced one
spurious ``miss'' that only careful write-up caught. (That failure mode is now blocked by the
analysis script rather than by prose: an arm with no registration of its own prints
\emph{not scored}.) \input{tables/cifar_scoreline}

The two misses are the informative ones and they are the paper's finding, not a footnote to it:
$f(\mathrm{W5})$ came in at half its registered value, and the bracket claim ``W10 lands between
W4 and W5'' broke \emph{upward} because W10 overtook W5. Both are the crossover of
\S\ref{app:crossover}, seen from the registration's point of view. Meanwhile the probability call
that ran on the encoding's algebra rather than on extrapolation, that $f(\mathrm{W10})$ would rise with the output-channel count, resolved correctly.

\paragraph{S4e, and a failure mode neither mode covers.} The identifiability hunt registered nine
intervals and scored \textbf{7/9}. Its two misses are one event: the best functional residual at
$n=2$ came in at $5.9\times10^{-8}$ against a registered $3\times10^{-4}\,[10^{-6}, 3\times10^{-3}]$,
and the orbit residual there at $1.2\times10^{-7}$ against $0.35\,[0.05, 0.65]$, because the pilot that informed both predictions used $n=32$ restarts and never sampled the global basin, while
the confirmatory run at $n=128$ did. Those rows were flagged \emph{pilot-informed} in the ledger
before the run for exactly this reason, and they are the argument for that flag: an under-powered
pilot mispredicts the powered run, and in this case in the direction that made the result better.

S4e also exposed a third failure mode, which is about \emph{criteria} rather than point
predictions: a pre-registered threshold can be under-specified in a way only data reveals
(\S\ref{app:s4e}, the missing absolute floor). The lesson we take is that a registered criterion
should be checked against its instrument's resolution at registration time, and that is now part of
the template.

The luminance-CIFAR arm (\S\ref{app:crossover}) scored $9/10$ and both of its probability calls
resolved against a mechanism this paper had previously proposed, which is why that mechanism is
withdrawn in \S\ref{app:crossover} rather than qualified.
