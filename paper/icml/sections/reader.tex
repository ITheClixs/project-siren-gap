\section{A reader that quotients the group}\label{sec:reader}

Every rung so far changes the feature map and holds the reader fixed. That is what makes the
decomposition interpretable, and it is also the obvious objection: the field does not read weights
with a plain MLP, it builds permutation-equivariant architectures
\citep{navon2023dws,zhou2023nfn,lim2024gmn,kofinas2024graph,tran2024monomial}. \textbf{W11a} is
capacity-matched bipartite message passing on the raw weights, equivariant to
$S_{n_1}\times S_{n_2}$ and \emph{not} invariant to $\Dinf$, which matches what that family covers
for periodic activations. \textbf{W11b} feeds W10's own invariants to an equivariant reader with
learned pooling.

\textbf{W12} is the reader neither of those is: exactly $G$-invariant on the \emph{raw} parameters.
Under $g_{d,j}$ the phasor coordinates of the bias transform with $j$ only through its
\emph{parity}, since $\cos b \mapsto (-1)^j \cos b$ and $\sin b \mapsto (-1)^{d+j}\sin b$, so
replacing $b$ by $(\cos b, \sin b)$ turns the infinite group $\Z\rtimes\Z_2$ into a finite
$\Z_2\times\Z_2$ acting by signs. Writing a \emph{character} $\chi=(a,c)$ for a feature picking up
$(-1)^{ad+cj}$, the raw parameters sort into four blocks,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(0,0)&: \|w_i\|^2,\ \cos 2b_i &\quad (1,0)&: w_i,\ \sin 2b_i\\
(0,1)&: \cos b_i &\quad (1,1)&: \sin b_i,\ u_i ,
\end{aligned}
\]
and the coupling matrix $W^2$ carries character $(1,1)$ on the layer-1 side and $(1,0)$ on the
layer-2 side, which leaves exactly two legitimate message channels per direction,
$\sum_i W^2_{ki} f_i^{(1,1)}$ and $\sum_i (W^2_{ki})^2 f_i^{(0,0)}$, together with their transposes.
That is the Gram coupling of Proposition~\ref{prop:deep} reappearing as a message rule, except that
here the network learns which couplings to use.

\begin{proposition}[Invariance of the phasor-graded reader]\label{prop:w12}
Let $f^{(\chi)}(\theta) \in \R^{n \times d_\chi}$ denote the block of node features of character
$\chi \in \{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\}$. For every $g \in \Dinf \wr S_n$ acting on a layer, the
feature map above satisfies $f^{(\chi)}(g\theta) = \chi(g)\, P_g\, f^{(\chi)}(\theta)$ with
$\chi(g) \in \{\pm1\}$. Then a reader built from \emph{(i)} per-character linear maps, carrying a
bias only on $\chi = (0,0)$, \emph{(ii)} bilinear products routed to the sum of their characters,
\emph{(iii)} an \emph{odd} nonlinearity on every block with $\chi \ne (0,0)$, \emph{(iv)} the two
message channels above, and \emph{(v)} a head reading only the $(0,0)$ block after mean and max
pooling over nodes, computes a function of $\theta$ that is exactly invariant under
$\Dinf \wr S_n$.
\end{proposition}

The proof is an induction on layers carrying the covariance
$f^{(\chi)} \mapsto \chi(g) P_g f^{(\chi)}$; it is in Appendix~\ref{app:w12}. Two things it does not
say. It is a statement in exact arithmetic, so the floating-point audit is what covers the
implementation, and that audit puts the relative logit movement at $3\times10^{-6}$ on $512$ fitted
networks under windings to $|j| = 40$. And it requires feature standardization to be scale-only,
since dividing by a constant commutes with the sign action while subtracting a mean would not.

Every reader is sized by rule to the frozen decoder's $1{,}873{,}162$ parameters, so no row loses
for being smaller.

\paragraph{A $G$-aware reader on raw parameters beats every other treatment here.}
$s(\mathrm{W12}) = \mathbf{0.917}$, that is $87.64\%$ against the shared-initialization anchor at
$94.36\%$, from weights fitted independently. That is $+0.651$ over the permutation-only reader,
$+0.390$ over the same-family reader fed a fixed invariant front-end, and $+0.288$ over the best
exact reframing. We registered this badly: three hypotheses scored $0/3$ and every miss is in the
same direction. The probability call that mattered, at $0.25$ that a $G$-aware reader would beat
$\calign$, resolved true. Section~4 of the frozen registration committed in advance that if it did,
this paper's claim that within weight space the choice of orbit representative matters more than
reader architecture would be \textbf{withdrawn rather than qualified}. It is withdrawn. The
supported claim is the opposite one.

This closes a loop the theory had left open. The intervention found that within $\Dinf\wr S_n$ the
reflection and phase components carry four fifths of the damage and neuron relabelling one fifth
(\S\ref{sec:orbit}); W11a quotients only the relabelling and recovers $0.265$, while W12 quotients
all of it and recovers $0.917$. The group characterization is not decoration on the empirical part
of the paper. It tells you which quotient a reader has to take.

\paragraph{On the shared-initialization corpus.} Where there is nothing to quotient, W12 reaches
$95.46\%$ against W1's $94.36\%$, giving $f = 1.014$ strictly above one. So the phasor coordinates
are a better way to read parameters even with no group scatter present, and part of what W12 gains
on \texttt{P-random} is architecture rather than invariance. That caps how much of the recovery any
invariant construction may claim as symmetry work.

\paragraph{Against the published leaderboard.} The standard MNIST-INR corpus of
\citet{navon2023dws} is what DWSNets, NFN, NG-GNN and ScaleGMN all report on, and its INRs have
exactly the architecture our readers take, so W12 runs on it unchanged. In its frozen
configuration, with nothing tuned per dataset, W12 reaches $93.08 \pm 0.44$, $75.15 \pm 0.31$ and
$38.16 \pm 0.22$ over five seeds: third, third and second of seven. It is above DWSNets, both NFN
variants and NG-GNN on all three, and above ScaleGMN itself on CIFAR-10 by $1.7$ points, while
trailing ScaleGMN-B everywhere (Table~\ref{tab:bench}, Appendix~\ref{app:bench}). We report it
as a competitive reader rather than a leading one, and we quote the baselines from their papers
rather than rerunning them.

\noindent Two things this establishes beyond the ranking. The benchmark corpus is
\emph{independently} initialized, with median pairwise relative parameter distance $1.419$ against
$1.40$ for our own \texttt{P-random} and $0.19$ for our shared corpus, so the published leaderboard
is set in the hard regime and it is those architectures rather than their corpora doing the work.
And W12 scores $93.1\%$ there against $87.6\%$ on ours at the same architecture, so some of our
ladder's difficulty is a property of our fitting protocol rather than of the problem.

\paragraph{What in the construction works.} Two matched controls vary one ingredient each at the
same capacity. Removing the layer grading while keeping the phasor coordinates gives $0.858$;
keeping the grading and feeding it the raw bias gives $0.602$; carrying neither gives $0.557$. The
phasor lift is worth about $+0.31$ and the grading about $+0.05$, with an interaction of $+0.013$,
so within this skeleton the two are close to additive and the layer-level grading is worth little
next to the coordinates. Both controls were registered before running and score $5/5$ intervals.
Appendix~\ref{app:square} gives the full square and what the grading actually buys, which is not
the mechanism we would have named.
