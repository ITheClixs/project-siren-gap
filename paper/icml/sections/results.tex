\section{Results: what the ladder measures}\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{The size of the gap.} On MNIST, W1 $=94.36$ against W3 $=13.92$: an $80.4$-point gap
between two corpora of networks fitted to \emph{the same images}, with the same architecture,
differing only in whether the initialization was shared. FashionMNIST gives $70.3$ points and
CIFAR-10 $31.6$. The two representations are not even rescalings of one another: a decoder trained
on W1 features scores at chance on W3 features and the reverse.

\paragraph{It is not decoder inadequacy.} A decoder reading oracle renders of the fitted INRs
matches one reading real pixels everywhere, with TOST equivalence at a $1.0$-point margin on MNIST
($p = 2.5\times10^{-5}$), so fitting causes no loss the render classifier can detect. Resampling the optimizer's stochasticity at
fixed initialization moves accuracy by less than a point, against the $80$ that changing the
initialization moves it (Appendix~\ref{app:ladder}).

\paragraph{Reframing recovers much of the gap, and the fraction is not constant.}
$f(\mathrm{W5}) = 0.628$ on MNIST and $0.664$ on FashionMNIST. We had registered $0.10$ with an
$80\%$ interval reaching only to $0.30$, and a pre-committed rule stating that $f > 0.5$ falsifies
the claim that canonicalization is not enough. It fired. We then registered the restatement as a
CIFAR-10 prediction, $0.62$ with interval $[0.42,0.78]$, and it missed in the other direction:
$f(\mathrm{W5}) = 0.325$. The two-thirds figure is a property of the grayscale corpora, not a law.
What holds on all three is that an exact reframing recovers between a third and two thirds of the
gap while no inexact treatment recovers more than an eighth.

\paragraph{The choice of representative matters more than exactness.} Template-free sorting
recovers $0.177$, $0.170$ and $0.108$; aligning to a fixed reference recovers $0.628$, $0.664$ and
$0.325$. Both are exact elements of $G$, and the difference is entirely which orbit representative
is chosen. Five alignment templates show the reference need not be privileged: an \emph{unrelated}
random initialization does marginally better ($0.640$) than the corpus's own $\theta_0$ ($0.628$).
Alignment buys a consistent frame, and any fixed reference network supplies one.

\paragraph{The inexact treatments recover little.} Across the three datasets, bounded group
augmentation recovers $0.054 / 0.032 / 0.128$, $K$-marginalization $0.048 / 0.034 / 0.101$, and
frame averaging $0.003 / -0.008 / 0.001$. Canonicalizing and \emph{then} augmenting collapses to
chance on all three, because augmenting inside a canonical frame destroys the frame the decoder just
gained. Frame averaging fails in the expected way: parity-odd coordinates cancel toward zero, so it
is a lossy invariantization where W10 is an exact one. The scope of these three nulls is limited.
$\Dinf$ is infinite, so there is no uniform measure to average or augment over; W6 and W9 are
instantiated at one winding distribution, and a different truncation could behave differently. What
the ladder establishes is that \emph{these} instantiations recover at most $12.8\%$.

\subsection{What \texorpdfstring{$f$}{f} does not measure}\label{sec:bounds}

An earlier version of this paper claimed $f$ is a certified lower bound on the share of the gap
caused by symmetry. That claim is wrong, and it fails at a step that is implicit elsewhere in this
literature: an exact reframing preserves the function, so it creates no information, so any accuracy
it buys must come from removing nuisance. The last step does not follow, because an orbit-valued map
can \emph{route} an orbit-invariant quantity into a coordinate the reader finds easy to read. Let
$y(\theta)$ be any orbit-invariant binary property and define $c$ to apply $\tau_k$ at the first
neuron with $k = M\,y(\theta)$ for a large constant $M$. Then $c(\theta) \in G\theta$ and
$f_{c(\theta)} = f_\theta$ exactly, yet a linear probe on the first bias now predicts $y$: no
function-level information was created, and the group's degrees of freedom were used as a
communication channel. Two further gaps separate $f$ from a causal share. The decoder is retrained
per rung, so what is held fixed is the learning \emph{algorithm} rather than a fixed predictor; and
the comparison contrasts corpora fitted from \emph{different initializations}, which is an
intervention on the fit map and not on the group. The estimand that \emph{is} causal requires
intervening on the group while holding the network and its function fixed, which is
\S\ref{sec:orbit}.
