\section{Conclusion}

For periodic-activation networks the group is $\Dinf\wr S_n$. Prior work uses two of its generators
as augmentations; what is new is the group they generate, the affine phase component no
monomial-matrix classification contains, and the proof that at one hidden layer it exhausts generic
functional equivalence. Exact reframing recovers $63\%$, $66\%$ and $32\%$ of the gap, but that is
algorithm-relative, so we also measure the group's effect directly: randomizing it over a corpus
with no initialization nuisance costs $79.1$ points against an $80.4$-point observed gap. Within the
group, sign flips carry about $63$ of those points and relabelling about $15$, so the affine phase
component is mathematically necessary for the identifiability statement and empirically minor as a
source of scatter. Some residual survives an intervention removing the group entirely, so the gap is
not \emph{wholly} reducible to parameter symmetry, but a stronger invariant reader lowered that
residual from $28.6$ points to $7.8$, and every such measurement is an upper bound.

Acting on the characterization gives a reader that quotients $\Dinf\wr S_n$ on the raw parameters
and recovers $0.917$ at matched capacity, against $0.628$ for the best exact reframing; the
registration committed in advance to withdrawing this paper's earlier claim that frame choice beats
reader architecture if this happened, and it is withdrawn. The practical conclusion nonetheless
points away from weight space at this scale, since every pipeline we tested is dominated by function
access on both accuracy and inference cost.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Identifiability is proved only at $L=1$ while every experiment is $L=2$,
and Appendix~\ref{app:s4e} shows the question cannot be settled empirically at production width, so
the remaining route is analytic. The non-symmetry residual is reader-relative and never measured
against a corpus-level quantity. Signal complexity remains confounded with image statistics. All
fits end unconverged: a $33\times$ budget sweep does not lower the endpoint gradient norm and an
annealed-schedule attempt missed its own stationarity gate twice, so every claim here is scoped to
non-stationary fits. W11a is not the strongest member of the equivariant family, the domain is three
image datasets at one width and depth, and the uncertainty analysis resolves the decoder-seed level
only. Appendix~\ref{app:limits} states each in full. Corpora, pre-registrations and the full
prediction ledger are released with the code.
