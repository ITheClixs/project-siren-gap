\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

An implicit neural representation encodes a signal as the weights of a small network fitted to it
\citep{sitzmann2020siren,mildenhall2020nerf,tancik2020fourier}. Once a dataset of signals has been
converted into a dataset of \emph{weight vectors}, it is natural to ask whether a downstream model
can read semantics off those weights directly: a class label, a shape property. A large and
fast-moving literature builds architectures for this
\citep{navon2023dws,zhou2023nfn,lim2024gmn,kofinas2024graph,tran2024monomial,%
kalogeropoulos2024scalegmn}, and a parallel line treats weights as data for property prediction and
generation \citep{unterthiner2020predicting,schurholt2022zoos,erkoc2023hyperdiffusion}.

The field runs into one stubborn empirical fact. If every INR in a corpus is fitted from the
\emph{same} initialization, a plain multilayer perceptron reading the flattened weights nearly
matches a pixel classifier. If each is fitted from its \emph{own} random initialization, which is
the setting any realistic collection of independently trained models is in, the same reader
collapses to a few points above chance. Methods that work in the first regime are, at least in
part, exploiting an artifact of corpus construction
\citep{deluigi2023inr2vec,papa2024howtotrain}. We call the difference the \textbf{weight-space
perception gap}.

The standard explanation is nuisance variability from parameter symmetry: two networks computing
the same function can have very different weights, so a reader must learn to quotient a group it
knows nothing about structurally. The explanation is plausible and it motivates most of the
equivariant architectures cited above. To our knowledge it has never been measured against its
rival, which holds that independent fits land in genuinely different loss basins whose difference
is \emph{not} a group action, and so cannot be removed by any canonicalization, augmentation or
equivariant layer at all. The two accounts prescribe opposite research programmes and the field has
been proceeding on the first without checking.

This paper tests it. We characterize the group these transformations generate
(\S\ref{sec:group}): each hidden neuron carries an infinite dihedral action $\Dinf$ and a layer
carries $\Dinf \wr S_n$, with phase generators that are \emph{affine} rather than linear, which is
why a classification restricted to monomial-matrix actions cannot contain them. At one hidden layer
that group is \emph{all} of the functional equivalence on an explicit generic stratum
(\S\ref{sec:ident}), from which a global continuous-canonicalization obstruction follows, and so
does the observation that any \emph{complete} invariant factors through the realized function, so
weight access can beat function access only computationally or by being deliberately incomplete.

We then measure. A pre-registered decomposition ladder (\S\ref{sec:bench}, \S\ref{sec:results})
applies feature maps to corpora that differ in exactly the nuisance under study and decodes each
with one frozen apparatus. An exact element of $\Dinf \wr S_n$, applied as an alignment to a fixed
reference network and changing no network's function, recovers $63\%$, $66\%$ and $32\%$ of the gap
on MNIST, FashionMNIST and CIFAR-10, while the treatments most often applied to this nuisance,
namely group augmentation, refit marginalization and frame averaging, recover at most $13\%$. Those
are algorithm-relative recoverable fractions rather than causal shares, for a reason
\S\ref{sec:results} sets out, so we measure the causal quantity separately by intervening on the
group directly (\S\ref{sec:orbit}): randomizing it while holding every network and its function
fixed costs $79.1$ of the $80.4$ points, and yet an exactly invariant reader still loses points
between the two corpora, $28.6$ for one reader and $7.8$ for a stronger one.

Acting on the characterization closes most of what remains (\S\ref{sec:reader}). Bias phasors reduce
the winding to a parity, leaving a finite $\Z_2\times\Z_2$ grading the network preserves layer by
layer; the resulting reader is exactly invariant on the \emph{raw} parameters, scores $0.92$ at
matched capacity against $0.63$ for the best exact reframing, is competitive on the three published
benchmarks, and overturns this paper's own earlier claim that frame choice beats reader
architecture. A FLOPs-matched comparison against function access (\S\ref{sec:function}) nonetheless
finds weight access dominated on both accuracy and inference cost.
