\begin{center}\textbf{Abstract}\end{center}
\small
A classifier reading the raw weights of independently fitted implicit neural representations (INRs)
performs near chance; reading weights fitted from a \emph{shared} initialization, it nearly matches
a pixel model. That gap is routinely attributed to parameter symmetry. It has not been measured.

We characterize the group generated by the function-preserving transformations of sine networks:
each hidden neuron carries an infinite dihedral action $\Dinf$ whose phase component is
\emph{affine}, placing it outside the monomial-matrix actions previous classifications cover, and a
layer carries $\Dinf \wr S_n$. For generic one-hidden-layer sine networks we prove this group
exhausts functional equivalence.

Exact orbit-valued reframing recovers $63\%$, $66\%$ and $32\%$ of the gap, but that is relative to
the reader and its training algorithm rather than a causal share. Intervening instead, by
randomizing the group while holding every network and its realized function fixed, costs $79$ of
the $80$ points, so group scatter is \emph{sufficient} to reproduce almost the whole degradation,
though it does not identify what fraction of the natural gap symmetry mediates.

Acting on the characterization, bias phasors reduce the winding to a parity, leaving a finite
grading a network preserves layer by layer; we prove the resulting reader on \emph{raw} parameters
is exactly invariant. Against the numbers published for the three standard INR-classification
benchmarks it is competitive rather than leading: above DWSNets, both NFN variants and NG-GNN on
all three, above ScaleGMN on CIFAR-10, below ScaleGMN-B throughout. We quote those baselines from
their papers rather than rerunning them, so this is a comparison with published results and not a
controlled head-to-head. Querying the same INR at $64$ learned coordinates is nonetheless more
accurate than every weight-space pipeline we built, and about $100\times$ cheaper. All fits use
constant-step Adam, which our own sweep shows does not reach stationarity.
\end{minipage}
\end{center}
