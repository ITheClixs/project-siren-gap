\begin{center}\textbf{Abstract}\end{center}
\small
Classifiers that read the raw weights of independently fitted implicit neural representations
(INRs) perform near chance, while the same classifier reading weights fitted from a \emph{shared}
initialization performs almost as well as it does on pixels. This gap is usually attributed to
parameter symmetry without direct measurement.

Prior work uses two function-preserving transformations of sine networks --- neuron negation and
integer-$\pi$ bias shifts --- as weight-space augmentations \citep{shamsian2024augmentations}. We
characterize the group they generate: each hidden neuron carries an infinite dihedral action
$\Dinf$ whose phase component is \emph{affine} and therefore outside the monomial-matrix actions
previous classifications cover, and a layer carries $\Dinf \wr S_n$. For generic one-hidden-layer
sine networks we prove this group exhausts functional equivalence, and at depth two we construct
exact cross-layer invariants through the second-layer Gram matrix.

Exact orbit-valued reframing recovers $63\%$, $66\%$ and $32\%$ of the shared-versus-random accuracy
gap. That fraction is \emph{algorithm-relative}, not a causal share; we measure the causal quantity
by randomizing the group while holding each fitted network and its function fixed. Randomizing the
group costs $79$ of the $80$ points --- yet a reader whose invariance to it we \emph{measure} still
loses $28.6$ points between the two corpora, so the gap is not reducible to group scatter. Within
the group, sign flips carry four fifths of the damage and integer phase shifts about one point. A
control matched to our invariant encoding in degree, trigonometric order, pooling and dimension,
differing only in whether $\Dinf$ is quotiented out, attributes $0.32$ of that encoding's $0.53$
CIFAR-10 recovery to invariance. Finally, querying an INR at $64$ learned coordinates outperforms
every weight-space pipeline we tested, at lower cost.

\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================================

An implicit neural representation encodes a signal as the weights of a small network fitted to
it \citep{sitzmann2020siren,mildenhall2020nerf,tancik2020fourier}. Once a dataset of signals has
been converted into a dataset of \emph{weight vectors}, it is natural to ask whether a downstream
model can read semantics --- a class label, a shape property --- directly off those weights. A
large and fast-moving literature builds architectures for exactly this
\citep{navon2023dws,zhou2023nfn,zhou2023nft,lim2024gmn,kofinas2024graph,tran2024monomial,%
zhou2024unf,kalogeropoulos2024scalegmn,schurholt2024sane}, and a parallel line treats weights as
data for property prediction and generation
\citep{unterthiner2020predicting,schurholt2021hyperrep,schurholt2022zoos,erkoc2023hyperdiffusion,%
peebles2022gpt}.

The field runs into one stubborn empirical fact. If every INR in a corpus is fitted from the
\emph{same} initialization, a plain multilayer perceptron reading the flattened weights nearly
matches a pixel classifier. If each INR is fitted from its \emph{own} random initialization ---
the setting any realistic collection of independently trained models is in --- the same reader
collapses to a few points above chance. Methods that work in the first regime are, at least in
part, exploiting an artifact of corpus construction
\citep{deluigi2023inr2vec,papa2024howtotrain}. We call the difference the \textbf{weight-space
perception gap}.

The standard explanation is nuisance variability from parameter symmetry: two networks computing
the same function can have very different weights, so the reader must learn to quotient a group
it has no structural knowledge of. This is plausible, it motivates most of the equivariant
architectures cited above, and --- to our knowledge --- it has never been measured against the
alternative, which is that independent fits land in genuinely different loss basins whose
difference is \emph{not} a group action and therefore cannot be removed by any canonicalization,
augmentation, or equivariant layer.

This paper measures it. Our contributions:

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{The group these transformations generate} (\S\ref{sec:group}). The two
function-preserving maps themselves are not new: \citet{shamsian2024augmentations} use neuron
negation and integer-$\pi$ bias shifts as SIREN weight-space augmentations, and
\citet{tran2024monomial} incorporate sign symmetries for sine and tanh activations into a
monomial-matrix framework. We ask what group they generate and whether it exhausts functional
equivalence. The answer is that each hidden neuron carries an infinite dihedral action $\Dinf$ and
a layer carries $\Dinf \wr S_n$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:po1}), and that the phase generators are
\emph{affine} rather than linear --- which is why a classification restricted to monomial-matrix
actions cannot contain them.
\item \textbf{Generic identifiability at one hidden layer} (\S\ref{sec:identifiability}).
$\Dinf \wr S_n$ is \emph{all} of the functional equivalence on an explicit generic stratum
(Theorem~\ref{thm:po2}), by a Fourier-atom argument that is the periodic-activation analogue of a
classical line \citep{hechtnielsen1990algebraic,sussmann1992uniqueness,fefferman1994reconstructing}.
Two consequences follow rather than stand alone: the sine instance of
\citeauthor{dym2024impossibility}'s continuous-canonicalization obstruction, which here is global
rather than confined to sorting ties (Proposition~\ref{prop:po5}); and the observation that any
\emph{complete} invariant factors through the realized function
(Proposition~\ref{prop:po6}), so weight access can beat function access only computationally or by
being deliberately incomplete.
\item \textbf{Exact invariants at depth two} (\S\ref{sec:deep}). Per-neuron invariants break at
$L\ge 2$ because a hidden neuron's outgoing vector is acted on by the next layer. Coupling the
layers through the second-layer Gram $G = W_2^\top W_2$ repairs this and yields a finite family
of exactly invariant features, verified numerically to $3\times10^{-7}$ relative. A control
matched to it in monomial degree, trigonometric order, pooling and dimension --- differing only in
whether the $\Dinf$ component is quotiented out --- separates the encoding's gain from generic
nonlinear feature engineering (\S\ref{sec:control}).
\item \textbf{A pre-registered decomposition ladder} (\S\ref{sec:ladder}--\ref{sec:results}). Ten
feature maps applied to the same corpora and decoded by one frozen apparatus separate the gap
into what an exact change of frame removes and what it cannot. Registered before any cell was
computed, and scored against the frozen text.
\item \textbf{An orbit-only intervention} (\S\ref{sec:orbit}). Holding each fitted network and its
realized function fixed while randomizing the group isolates the degradation the group alone
causes, which the ladder's shared-versus-random contrast cannot. It shows that the group reproduces
nearly the whole gap, and --- via a reader whose invariance to it we measure rather than assume ---
that the gap is nevertheless not reducible to the group.
\item \textbf{A FLOPs-matched comparison against function access} (\S\ref{sec:s5}), in which
weight access is dominated on both accuracy and inference cost.
\end{enumerate}

We also report a mechanism for the initialization's dominance (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}), a
capacity-matched comparison against equivariant reader architectures (\S\ref{sec:w11}), a
falsification hunt against identifiability at depth two (\S\ref{sec:s4e}) built on an exact
minimizer over $G$, and a calibration audit of the program's own interval forecasts
(App.~\ref{sec:calibration}). Which components are ours and which are inherited is set out
component by component in \S\ref{sec:related}, Table~\ref{tab:provenance}.

\paragraph{Headline result.} A transformation that changes no network's function at all --- an
exact element of $\Dinf \wr S_n$, applied as an alignment to a fixed reference network --- recovers
$63\%$, $66\%$ and $32\%$ of the perception gap on MNIST, FashionMNIST and CIFAR-10. The exact
invariant \emph{encoding} of \S\ref{sec:deep} recovers $27\%$, $43\%$ and $53\%$; we keep the two
families separate throughout, because an encoding returns features rather than a parameter vector
and its gain has to be separated from nonlinear feature engineering by a matched control
(\S\ref{sec:control}). Both are algorithm-relative recoverable fractions rather than causal shares:
an exact reframing creates no function-level information, but it can still route an orbit-invariant
property into a coordinate the reader finds accessible, so the inference from ``no information
created'' to ``the gain measures nuisance removal'' does not hold (\S\ref{sec:bounds}). The causal
quantity is measured separately, by intervening on the group directly (\S\ref{sec:orbit}), and the
answer has two halves: randomizing the group costs $79.1$ of the $80.4$ points, and yet a reader
whose invariance to that randomization is \emph{measured} at $0.59$ points still loses $28.6$
between the two corpora. The gap looks like group scatter and is not reducible to it. Under our
implementations and training budgets, the treatments most often applied to this nuisance ---
augmenting by group elements \citep{shamsian2024augmentations}, marginalizing over refits, averaging
over a frame \citep{puny2022frame} --- recover at most $13\%$ on any dataset and under $6\%$ on both
grayscale ones. And \emph{which} exact method wins changes with the signal (\S\ref{sec:crossover}).

\section{Setting and the symmetry group}\label{sec:group}
% =====================================================================================

Fix input dimension $m$ (for images $m=2$), output dimension $c$, and hidden widths
$n_1,\dots,n_L$. A \emph{sine network} in canonical form is
\begin{equation}
h^0 = x, \qquad h^\ell = \sin\!\big(W^\ell h^{\ell-1} + b^\ell\big)\ \ (\ell = 1,\dots,L),
\qquad f_\theta(x) = W^{L+1} h^L + b^{L+1},
\label{eq:siren}
\end{equation}
with $\theta = (W^1,b^1,\dots,W^{L+1},b^{L+1})$. SIREN's frequency scale $\omega_0$
\citep{sitzmann2020siren} is absorbed into the stored weights at corpus creation. For a hidden
neuron $i$ write $w_i$ for its row of $W^\ell$, $b_i$ for its bias, and $u_i$ for its column of
$W^{\ell+1}$.

\begin{definition}[Per-neuron maps]\label{def:gen}
$\tau_k : (w,b,u) \mapsto (w,\, b + 2\pi k,\, u)$ for $k\in\Z$; \quad
$\rho : (w,b,u) \mapsto (w,\, b+\pi,\, -u)$; \quad
$\sigma : (w,b,u) \mapsto (-w,\, -b,\, -u)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Normal form]\label{lem:normal}
Every element of $\langle \tau_1,\rho,\sigma\rangle$ acts as
\begin{equation}
g_{d,j}\colon (w,b,u)\ \longmapsto\ \big((-1)^d w,\ (-1)^d b + \pi j,\ (-1)^{d+j} u\big),
\qquad d \in \{0,1\},\ j\in\Z,
\label{eq:normalform}
\end{equation}
with composition $g_{d_2,j_2}\circ g_{d_1,j_1} = g_{\,d_1\oplus d_2,\ j_2 + (-1)^{d_2} j_1}$.
Hence $\langle \tau_1,\rho,\sigma\rangle \cong \Z \rtimes \Z_2 = \Dinf$, with $\rho^2 = \tau_1$,
$\sigma^2 = \mathrm{id}$ and $\sigma\rho\sigma = \rho^{-1}$; $\tau_1$ is a redundant generator.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Symmetry group]\label{thm:po1}
For every $g_{d,j}$ applied at any hidden neuron, and every permutation $\pi \in S_{n_\ell}$
applied jointly to the rows of $W^\ell$, the entries of $b^\ell$ and the columns of $W^{\ell+1}$,
the network function is exactly preserved. Hence
\begin{equation}
G \;=\; \prod_{\ell=1}^{L} \Dinf \wr S_{n_\ell}
    \;=\; \prod_{\ell=1}^{L} \big(\Dinf^{\,n_\ell} \rtimes S_{n_\ell}\big)
\end{equation}
acts on parameter space with $f_{g\cdot\theta} = f_\theta$ for all $g\in G$, and the actions of
distinct layers commute.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With pre-activation $z = \langle w,h\rangle + b$,
$(-1)^{d+j} u\,\sin\!\big((-1)^d z + \pi j\big)
 = (-1)^{d+j}(-1)^{j} u \sin\!\big((-1)^d z\big)
 = (-1)^{d+j}(-1)^{j}(-1)^{d} u \sin z = u \sin z$,
so the neuron's contribution to the next pre-activation is unchanged. Permutations relabel
neurons while re-wiring outgoing columns consistently. Row and column operations on the shared
matrix $W^{\ell+1}$ touch different indices and commute.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:po1} asserts \emph{containment}. That $G$ is maximal is
Theorem~\ref{thm:po2} for $L=1$. \citet{tran2024monomial} establish maximality among
\emph{linear} (monomial-matrix) actions and leave the general sine case open; the phase maps
$g_{0,j}$, $j\neq0$, are affine and lie outside every linear action, which is exactly why their
classification cannot see the phase component.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Where genericity is needed.} Three closed, Lebesgue-null strata carry functional
equivalences \emph{beyond} the $G$-orbit: $w_i = 0$ (dead), $u_i = 0$ (invisible), and
$w_j = \pm w_i$ (\emph{parallel-frequency}). On the last, phasor addition
$u_i\sin(z+b_i) + u_j\sin(z+b_j) = \operatorname{Im}\big((u_ie^{\mathrm i b_i}
+ u_je^{\mathrm i b_j})e^{\mathrm i z}\big)$
shows only the complex sums are functionally visible: two parallel neurons carry one neuron's
worth of function, for \emph{arbitrary} bias pairs. Genericity is an assumption we measure on
every corpus rather than assume (\S\ref{sec:bench}).

\section{Identifiability, impossibility, and a ceiling on weight access}
\label{sec:identifiability}
% =====================================================================================

Let $\Thgen$ be the parameters with all $w_i \neq 0$, all $u_i \neq 0$, and no pair
$w_j = \pm w_i$.

\begin{theorem}[Generic identifiability, $L=1$]\label{thm:po2}
Let $\theta,\theta' \in \Thgen$ with widths $n, n'$ and $f_\theta = f_{\theta'}$ on some open
$U \subseteq \R^m$. Then $n = n'$ and $\theta' = g\theta$ for a \emph{unique}
$g \in \Dinf \wr S_n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch; App.~\ref{app:po2} gives it in full]
$f_\theta - f_{\theta'}$ is entire and vanishes on an open set, hence everywhere. As tempered
distributions,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{f_\theta} \;=\; \beta\,\delta_0 \;+\; \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{u_i}{2\mathrm i}
\Big( e^{\mathrm i b_i}\,\delta_{w_i} \;-\; e^{-\mathrm i b_i}\,\delta_{-w_i} \Big),
\label{eq:atoms}
\end{equation}
an atomic measure whose $2n$ support points $\{\pm w_i\}$ are distinct and nonzero on $\Thgen$
and whose coefficients are all nonzero. Equality of the transforms forces equal supports and
equal coefficients: the $\delta_0$ terms give $\beta=\beta'$, the punctured supports biject
(giving $n=n'$ and the permutation), and each matched pair solves to either
$(u',b') = (u,\, b+2\pi k)$ or $(-u,\, b+\pi+2\pi k)$ --- exactly the $\rho/\tau$ family --- or
to their $\sigma$-image. Uniqueness: a $g$ fixing $\theta \in \Thgen$ must fix the distinct
unordered pairs $\{\pm w_i\}$, so its permutation is trivial, and $g_{d,j}(w,b,u)=(w,b,u)$ with
$w\neq0$ forces $d=j=0$.
\end{proof}

The parallel-frequency exclusion is not technical convenience: when $w_j = \pm w_i$ the two atoms
in \eqref{eq:atoms} share a support point and add as phasors, so separate recovery is genuinely
impossible. In the vocabulary of maximal symmetry groups, $\Dinf\wr S_n$ is a maximal symmetry
group of the $L=1$ sine architecture. The result is \emph{non-asymptotic}: the only hypothesis is
membership in $\Thgen$, an explicit and measurable condition.

Theorem~\ref{thm:po2} is the periodic-activation entry in a classical line: permutation and sign
for odd activations \citep{hechtnielsen1990algebraic}, uniqueness for minimal tanh networks
\citep{sussmann1992uniqueness}, functional equivalence more generally
\citep{kurkova1994functionally}, and reconstruction of sigmoid networks from their output via pole
structure \citep{fefferman1994reconstructing} --- of which our Fourier-atom argument is the
periodic analogue. What is new here is the \emph{phase} component: none of those groups contains a
continuous family of bias shifts, and it is the $\tau$-circle that makes canonicalization globally
obstructed (Proposition~\ref{prop:po5}) rather than obstructed only at ties.

\begin{conjecture}[Deep case]\label{conj:deep}
For $L\ge2$, off a proper analytic subset of parameter space, $f_\theta = f_{\theta'}$ implies
$\theta' = g\theta$ for some $g\in G$.
\end{conjecture}

Our own attempt on Conjecture~\ref{conj:deep} reduces it, via Jacobi--Anger, to the uniqueness of a
CP decomposition in a Bessel basis, and stalls on two lemmas: generic full column rank of the
Bessel--Vandermonde matrices $[J_k(x_j)]$, and control of the infinite tail when the decomposition
is identified from finitely many observed coefficients. The rank lemma is numerically supported and
looks completable; the same numerics show the system is severely ill-conditioned by $n=5$
($\min|\det|$ falling from $4.4\times10^{-3}$ to $4.4\times10^{-13}$ between $n=2$ and $n=5$), which
is what motivated attacking the conjecture empirically instead (\S\ref{sec:s4e}). The attempt is released as a proof memo.

\begin{proposition}[No continuous canonicalization]\label{prop:po5}
There is no continuous $\kappa : \Thgen \to \Thgen$ with $\kappa(\theta) \in \Orb(\theta)$ and
$\kappa(g\theta) = \kappa(\theta)$ for all $g \in \langle\sigma,\rho,\tau\rangle$. One hidden
neuron and $m=c=1$ suffice.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take $\theta_t = (1,t,1)$, $t\in[0,2\pi]$. Write $\kappa(\theta_t) = g_{d_t,j_t}\theta_t$.
Continuity of the first coordinate forces $d_t \equiv d$; of the third, the parity of $j_t$ is
constant; then $j_t$ is a continuous integer-valued function, hence constant. But
$\theta_{2\pi} = \tau_1\theta_0$, so invariance requires equal bias coordinates while the normal
form \eqref{eq:normalform} gives a difference of $(-1)^d 2\pi \neq 0$.
\end{proof}

This is the sine-specific counterpart of the general topological obstruction of
\citet{dym2024impossibility}. It is stronger than the permutation case in an operationally
relevant way: for permutations alone the obstruction is confined to sorting-key ties, whereas the
$\tau$-circle makes it global. Any exact canonicalizer must therefore be discontinuous on a set
meeting every loop that winds the bias circle; we measure the empirical mass near those sets.

\begin{proposition}[Completeness is function access]\label{prop:po6}
Assume the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:po2}. If $I : \Thgen \to X$ is $G$-invariant and
\emph{complete} ($I(\theta)=I(\theta') \Rightarrow \theta' \in G\theta$), then $I = \bar I \circ R$
factors through the realization map $R:\theta \mapsto f_\theta$, with $\bar I$ injective on
$R(\Thgen)$. Conversely any injective encoding of $R(\Thgen)$ pulls back to a complete invariant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:ceiling}
A complete-invariant weight-space perceiver receives \emph{exactly} the information of a
function-space perceiver. Any advantage of weight access over function access must therefore be
computational (amortization: canonicalize once, decode many times), or must come from
deliberately \emph{incomplete} invariants, or from non-pointwise processing.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Scope]\label{rem:scope}
Proposition~\ref{prop:po6} and Corollary~\ref{cor:ceiling} are derived \emph{under the conclusion
of Theorem~\ref{thm:po2}}, which is proved for $L=1$ only. At $L=2$, where all our corpora live,
they are conditional on Conjecture~\ref{conj:deep}. The FLOPs-matched comparison of
\S\ref{sec:s5} is therefore an empirical result at $L=2$, not a consequence of the proved theorem;
we present it as such.
\end{remark}

Corollary~\ref{cor:ceiling} is a ceiling on the enterprise, not a refutation of it: it says the
justification for weight-space learning has to be stated in the currency of compute or of
deliberate lossiness, and it is what makes a FLOPs-matched comparison against function-query
methods such as \citet{horwitz2025probegen} the appropriate test.

\section{Exact invariants at depth two}\label{sec:deep}
% =====================================================================================

Requiring monomial invariance $g(b)P_\alpha(w)Q_\beta(u)$ under \eqref{eq:normalform} --- $g$
must be $2\pi$-periodic, $g(b+\pi) = (-1)^\beta g(b)$, $g(-b)=(-1)^{\alpha+\beta}g(b)$ --- yields
the minimal-frequency solutions $(\alpha,\beta) = (0,0) : 1, \cos 2b$; $(1,0): \sin 2b$;
$(0,1): \sin b$; $(1,1): \cos b$, and hence a separating per-neuron feature map on
$\{w\neq0,u\neq0\}$:
\begin{equation}
\Phi(w,b,u) \;=\; \Big( w\otimes w,\ \cos 2b,\ (\sin 2b)\,w,\ (\sin b)\,u,\ (\cos b)\,(w\otimes u)\Big).
\label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
\emph{Note} that the features one would guess --- $\cos(2b)(w\otimes u)$, $\sin(2b)(w\otimes u)$
--- are \emph{not} invariant: the first has the wrong bias frequency for $\rho$, the second the
wrong parity for $\sigma$. Both are refuted numerically in our test suite.

Equation~\eqref{eq:phi} is scoped to $L=1$. At depth two a hidden neuron's outgoing vector $u_i$
is a \emph{column of a matrix the next layer's group also acts on}, so the $(0,1)$ and $(1,1)$
classes stop being invariant and per-neuron constructions fail. Our repair couples the layers.

\begin{proposition}[Exact $L=2$ invariant encoding]\label{prop:deep}
Let $G_2 = W_2^\top W_2$. Then $G_2$ is invariant under the \emph{entire} layer-2 group (row sign
flips cancel inside each inner product, row permutations reindex the sum, phase shifts touch
$b^2$ and $W^3$ only), and under layer~1 it picks up $\varepsilon_i \varepsilon_l$ with
$\varepsilon_i = (-1)^{d_i + j_i}$. Since $\sin b_i$ carries exactly $\varepsilon_i$ and
$\cos b_i\, w_i$ carries $\varepsilon_i$ after contraction, the matrices
\begin{equation}
A = (\sin b_i \sin b_l)\,G_2, \qquad
B = (\cos b_i w_i \!\cdot\! \cos b_l w_l)\,G_2, \qquad
C = (\sin 2b_i w_i \!\cdot\! \sin 2b_l w_l)
\end{equation}
are sign-cancelling and transform as $M \mapsto P M P^\top$ under the layer-1 permutation. Their
sorted eigenvalue spectra are therefore invariant under the full product group.
\end{proposition}

Per-neuron even scalars are emitted as order statistics under a shared invariant sort key, and
layer~2 uses \eqref{eq:phi} contracted over the layer-1 index. The encoding has $D = 320$ for our
grayscale corpora and $D = 384$ for CIFAR-10 (three output channels).
Appendix~\ref{app:w10} specifies the emitted vector coordinate by coordinate, proves invariance of
each coordinate, and states what is not claimed --- the family is invariant but not shown to
separate orbits, the spectra are $1$-Lipschitz by Weyl's inequality while the shared-key order
statistics are discontinuous where the key ties. We verify invariance by
applying random group elements with windings $|j|\le3$ and non-trivial permutations: the relative
residual move is $\approx 3\times10^{-7}$, i.e.\ fp32 round-off rather than tolerance slack, with
controls for non-constancy and for wrong depth. Whether a finite invariant family stays separating
at $L \ge 3$ is open (\S\ref{sec:limits}).

% =====================================================================================
\section{INR-Bench: corpora, protocols, gates}\label{sec:bench}
% =====================================================================================

A decomposition needs corpora that differ in \emph{exactly} the nuisance under study. We fit one
SIREN per image of MNIST \citep{lecun1998mnist}, FashionMNIST \citep{xiao2017fashion} and CIFAR-10
\citep{krizhevsky2009cifar} ($L=2$, width $32$, $\omega_0$ absorbed) under four protocols that intervene on
the arguments of the fit map $F : (y, \theta_0, \xi) \mapsto \theta_T$, where $y$ is the target
signal, $\theta_0$ the initialization and $\xi$ the optimization noise:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
protocol & $\theta_0$ & $\xi$ & isolates \\
\midrule
\texttt{P-shared-det}   & fixed & fixed & the ceiling: no nuisance at all \\
\texttt{P-shared-stoch} & fixed & drawn & optimization noise alone \\
\texttt{P-random}       & drawn & drawn & the realistic, independently-fitted setting \\
\texttt{P-random-K}     & $K{=}8$ draws per image & drawn & the nuisance, sampled, for marginalization \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Quality gates.} A corpus is admitted only if a small CNN trained on \emph{renders} of
the fitted INRs matches one trained on real pixels, so that no rung can be explained by lost
signal. All gates pass: render-vs-pixel accuracy gaps of $-0.09$ to $+0.84$ points across nine
dataset$\times$protocol cells, with median render PSNR $39.2$\,dB (MNIST), $43.4$\,dB
(FashionMNIST) and $40.1$\,dB (CIFAR-10) on the shared-deterministic corpora. We audit the strata of
\S\ref{sec:group}: production fits satisfy genericity, but \emph{marginally} --- with 32
first-layer directions in a 2-D input space, minimal parallel angles are
$3\times10^{-4}$--$2\times10^{-3}$\,rad, so orbits pass near the parallel stratum. That is a
conditioning statement, not an identifiability failure, and we report it as such.

\paragraph{Splits.} Splits are assigned by \emph{image}, and a schema check refuses a corpus whose
image ids are not disjoint across train, validation and test; for \texttt{P-random-K} this puts all
$K=8$ fitted views of an image in the same partition, and for the random-initialization protocols a
second check enforces disjointness of \texttt{(init\_seed, fit\_seed)} pairs between test and the
rest. The quality gates below are computed on the training and validation splits; no test cell was
decoded before the corpus and the fitting protocol were accepted.

\paragraph{Scale.} Three datasets $\times$ four protocols $=$ approximately $1.8$M fitted INRs,
generated on a single Apple M4 laptop. CIFAR-10 alone is $540{,}000$ fits ($60$k $+$ $60$k $+$
$60$k $+$ $8\times45$k) in $55{,}510$\,s of recorded wall-clock, i.e.\ $15.4$ hours at $9.7$ fits per
second. That rate comes from fitting a whole shard as one batched optimization: the shard's INRs
share the forward pass, with per-INR parameters carried in the leading tensor dimension, and
per-INR losses share no parameters, so Adam on their mean is exactly independent per-INR Adam. A
``fit'' is therefore an independently initialized and independently optimized parameter vector, not
an independent process. Shard sizes, batch dimensions, peak memory, per-corpus wall-clock and the
separate active-compute accounting are in the released compute ledger. Every corpus, gate and audit
is generated by a numbered, idempotent script.

\section{The decomposition ladder}\label{sec:ladder}
% =====================================================================================

The ladder applies different \emph{feature maps} to the same underlying corpora and decodes each
with one frozen apparatus: a matched MLP $[D \to 1024 \to 512 \to 256 \to 10]$, GELU, dropout
$0.1$, AdamW $10^{-3}$, cosine schedule, early stopping on a held-out INR validation split. Only
the feature map changes.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llp{7.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
rung & corpus & feature map \\
\midrule
P0 & --- & real pixels (the reference task) \\
P1 & shared-det & oracle render of the fitted INR (information-preservation control) \\
W1 & shared-det & raw flattened weights, \emph{no} nuisance \\
W2 & shared-stoch & raw weights, optimization noise only \\
W3 & random & raw weights, the realistic setting \\
W4 & random & $\csort$: exact canonicalization by neuron sort, template-free \\
W5 & random & $\calign$: exact canonicalization by sort $+$ sign/phase alignment to $\theta_0$ \\
W6 & random & raw weights $+$ bounded group augmentation at train time \\
W7 & random-K & $K$-marginalization: all 8 fitted views as training rows \\
W8 & random & canonicalize, \emph{then} augment inside the canonical frame \\
W9 & random & frame averaging over $R=64$ group elements \\
W10 & random & the exact $L{=}2$ invariants of Proposition~\ref{prop:deep} \\
X1 & both & train on W1 features, evaluate on W3 features, and the reverse \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The quantity of interest is the \textbf{algorithm-relative recoverable fraction}
\begin{equation}
f(\mathrm{W}k) \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{W}k - \mathrm{W3}}{\mathrm{W1} - \mathrm{W3}} \;\in\; \R,
\label{eq:f}
\end{equation}
the share of the shared-versus-random \emph{accuracy} gap that feature map $k$ recovers for this
learning algorithm. Absolute accuracies inherit the task ceiling; $f$ does not, which makes it the
object that can be compared across datasets. It is \emph{not} a causal share of the gap, for
reasons set out in \S\ref{sec:bounds}; negative values occur (W8, W9) and simply mean the map is
worse than raw weights.

\paragraph{Pre-registration.} Every rung definition, hypothesis, point prediction with an $80\%$
interval, seed count, exclusion rule and falsification condition was frozen in a committed,
hash-stamped document before any cell was computed, following the pre-registration practice
argued for in \citep{munafo2017manifesto,nosek2018preregistration}. Seed counts were sized from a
\emph{measured} paired-difference SD ($0.210$ points for rungs whose decoder input is a fixed
matrix, $0.721$ for rungs that redraw it each step), giving $n=5$ and $n=15$ respectively;
at $n=5$ the second class would have had TOST power $0.20$. Rung comparisons use paired-by-seed
$t$ and Wilcoxon tests with Holm correction \citep{holm1979simple}, equivalence via TOST
\citep{schuirmann1987tost}, and a label-shuffle control at W1, W3 and W5
\citep{zhang2017rethinking}. All registered predictions are scored, and deviations and exploratory
analyses are identified as such wherever they appear; the full ledger and its calibration are in
App.~\ref{sec:calibration}.

The registrations are \emph{sequential}, not one joint confirmatory experiment, and the sequence
matters for how they should be read: the grayscale arm was registered first and its outcomes
informed the CIFAR-10 predictions, which in turn informed the mechanism and identifiability arms;
two identifiability rows were informed by an under-powered pilot and are flagged pilot-informed in
the ledger. Sample sizes are also structured. The $1.8$M fitted INRs are not $1.8$M independent
replications: the units of uncertainty are datasets, images, fit realizations and decoder seeds,
and the paired tests here resolve only the last of those.

\paragraph{The size of the gap.} On MNIST, W1 $=94.36$ against W3 $=13.92$: a
$\mathbf{80.4}$-point gap between two corpora of networks fitted to \emph{the same images}, with
the same architecture, differing only in whether the initialization was shared. FashionMNIST gives
$70.3$ points and CIFAR-10 $31.6$. X1 shows the two representations are not even rescalings of one
another: on all three datasets a decoder trained on W1 features scores at chance on W3 features
and vice versa ($10.7 / 13.2$ on MNIST; $10.4 / 12.0$ on CIFAR-10).

\paragraph{It is not decoder inadequacy.} P1 $\approx$ P0 everywhere --- on MNIST TOST declares
equivalence at a $1.0$-point margin ($p = 2.5\times10^{-5}$), and on CIFAR-10 the render is
\emph{marginally better} than real pixels ($56.23$ vs $55.81$), which at $40$\,dB is the mild
denoising a near-exact render provides. Fitting destroys no class information. W1 then sits
$3.2$ points below P1 on MNIST and $11.9$ on CIFAR-10, which bounds decoder-vs-representation loss
under \emph{zero} nuisance. A decoder that were simply too weak could not be rescued by a
function-preserving change of frame --- and reframing rescues it by $50$ points on MNIST.
(A striking aside: on CIFAR-10 a \emph{linear} probe reads the shared-init weights better than it
reads the pixels, $40.1$ vs $33.0$. The fit map is doing useful feature extraction; the problem is
purely that independent fits write the result in incompatible coordinates.)

\paragraph{Optimization noise is null on all three datasets.} W1 $-$ W2 $= -0.68$ (MNIST),
$-0.70$ (FashionMNIST), $-0.90$ (CIFAR-10): stochastic fitting from a shared initialization is, if
anything, marginally \emph{better}. We had registered $+2.0$ points $[0,6]$ on MNIST and were
wrong; having learned that, we registered $-0.7$ $[-2.5,+1.0]$ for CIFAR-10 and hit. The gap is
attributable to the initialization, not to the trajectory.

\paragraph{Reframing recovers much of the gap, and the fraction is not a constant.}
$f(\mathrm{W5}) = 0.628$ (MNIST) and $0.664$ (FashionMNIST). We had registered $0.10$, with an
$80\%$ interval reaching only to $0.30$, and a pre-committed rule stating that $f > 0.5$ falsifies
the ``canonicalization is not enough'' claim and requires it to be restated. It fired: an exact
orbit-valued reframing recovers about two thirds of the accuracy gap on these corpora.

We then registered that restatement as a prediction for CIFAR-10 --- $f(\mathrm{W5}) = 0.62$,
$80\%$ $[0.42, 0.78]$, with an explicit falsifier at $f < 0.30$ --- and it missed in the other
direction: $f(\mathrm{W5}) = \mathbf{0.325}$, just clear of the falsification line. The two-thirds
figure is a property of the grayscale corpora, not a law. What survives on all three datasets is
the weaker claim that \emph{an exact reframing recovers between a third and two thirds of the
accuracy gap, while no inexact treatment recovers more than an eighth of it}. Whether the
unrecovered part is non-group structure or merely a better representative we did not find is not
decided by these numbers; \S\ref{sec:orbit} is the design that addresses it.

\paragraph{The 0.11-to-0.63 span is a property of the frame, not of the information.} Template-free
sorting recovers $f = 0.177 / 0.170 / 0.108$; aligning to a fixed reference network recovers
$0.628 / 0.664 / 0.325$. Both are exact elements of $G$; the difference is entirely which orbit
representative is chosen. The practical message for architecture designers is that the ceiling on
frame choice is high and current template-free canonicalization is nowhere near it.

\paragraph{The alignment template does not matter} (Fig.~\ref{fig:template}). A natural objection

\paragraph{The inexact treatments recover little, under our implementations and budgets.} Across

decoder just gained. Frame averaging's failure is the expected one: parity-odd coordinates cancel
toward zero, so it is a lossy invariantization where W10 is an exact one.

\paragraph{Scope of the three null results.} $\Dinf$ is infinite, so there is no uniform measure to
average or augment over and no canonical approximation to group integration: W6 and W9 are
instantiated at one winding distribution (uniform on $|j|\le3$), on raw bias coordinates, averaging
features rather than predictions. A different truncation, a bias reduction modulo $2\pi$, or
averaging at the prediction level could behave differently, and the augmentation distribution can
itself put mass on bias magnitudes no fitted network occupies. W7 likewise consumes its $K$ views as
independent training rows rather than jointly at inference, which is the weaker of the two things
``marginalization'' can mean. What the ladder establishes is that \emph{these} instantiations
recover at most $12.8\%$ of the gap; it does not establish that principled invariant integration
cannot do better. The one place augmentation looks best is CIFAR-10, where its $0.128$ edges past
$\csort$'s $0.108$ and past its own registered ceiling of $0.12$; we record that as a miss (H-C1-9).

\section{What the ladder measures, and what it does not}\label{sec:bounds}
% =====================================================================================

An earlier version of this paper claimed that $f$ is a certified lower bound on the share of the
gap caused by symmetry. That claim is wrong. The argument for it fails at a specific step, which we set out
because the same step is implicit elsewhere in this literature.

The argument was: an exact reframing $c(\theta) \in G\theta$ preserves the function, so it creates
no information about the represented signal, so any accuracy it buys must come from removing
nuisance. The last step does not follow. An orbit-valued map can \emph{route} an orbit-invariant
quantity into a coordinate the reader finds easy to read. Concretely, let $y(\theta)$ be any
orbit-invariant binary property and define $c$ to apply $\tau_{k}$ at the first neuron with
$k = M\,y(\theta)$ for a large constant $M$. Then $c(\theta) \in G\theta$ and
$f_{c(\theta)} = f_\theta$ exactly, yet a linear probe on the first bias now predicts $y$. No
function-level information was created; the group's degrees of freedom were used as a
communication channel. Function preservation therefore does not license a causal reading.

Two further gaps separate $f$ from a causal share. The decoder is retrained per rung, so what is
held fixed is the learning \emph{algorithm}, not a fixed predictor --- the quantity is relative to
that algorithm and its sample size. And the comparison that defines $f$ contrasts corpora fitted
from \emph{different initializations}, which is an intervention on the fit map, not on the group.

\begin{definition}[Algorithm-relative recoverable fraction]\label{def:f}
For a learning algorithm $A$ (architecture, optimiser, budget) and a feature map $\phi$,
\begin{equation}
f_A(\phi) \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{acc}_A(\phi(\Theta_{\mathrm{rand}})) - \mathrm{acc}_A(\Theta_{\mathrm{rand}})}
{\mathrm{acc}_A(\Theta_{\mathrm{shared}}) - \mathrm{acc}_A(\Theta_{\mathrm{rand}})} \;\in\; \R .
\end{equation}
It is the share of the shared-versus-random \emph{accuracy} gap that $\phi$ recovers for $A$.
Values below zero mean $\phi$ is worse than raw weights; values above one would mean it beats the
shared-initialization corpus.
\end{definition}

This is a real quantity and the ladder measures it cleanly, but it is not ``the fraction of the gap
caused by symmetry''. The estimand that \emph{is} causal requires intervening on the group while
holding the network and its function fixed, which is the design of \S\ref{sec:orbit}: sample
$g_i \sim \mu_B$ independently per INR, apply it to a corpus that has no initialization nuisance,
and measure what that alone does. We report both, and we keep them typographically distinct: $f_A$
throughout for the ladder, $\Delta_{\mathrm{sym}}$ for the intervention.

\paragraph{$\calign$ is a canonicalizer on these corpora, not an alignment heuristic.} The
distinction matters for how W4 and W5 may be described, so we measure it rather than assume it. On
$512$ held-out INRs per dataset, under four random group elements each with windings $|j|\le3$ and
non-trivial permutations, the residual $\|c(g\theta) - c(\theta)\|$ relative to each INR's own scale
has median $1.2\times10^{-7}$ and maximum $1.5\times10^{-7}$ for both $\csort$ and $\calign$, on
MNIST and on CIFAR-10, with \emph{no} INR above the $10^{-4}$ tolerance. That is fp32 round-off.
Proposition~\ref{prop:po5} still applies --- these maps must be discontinuous somewhere, and the
audit of \S\ref{sec:bench} shows the corpora pass close to the stratum where the sort keys nearly
tie --- but on the sampled orbits they are exact canonicalizers, not heuristics.

\paragraph{Reframings and encodings are different objects.} W4 and W5 are \emph{reframings}: they
return another parameter vector in the same orbit, computing no new features. W10 is an
\emph{invariant encoding} and W11b a reader over the same invariant objects: they return features,
and those features are nonlinear in the parameters ($w \otimes w$, $(\sin b)u$, eigenvalue spectra).
An encoding's gain is therefore not separable from ordinary nonlinear feature engineering except by
a matched non-invariant control, which is \S\ref{sec:control}. We keep the two families apart in
every table and we do not quote an encoding's number as though it were a reframing's.

Finally, Proposition~\ref{prop:po5} says no continuous exact canonicalizer exists, so even the best
reframing is discontinuous somewhere, and the residual after any one of them mixes imperfect frame
choice with whatever is not a frame at all. That is why the residual is not interpreted here.
Section~\ref{sec:orbit} attacks the same question from the other side, with an intervention that
removes the group instead of a map that tries to quotient it.

% =====================================================================================
\section{The orbit-only intervention}\label{sec:orbit}
% =====================================================================================

Section~\ref{sec:bounds} showed that the ladder measures an algorithm-relative recoverable
fraction, not a causal share. The causal quantity needs an intervention on the group rather than
on the initialization, and the design is direct.

Take a corpus with \emph{no} initialization nuisance --- \texttt{P-shared-det}, every INR fitted
from the same $\theta_0$. Hold each fitted network and its realised function fixed. Draw an
independent group element per INR and apply it:
\begin{equation}
\theta_i \;\longmapsto\; g_i\theta_i, \qquad g_i \sim \mu_B \ \text{i.i.d.}
\label{eq:orbit-intervention}
\end{equation}
Because the same networks and the same functions appear on both sides --- we verify the residual
functional gap is at machine precision --- any degradation is caused by the group action and by
nothing else. Write $\Delta_{\mathrm{sym}}(A,\mu_B)$ for that degradation under learning algorithm
$A$, and score each treatment by the share of $\Delta_{\mathrm{sym}}$ it recovers.

There is no uniform probability measure on the infinite group $\Dinf$, so $\mu$ must be a family
rather than a single distribution: windings $j \sim \mathrm{Unif}\{-B,\dots,B\}$, signs
$d \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)$, permutations uniform on $S_n$. Every quantity is reported as a
function of $B$, and we run a second arm with the permutation fixed to the identity, which isolates
the $\Dinf$ part from the permutation part --- the decomposition that decides whether the affine
phase component this paper emphasises matters empirically or only mathematically.

\input{tables/orbit_table}

\paragraph{The group alone costs about as much as the whole observed gap.} Randomizing $g$ over the
untouched \texttt{P-shared-det} corpus takes the decoder from $94.36\%$ to $15.3\%$:
$\Delta_{\mathrm{sym}} = 79.07$ at $B=0$ and $79.09$ at $B=10$, flat in the winding bound. The
entire shared-versus-random gap on the same dataset is $80.4$ points. Known group scatter alone
therefore reproduces nearly all of the degradation that independent initialization produces --- but
the two are not the same intervention, and the recoveries separate them. $\calign$ returns $86\%$ of
$\Delta_{\mathrm{sym}}$ against $63\%$ of the observed gap; the invariant encoding $72\%$ against
$27\%$; $\csort$ $58\%$ against $18\%$. Every treatment does markedly better against synthetic
scatter than against independent fits, which is the first direct evidence that the observed gap
contains something the group does not. The registration named the caveat in advance: the scattered
corpus is a favourable case for $\calign$ by construction, since every scattered network is exactly
a group image of one fitted from the template's own initialization.

\paragraph{An independently initialized corpus is already group-saturated.} Applying the same
intervention to \texttt{P-random} costs $+0.13$ points at $B=0$ and $-0.50$ at $B=3$ --- nothing,
within seed noise, and the registered call P-S6-B ($0.85$) resolves as expected. The raw reader on
\texttt{P-random} is at a floor that additional exact group scatter cannot lower. Note also that
$\calign$ reaches $64.39\%$ on the scattered \texttt{P-random} corpus against $64.41\%$ on the
unscattered one, a second empirical statement of the canonicalizer property of \S\ref{sec:bounds}.

\paragraph{An exactly invariant reader still loses $28.6$ points, and the group cannot explain it.}
This is the sharpest result of the intervention, and it comes from a triple. W11b --- the
$G$-invariant equivariant reader of \S\ref{sec:w11} --- scores $84.81\%$ on the untouched
\texttt{P-shared-det} corpus and $85.39\%$ on the \emph{same corpus after the group has been
randomized} at $B=3$. The difference, $0.59$ points, is seed noise: its invariance is not merely
asserted by construction but measured (this was registered as validity check H-S6-5 and hits). The
same reader scores $56.24\%$ on \texttt{P-random}. So a reader that is empirically blind to
$\Dinf \wr S_n$ still loses $28.6$ points between the shared-initialization and
independent-initialization corpora, and \emph{that} loss cannot be group scatter.

What it can be is narrower than before but not yet settled. It is not lost class information in the
signal: the render gates pass on both protocols and function-query accuracy moves only $5.4$ points
between them (\S\ref{sec:s5}). It is not the group. The two surviving explanations are that
independent fits land on genuinely different orbits --- which S4e measures directly, two fits of the
same image sitting at orbit distance $0.279$ against $0.280$ for two fits of \emph{different} images
--- and that W10's invariant family is incomplete in a way that reads more from a shared chart than
from independently scattered ones. An incomplete invariant cannot separate those two, so we do not
claim a decomposition; what the triple does establish is that the residual survives an intervention
that removes the group entirely, which the canonicalizer residual of \S\ref{sec:bounds} could not
show.

\paragraph{Within the group, reflection dominates and winding is nearly free.} With the permutation
fixed to the identity, $\Delta_{\mathrm{sym}}$ is still $62.9$ points at $B=0$ and $64.1$ at
$B=10$: of the $79$ points that the full group costs, roughly $63$ are already bought by
per-neuron sign flips alone, about $15$ by relabelling $32$ neurons, and about $1$ by windings up
to $|j|=10$. Two registered rows are badly missed by this. H-S6-1 put sign flips at $10$ points
$[2,30]$ and H-S6-3 put the permutation share at $64$ $[45,76]$; the observed values are $62.90$ and
$14.74$, i.e.\ the two components' roles are the reverse of what we registered, and the probability
call P-S6-A ($0.80$ that permutations dominate) resolves false. S6 scores $3/6$ intervals, its worst
arm, and all three misses are this one finding.

The consequence for this paper's framing is specific and cuts both ways. The $\Dinf$ component
dominates the permutation component by four to one, which is the empirical case for treating the
sine group as more than $S_n$. But \emph{within} $\Dinf$ it is the reflection generator $\sigma$
that carries almost all of it, and $\sigma$ is exactly the part that monomial-matrix frameworks
already cover for periodic activations \citep{tran2024monomial}. The affine phase component ---
the piece outside every linear classification, and the one this paper's group-theoretic
contribution turns on --- costs about one accuracy point at $B=10$ under this measure. It is
mathematically necessary for the identifiability statement and empirically minor as a source of
scatter. Those are different claims and the paper makes them separately.

\section{Is the invariant encoding's gain about invariance?}\label{sec:control}
% =====================================================================================

W10 is both a nonlinear feature map and a $G$-invariant one, so its accuracy attributes nothing to
symmetry on its own; a map that is equally nonlinear and not invariant might do as well. S7 supplies
the control the ladder was missing. Rung \textbf{W10c} emits, coordinate for coordinate, the same
monomials in $(w,u)$ at the same trigonometric orders as W10, pooled by the same eigenvalue spectra
under the same $\|w\|^2$ sort key, at the same dimension, decoded by the same frozen apparatus. Only
the parity class of each trigonometric factor changes: $\sin b_i \sin b_l \to \cos b_i \cos b_l$
against the Gram, $\cos b_i w_i \to \sin b_i w_i$, $\sin 2b_i w_i \to \cos 2b_i w_i$, and similarly
per neuron and in layer two. The three matrices remain symmetric and still transform as
$M \mapsto PMP^\top$, so W10c is \emph{still exactly permutation-invariant} and is broken only in
$\Dinf$; the test suite asserts both halves, at a relative move above $10^{-2}$ under the full group
and below $10^{-5}$ under permutations alone. The difference between the two rungs is therefore
attributable to the $\Dinf$ component specifically.

\input{tables/control_table}

\paragraph{Most of the encoding's CIFAR-10 gain is invariance; a substantial minority is not.}
$f(\mathrm{W10c}) = 0.125$ on MNIST against W10's $0.269$, and $0.216$ on CIFAR-10 against
$0.534$. All three registered intervals hit --- $0.14\,[0.02,0.28]$, $0.22\,[0.05,0.42]$ and, for
the difference the review actually asked for, $0.31\,[0.11,0.48]$ against an observed $0.318$. The
pre-committed falsifier ($f(\mathrm{W10c}) \ge f(\mathrm{W10}) - 0.05$ on CIFAR-10, which would
have voided every symmetry reading of the CIFAR-10 encoding result) did not fire.

So of W10's $0.534$ on CIFAR-10, $0.318$ is attributable to quotienting $\Dinf$ and $0.216$ is
what the same nonlinearity buys without it. On MNIST the split is $0.144$ against $0.125$. The
encoding's advantage over an exact reframing on natural images is therefore a genuine symmetry
effect and not a feature-engineering artifact --- but it is not \emph{purely} a symmetry effect
either, and the second registered probability call is the one that says so.

\paragraph{The nonlinearity contributes on its own, which changes how W10 and W4 compare.}
P-S7-B put $0.55$ on $f(\mathrm{W10c}) \le f(\mathrm{W4})$ on CIFAR-10 --- that breaking $\Dinf$
invariance would cost more than the nonlinear pooling buys. It resolves false: $0.216$ against
$\csort$'s $0.108$. A feature map with the same nonlinearity and \emph{no} $\Dinf$ invariance
already doubles template-free canonicalization. The registration fixed in advance that this must be
stated wherever W10 is compared with W4, so: the W10-versus-W4 comparison is not a clean
symmetry-handling comparison, because the two differ in nonlinearity as well. The clean comparison
is W10 against W10c, and it is the one we now quote.

\section{The crossover: alignment and invariance trade places}\label{sec:crossover}
% =====================================================================================

The two exact methods move in \emph{opposite} directions as the signal gets harder:
\begin{center}\small

\toprule
& MNIST & FashionMNIST & CIFAR-10 \\
\midrule
$f(\mathrm{W5})$, alignment to a fixed reference & 0.628 & 0.664 & \textbf{0.325} \\
$f(\mathrm{W10})$, exact $L{=}2$ invariants      & 0.269 & 0.428 & \textbf{0.534} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Alignment halves; the invariant encoding nearly doubles, and on CIFAR-10 it \emph{overtakes}
alignment. This is not a post-hoc observation about which method happened to win. The
pre-registration for the CIFAR arm carried an explicit probability call --- $P = 0.60$ that
$f(\mathrm{W10})_{\text{CIFAR}} > f(\mathrm{W10})_{\text{MNIST}} = 0.269$ --- justified by the
encoding's algebra rather than by extrapolating the two observed values: with $c = 3$ output
channels each neuron's outgoing vector $u_i \in \R^3$ carries strictly more $\Dinf$-visible
structure than the $c = 1$ case, so the invariants built from it have more to see (and the
encoding grows from $D = 320$ to $D = 384$). That call resolved correctly. The companion call ---
that the grayscale ordering $f(\mathrm{W5}) > f(\mathrm{W10})$ would persist, $P = 0.65$ ---
resolved incorrectly, and the crossover is the reason.

\paragraph{The registered mechanism is real but small (ablation).} P-C1-B attributed W10's rise to
the three output channels. We can test that \emph{within} the CIFAR corpus, without new fitting,
by changing only what the encoder is allowed to read (exploratory, not registered):

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llrr@{}}
\toprule
arm & what the encoder reads & $D$ & $f(\mathrm{W10})$ \\
\midrule
full        & all three output channels                & 384 & 0.534 \\
truncated   & output channel 0 only                    & 320 & 0.457 \\
averaged    & the three channels' mean                 & 320 & 0.425 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Truncating to one channel --- which restores exactly the grayscale encoding dimension --- costs
only $0.077$. Against the $0.265$ rise from MNIST's $0.269$ to CIFAR's $0.534$, the channel count
therefore explains about $29\%$; the other $71\%$ survives at $D = 320$ and is a property of the
corpus, not of $c$. The registered mechanism is therefore right in direction and wrong in magnitude. (The \emph{averaged} arm is
the useful control: it has the same dimension as \emph{truncated} and strictly more of the
network's information, yet does \emph{worse}, so the effect is not ``more feature dimensions'' and
not ``more information'' --- channel-averaging cancels the per-channel sign structure that
$(\sin b_i)u_i$ exists to carry.)

\paragraph{It is not that CIFAR's fits ran further.} The obvious confound is fit length: the
CIFAR corpora were frozen at $1000$ steps against $300$ for the grayscale ones, and a fit that
travels further from $\theta_0$ should be harder to align back to it. We measured the travel
directly on the shared-initialization corpora, with no new fitting. It is
\emph{indistinguishable} across datasets: median relative travel
$\|\theta_T - \theta_0\| / \|\theta_0\|$ of $0.186$ (MNIST), $0.191$ (FashionMNIST) and $0.187$
(CIFAR-10), with median layer-1 direction cosine to the initialization of $0.998$, $0.999$ and
$0.999$ respectively --- CIFAR-10 is if anything the \emph{least} moved. The extra $700$ steps did
not translate into extra displacement, so the drop in $f(\mathrm{W5})$ is not a fit-length
artifact and the laziness that makes alignment work at all is equally present everywhere.

\paragraph{It is not the output-channel count either --- a conjecture of ours, tested and
withdrawn.} An earlier draft of this paper conjectured a mechanism: $\calign$ matches neurons by
correlating their layer-1 \emph{activations}, a statistic blind to the outgoing structure --- which
is exactly the part that grows with $c$, and exactly what W10's Gram coupling reads. That predicts
alignment should recover once $c=1$. We registered it as a falsifiable prediction
(\texttt{S1-gray}, probability calls at $0.35$ and $0.45$) and built the corpus that tests it:
\textbf{luminance CIFAR-10}, the identical images at the identical geometry, architecture and fit
budget, with the output-channel count changed from three to one.

\input{tables/gray_table}

The conjecture is \textbf{wrong}, and the registration fixed in advance that both calls resolving
against it would withdraw rather than qualify it. At $c=1$ the recovery fraction is $f(\mathrm{W5}) = 0.324$ ---
\emph{identical}, to three decimals, to the $0.324$ measured at $c=3$ on the same images --- and the
crossover does not reverse ($f(\mathrm{W10}) = 0.493$, still above W5). Luminance CIFAR behaves like
RGB CIFAR, not like the grayscale corpora. Both probability calls resolved false.

The arm also removes one alternative explanation. Dropping channels makes the fit
over-parameterised, taking median render PSNR from $40$\,dB to $59.8$\,dB --- so this corpus is
fitted \emph{much more accurately} than MNIST's $39.2$\,dB and still aligns far worse. Higher
fidelity does not buy alignment recovery.

\paragraph{What survives.} Three candidate causes for the cross-dataset behaviour have now been
tested and eliminated: fit length (parameter travel is identical across corpora), output-channel
count (this arm), and render fidelity (this arm, in the helpful direction). What remains is image
statistics --- natural scenes versus centred strokes and silhouettes. We do not offer a replacement
mechanism: the one we did offer was well motivated by the algebra and false, and a second
explanation fitted to the same evidence would carry no more weight than the first.

\paragraph{The practical reading.} On low-complexity grayscale corpora, choosing a good orbit
representative is the dominant lever and an exact invariant front-end is a poor second. On natural
images the ranking reverses, at either channel count. A weight-space method that fixes one strategy
will therefore look much better on some benchmarks than others for reasons that have nothing to do
with its reader architecture --- and, as this arm shows, nothing to do with the obvious surface
properties of the corpus either.

\section{Mechanism: the fit map does not leave its initialization}\label{sec:mechanism}
% =====================================================================================

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig2_mechanism.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Why the initialization dominates.} (a) The profiled loss of the one-neuron
microcosm, certified against quadrature to $5.6\times10^{-16}$; its zero set is exactly the
$\Dinf$ orbit, and it carries 19 spurious minima besides. (b) The fraction of initializations
reaching the global orbit is non-monotone in the initialization range, peaking at range
$\approx\omega$, and this replicates across Nelder--Mead, Adam and plain gradient descent.
(c) At the setting our corpora are actually fitted with, the median parameter travel is
$\approx0.24$ \emph{independently of the initialization range}: the fit stays in its
initialization's neighbourhood.}
\label{fig:mechanism}
\end{figure}

Why should the initialization matter so much more than the trajectory? We answer in a microcosm
where everything is computable: one neuron, $g(t) = u\sin(wt+b)+c$ fitted to
$y(t) = A\sin(\omega t + \varphi) + c_0$ on $[-1,1]$. Profiling out the linear parameters
$(u,c)$ gives a closed form $\mathcal{L}^*(w,b)$ whose Gram entries are elementary; we certify it
against numerical quadrature to $5.6\times10^{-16}$. Its zero set is \emph{exactly} the $\Dinf$
orbit of the realizing parameters, and it has $19$ additional spurious local minima.

Two findings follow. First, \textbf{basin capture is non-monotone} in the initialization range,
with a sweet spot at range $\approx \omega$, and this replicates across optimizer classes
(global-capture fractions $0.00/0.20/0.56/0.31$ for converged Adam, $0.00/0.18/0.58/0.33$ for
plain gradient descent and $0.00/0.26/0.62/0.32$ for Nelder--Mead on the profiled surface, at
ranges $2/5/10/20$). Second, \textbf{at the setting our corpora are actually fitted with}
(Adam, $10^{-3}$, $300$ steps) \emph{every} initialization ends unconverged, with endpoint
gradient norm $\approx 0.5$--$0.7$ and median displacement $|\Delta w| \approx 0.24$
\emph{independently of the initialization range}: the fit stays in its initialization's
neighbourhood, so the final weights are close to the initialization plus a signal-dependent
perturbation. A shared initialization then means a shared frame, and independent initializations
mean independently scattered ones, which is what the null W1$-$W2 rung reports.

We describe this as small displacement and not as a lazy or NTK regime
\citep{jacot2018ntk,chizat2019lazy}, because displacement alone does not establish one: that would
need the linearization error, the kernel drift, or the linearized model's own fit, none of which we
measure (\S\ref{sec:limits}). The claim here is the weaker and sufficient one --- the fit map's
output is dominated by its initialization argument at this budget.

\begin{remark}[A sub-claim that was an artifact]
Our first census reported ``$100\%$ degenerate-ridge capture at range $2$''. Re-running under
gradient methods rather than Nelder--Mead on the profiled surface shows ridge capture is $0.00$:
those runs are still \emph{descending}, not sitting in a $w\approx0$ basin. An
\texttt{unconverged} class had to be added; without it we would have made a false claim about the
landscape. The headline non-monotonicity survives; the sub-claim does not.
\end{remark}

\section{Weight access against function access}\label{sec:s5}
% =====================================================================================

Corollary~\ref{cor:ceiling} says a \emph{complete} $G$-invariant of the weights carries exactly the
information of the realised function, so weight access can only win on \emph{compute}. S5 measures the comparison directly. The registration predicted, at $P = 0.85$, that querying the
function would beat every weight-space rung on \emph{both} axes.

\paragraph{The comparison.} Function access evaluates $f_\theta$ at $K$ \emph{learned} probe
coordinates and classifies the outputs; nothing reads a weight, and our test suite certifies the
reader is exactly $G$-invariant while the fitted INR receives no gradient. Learning the probes is
the strong form of the baseline: it can only move the function frontier up, which is the
conservative direction for the comparison being made here. FLOPs are analytic (one MAC $=2$ FLOPs, per INR, at
inference), not wall-clock, so the frontier reproduces off this laptop.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig7_pareto.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{The FLOPs--accuracy frontier (MNIST, \texttt{P-random}).} (a) Function access
(blue) dominates every weight-access rung (orange): $K{=}64$ learned probes reach $95.3\%$ at
$1.59$\,MFLOP where the best weight rung, $\calign$, reaches $64.4\%$ at $5.45$\,MFLOP. W1 (grey) is
the shared-initialization ceiling, which is not available for independently fitted corpora.
(b) Amortization does not rescue weight access: its \emph{per-task} decoder cost already exceeds
function-query's entire per-task cost, so the lines never cross.}
\label{fig:pareto}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Weight access is dominated on both axes.} At $K = 64$, function access reaches
$95.34\%$ for $1.59$\,MFLOP per INR. The best weight-access rung on the same corpus, $\calign$,
reaches $64.41\%$ for $5.45$\,MFLOP: \textbf{$30.9$ accuracy points worse at $3.4\times$ the
compute}. At $K = 256$ function access reaches $98.23\%$, which is above the real-pixel MLP's
$97.97\%$ --- learned query points are a better input to the same decoder than the pixels are.

\paragraph{Amortization does not rescue it.} Corollary~\ref{cor:ceiling} left one escape: canonicalize
once, decode many times. Priced, that escape closes. Weight access costs
$1.70 + 3.74\,T$\,MFLOP over $T$ downstream tasks; function-query with task-specific probes costs
$1.59\,T$. The lines never cross, because the weight reader's \emph{per-task} cost on a $1185$-dimensional
input already exceeds function-query's \emph{entire} per-task cost on a $64$-dimensional one. The
mechanism is general: reading $P$ parameters into a decoder of first-layer width $W$ costs
$\approx 2PW$, while querying $K$ points costs $\approx 2KcW + K\cdot\mathrm{siren}$, so function
access is cheaper whenever $Kc \ll P$ --- a condition on how many probes the task needs, not on how
big the INR is.

\paragraph{And the nuisance simply does not arise.} Between \texttt{P-random} and
\texttt{P-shared-det}, function-query accuracy moves $5.4$ points --- a fit-quality effect, since the
two corpora differ in median PSNR ($37.5$ vs $39.2$\,dB), not a symmetry effect. Weight access moves
$\mathbf{80.4}$ points between the same two corpora. The entire object this paper decomposes is
invisible from the function side.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} On these corpora, at this scale, and for targets that are functions
of the represented signal, querying the network is both more accurate and cheaper than every
weight-space pipeline evaluated here, including the canonicalizers, the invariant encoding and
W11b introduced in this paper.

The scope is worth stating precisely, because it is not unlimited. The result holds where the
function is cheap to query relative to the parameter count; for representations whose evaluation is
itself expensive (volumetric rendering, long-horizon dynamics) the $Kc \ll P$ condition can fail. It
holds for targets that are functions of the realised signal; a property of the \emph{training run}
that is not identifiable from the function is outside both access models' reach except through
nuisance correlation. And it is measured at width 32, depth 2, on $10$-way classification. What it
does settle is the case that has been made for weight-space learning on INR benchmarks of this kind,
and how \S\ref{sec:results}--\S\ref{sec:w11} should be read in its light: those sections measure
the structure of a representation that, for this task, is not the one we would recommend choosing.

\section{Reader architecture against frame choice}\label{sec:w11}
% =====================================================================================

Every rung so far changes the feature map and holds the reader fixed. That is what makes the
decomposition interpretable, and it is also the obvious objection: the field does not read weights
with a plain MLP, it builds permutation-equivariant architectures
\citep{navon2023dws,zhou2023nfn,lim2024gmn,kofinas2024graph,tran2024monomial}. Rung W11 supplies
the missing comparison, pre-registered, in two variants.

\textbf{W11a} is bipartite message passing on the raw weights --- layer-1 neurons, layer-2 neurons,
$W^2$ as edges --- equivariant to $S_{n_1}\times S_{n_2}$ and \emph{not} invariant to $\Dinf$. That
last property is deliberate and is asserted by the test suite as a negative control: it matches what
the permutation-equivariant family covers for periodic activations, whose phase generators are
affine and outside every monomial-matrix action. It is not a claim that no $\Dinf$-aware reader
could be built --- \S\ref{sec:limits} names the phasor construction that would be the natural
attempt. \textbf{W11b} feeds W10's \emph{own} invariants --- the
per-neuron even scalars and the sign-cancelling matrices $A, B, C$ --- to an equivariant reader with
\emph{learned} pooling in place of sorted eigenvalue spectra.

Both readers are sized by rule to the frozen decoder's $1{,}873{,}162$ parameters (within $1.5\%$),
so no row loses for being smaller.

\input{tables/w11_table}

\paragraph{Frame choice wins against this reader, at matched capacity.}
$f(\mathrm{W11a}) = 0.265$ against $f(\mathrm{W5}) = 0.628$. A capacity-matched
permutation-equivariant reader on raw weights recovers about the same share of the gap as an exact
invariant encoding with lossy pooling ($0.269$), and less than half of what an exact change of frame
recovers. The scope is this construction, at this capacity, on this corpus: what it shows is that
permutation equivariance alone does not compensate for unmodelled phase symmetry as effectively as
choosing a good orbit representative does, not that equivariance in general cannot.

\paragraph{Replacing the encoding's pooling helps substantially.} W10 was limited to $0.269$;
keeping its invariants and replacing sorted eigenvalue spectra with learned equivariant pooling
gives $0.526$, against $\calign$'s $0.628$. We do \emph{not} decompose that movement into a
``pooling'' part and an ``incompleteness'' part, because W10 and W11b differ in more than the
pooling: reader architecture, parameter count ($0.99$M against $1.85$M), relational capacity and
optimisation geometry all change together. The supported statement is the weaker one --- replacing
fixed spectral pooling with a learned equivariant reader over the same invariant objects
substantially improves accuracy --- and the factorial ablation that would license a split
(matched-capacity MLP on the same objects; richer fixed invariant statistics; the same learned
pooling over non-invariant control objects) is left as future work. What the result does support is
a practical suggestion: a $G$-invariant equivariant reader needs no template and no assignment
problem, and lands within $0.10$ of alignment. W11b is therefore this paper's $\Dinf$-aware reader,
and the comparison W11a versus W11b is the one that isolates $G$-awareness at fixed reader family
--- $0.265$ against $0.526$. Its limitation is structural rather than architectural: it is invariant
because its \emph{input} already is, so it inherits whatever the invariant family discards, and a
reader that quotients $\Dinf$ on the parameters themselves (\S\ref{sec:limits}) is the construction
we did not build.

All five registered intervals hit and all three probability calls resolved as registered, including
the one that would have withdrawn the paper's practical claim had it failed.

\section{Does identifiability have empirical content at depth two?}\label{sec:s4e}
% =====================================================================================

Everything above rests on a theorem proved at $L=1$ while every experiment is $L=2$. The
pre-registered attack on that gap (S4e) asks the question empirically: if two two-layer sine
networks realise nearly the same function, are their parameters nearly related by an element of
$G$?

\paragraph{The instrument, and the control that makes it non-vacuous.} A large residual after
$\calign$ proves nothing, because $\calign$ is a heuristic choice of representative. We therefore
minimise over the group directly. Given the other layers fixed, the optimum for one layer is
\emph{exact}: the per-neuron $\Dinf$ cost
$\|(-1)^dw_i - w^*_t\|^2 + ((-1)^db_i + \pi j - b^*_t)^2 + \|(-1)^{d+j}u_i - u^*_t\|^2$
depends on $j$ only through its parity, so four $(d,\text{parity})$ cases give the exact minimum
over the whole infinite group, and the permutation is then a Hungarian assignment on those
per-pair minima. Layers are swept by coordinate descent from several restarts.

The control is planted recovery: take $\theta$, apply a known $g$, and demand the search return
machine zero. It does, at every width, to $\approx4\times10^{-8}$ relative. This control earned its
place --- the first confirmatory launch \emph{failed} it (coordinate descent stalls on about
$10\%$ of width-2 pairs), which tripped the registration's own void condition, and that run was
discarded rather than reported. Restarts fixed it.

\input{tables/s4e_table}

\paragraph{Local recovery is well conditioned; the basin is not.} Perturbing a network by a
relative $\varepsilon$ and measuring both residuals gives $\kappa = R_\theta/R_f$, the local
condition number of the inverse map. It is \emph{small} and \emph{decreases} with width
($0.042 \to 0.0055$ from $n=2$ to $n=32$), so the forward map $\theta\mapsto f$ is strongly
expansive and local recovery is easy --- the opposite of what the Bessel--Vandermonde
ill-conditioning in our own proof memo might suggest, and a distinction worth keeping: that
determinant governs the \emph{global} recovery system, not the local Jacobian. What collapses with
width instead is the \emph{basin}. Started a distance $\varepsilon=10^{-5}$ from the true orbit,
$78$--$91\%$ of runs return to it at $n\le8$, $44\%$ at $n=16$, and \textbf{none} at $n=32$, where
the optimiser instead walks from $R_\theta = 10^{-5}$ out to $1.3\times10^{-1}$ while the function
barely improves. That is not a training-budget artifact: a control at five times the step count
leaves it unchanged ($0\%$ at $n=32$, identical $R_f$ floor; open markers in
Fig.~\ref{fig:s4e}b).

\paragraph{Independent restarts witness the orbit only at the smallest width.} At $n=2$, one of
$128$ students converged to $R_f = 5.9\times10^{-8}$ and, after optimal alignment, agreed with its
teacher to $R_\theta = 1.2\times10^{-7}$ --- float32 relative epsilon, a maximum per-coordinate
relative disagreement of $2.8\times10^{-6}$, and $2.6\times10^{-7}$ of the unrelated-network scale
at that width. That is recovery to six or seven significant figures, and it is direct positive
evidence for Conjecture~\ref{conj:deep}. At every larger width no restart came close: the best
functional residual jumps to $10^{-2}$ and the orbit residual sits in the band occupied by
\emph{entirely unrelated} networks (Fig.~\ref{fig:s4e}a). Combined with the warm-start arm, which
returns $78$--$91\%$ of the time at those same widths when started \emph{inside} the basin, the
reading is specific: the basin does not become shallower with width, its \emph{volume} collapses
relative to what random initialisation samples.

\paragraph{The registered criterion fired, and it was wrong to.} Read literally, that $n=2$ student
satisfies the pre-registered falsification criterion: $R_f < 10^{-5}$ and
$R_\theta = 1.2\times10^{-7} > 20\kappa R_f = 5.0\times10^{-8}$. It is a false positive, and the
criterion is at fault in two independent ways. It is \emph{ratio-only with no absolute floor}, so
as $R_f$ approaches machine epsilon the threshold falls below the smallest residual a float32
aligner can represent and \emph{any} exact recovery fires it. And $\kappa$ is the \emph{wrong null}
for an optimiser residual: it was measured on random perturbation directions, whereas a minimiser's
residual lies in the flattest directions of the loss --- exactly where $R_f$ is least sensitive to
$R_\theta$ --- so $R_\theta/R_f > \kappa$ is expected for any converged minimiser ($2.10$ observed
against $\kappa = 0.042$). We report the firing, the defect, and a post-hoc amendment adding an
absolute floor $R_\theta > 10^{-3}$, marked as post-hoc with the frozen text unaltered, and score
the registered probability call against the criterion \emph{as written}.

\paragraph{The production arm.} At production width, two independent fits \emph{of the same image}
sit at $R_\theta = 0.279$ after optimal alignment. Two fits of \emph{different} images sit at
$0.280$. The difference is $-0.001$. Modulo the entire symmetry group, a same-image pair is no
closer than an unrelated pair --- which is the S1 ladder's W1-versus-W3 gap seen from parameter
space rather than from a decoder.

\paragraph{What this licenses.} Conjecture~\ref{conj:deep} survives, with one width's worth of
direct positive evidence and no counterexample. But identifiability at $L=2$ has \emph{no empirical
content at production width}: the configuration that would witness it is not reachable, and the
optimiser will leave the true orbit even when placed on it. What this supports is that the $L=1$ theorem is the guarantee we have, and that the $L=2$ corpora
this paper decodes are governed by basin structure rather than by identifiability.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig5_s4e.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{S4e: identifiability versus reachability at $L=2$.} (a) Independent students
fitted to a teacher's exact outputs: orbit residual against functional residual, with the local
conditioning line $R_\theta = \kappa R_f$ and the band occupied by unrelated networks. (b) The
fraction of runs that return to the true orbit when started a relative distance $\varepsilon$ from

and the linear-mode-connectivity line \citep{entezari2022lmc} align networks by permutation for
merging; our $\calign$ generalizes the assignment step to $\Dinf \wr S_n$ using the Hungarian
algorithm \citep{kuhn1955hungarian}. Learned canonicalization \citep{kaba2023canonicalization} and
frame averaging \citep{puny2022frame} are the standard principled alternatives; W9 is frame
averaging measured in this setting, and it recovers nothing. \citet{dym2024impossibility} give the
general impossibility of continuous canonicalization, of which Proposition~\ref{prop:po5} is the
sine instance. \citet{lim2024empirical} remove symmetries by \emph{architecture}; we remove them
by canonicalization on a fixed architecture, which is the complementary intervention and the one
that leaves the function untouched.

\paragraph{Concurrent 2026 work.} Three preprints bear directly on results here and are cited by
identifier, since our engagement with them is through the close-read memos released with this code
rather than through published versions: arXiv:2602.01083 proves approximation-theoretic
universality for permutation-invariant weight-space networks. Proposition~\ref{prop:po6} is
\emph{not} a competing universality theorem --- it is a quotient factorization, and all of its
content comes from Theorem~\ref{thm:po2}, without which it says nothing about sine networks;
arXiv:2604.23720 supplies the maximal-symmetry-group vocabulary in which
Theorem~\ref{thm:po2} is stated; and arXiv:2605.08281 reports that in a meta-learned regime the
bias column is a causal readout route while weight-space geometry does not predict reader accuracy
--- a dissociation that runs counter to the mechanism of \S\ref{sec:mechanism} and was named in
our pre-registrations.

\paragraph{INR corpora and the shared-initialization question.} inr2vec \citep{deluigi2023inr2vec}
relies on shared-initialization corpora; \citet{papa2024howtotrain} intervene on fit
hyperparameters and observe downstream deltas across a large benchmark. Their shared-versus-random
comparison is one rung-pair of our ladder (W1 vs W3), and we replicate it seed-for-seed as a
pre-registered anchor before decomposing it. \citet{shamsian2024augmentations} treat the nuisance
by augmentation; W6 measures that treatment against an exact alternative and finds it recovers
$5\%$ where reframing recovers $63\%$. Approaches that avoid the nuisance by construction ---
functa \citep{dupont2022functa}, hypernetwork and meta-learned regimes --- change the
data-generating process, and are reference points rather than rungs.

\section{Limitations and open problems}\label{sec:limits}
% =====================================================================================

\textbf{W11a is a member of the equivariant family, not its strongest member.} Our
permutation-equivariant reader is two rounds of bipartite message passing with mean/max pooling,
capacity-matched to the frozen decoder. Published architectures in this family are richer: NFN's
equivariant linear layers realise many more parameter-sharing patterns between weight-space
tensors than message passing does, and graph metanetworks operate on the full computation graph
with positional encodings and per-edge features we do not use. So a result in which W11a falls
short of an exact change of frame bounds \emph{this} construction at \emph{this} capacity on
\emph{one} corpus; it is not a proof that equivariance cannot close the gap, and we do not state it
as one. What it does establish is that the obvious equivariant construction, given the same
parameter budget as the decoder it replaces, does not substitute for choosing a good orbit
representative --- and that the phase component of $G$, which none of these architectures covers
for sine networks, is a plausible reason.

\textbf{The $\Dinf$-aware reader we built consumes an invariant front-end rather than raw
parameters.} W11b is $G$-invariant, and it is the best weight-space reader here that needs neither
template nor assignment ($f = 0.526$) --- but it is invariant \emph{because} its input is already
W10's invariants, so its ceiling inherits whatever that family discards. A reader that handles
$\Dinf$ on the parameters directly is a different and probably better object. The natural
construction replaces the raw bias by its phasor $(\cos b, \sin b)$, which removes the integer
$2\pi$ translation at the level of the representation and leaves reflection and parity to be carried
by finite-dimensional linear maps; equivalently, one can read the shallow network as the atomic
measure of \eqref{eq:atoms}, whose atoms at $\pm w_i$ with coefficient proportional to
$u_i e^{\mathrm i b_i}$ quotient the phase shift automatically and turn orientation reversal into
complex conjugation. We did not build either, and it is the comparison that would make the
architecture question in \S\ref{sec:w11} decisive rather than indicative.

\textbf{Signal complexity remains confounded with image statistics.} CIFAR-10 differed from the
grayscale corpora in three ways: image statistics, output-channel count and fit budget. Two are now
ruled out --- fit budget by the travel measurement and channel count by the luminance arm, both in
\S\ref{sec:crossover} --- which leaves image statistics as the surviving candidate and no
experiment here that isolates it. A $c=1$ natural-image corpus at the grayscale fit budget, or a
synthetic corpus that interpolates image statistics at fixed everything else, is what would.

\textbf{The mechanism is evidenced by displacement, not by linearization.} \S\ref{sec:mechanism}
argues the fits stay in their initialization's neighbourhood, and measures relative parameter travel
and direction cosine. Those are not the standard diagnostics of a lazy or NTK regime: we do not
measure Jacobian or kernel drift, the error of the first-order linearization, or the accuracy of the
corresponding linearized model. The production fits are also not at a stationary point --- the
microcosm at the corpus budget ends with $\|\nabla\|\approx0.5$--$0.7$ --- so what we have shown may
be specific to high-fidelity but early-stopped fits. A convergence sweep across step budgets,
learning rates and optimizers, with checkpoints matched by PSNR as well as by iteration, would
decide whether the recoverable fraction survives convergence. It is the single experiment we would
most want added, and we have not run it.

\textbf{Capacity is matched by rule, not across a frontier.} The decoder template is frozen, but
input dimension changes across feature maps, so parameter counts do too: W10's MLP is $0.99$M
against $1.85$--$1.87$M for W11b and W5. W11a and W11b are sized to the decoder within $1.5\%$; W10
is not, and the comparisons involving it should be read with that in mind. A capacity and
training-compute frontier, rather than single matched points, is the stronger design.

\textbf{Identifiability is proved only at $L=1$, and every experiment here is $L=2$.} This remains
the program's weakest theoretical link: Theorem~\ref{thm:po2}'s guarantee and our corpora do not
meet. S4e (\S\ref{sec:s4e}) closed the empirical half of the question --- the conjecture survives
with positive evidence at $n=2$ and no counterexample --- but not the proof: the deep case still
reduces to a Bessel--CP tensor decomposition with two open lemmas. S4e's own contribution to the
limitation is that it shows the question cannot be settled empirically at production width, so the
remaining route is analytic. \textbf{Depth $\ge 3$ invariants} need a Gram per successive layer and the
parity bookkeeping compounds; whether a finite family stays separating is unknown.
\textbf{Eigenvalue pooling in W10 is deliberately lossy}, and the $0.269$-versus-$0.628$ span
against $\calign$ bounds --- but does not identify --- what it discards. \textbf{The reader is a
plain MLP with no permutation structure}, so W3 is floor-level partly by construction; this is the
intended reading of the rung, and X1 plus the canonicalization rungs are what separate ``the
reader is weak'' from ``the representation is scrambled''. \textbf{Genericity holds marginally}:
minimal parallel angles of $3\times10^{-4}$\,rad mean orbits pass close to the stratum where
identifiability genuinely fails. \textbf{All results are single-device (Apple MPS)}; a CUDA
replication of one headline table is owed. \textbf{Widths are 32--64}; the Hungarian assignment is
$O(n^3)$ per layer and an amortized or Sinkhorn path is required before this scales to width 1024.

\textbf{The empirical domain is narrow.} Three image datasets, one architecture family, one width,
one depth, one initialization distribution, $10$-way classification. Nothing here speaks to audio,
to 3-D occupancy or radiance fields, to deeper or wider SIRENs, or to targets that are properties of
the training run rather than of the represented signal --- and that last category is where the case
for weight access is strongest, because it is the one place Corollary~\ref{cor:ceiling} does not
reach.

\textbf{The uncertainty analysis resolves one level of five.} Comparisons use $n=5$ paired decoder
seeds, which is adequate for the decoder-seed level and nothing else. Images, fit realizations,
protocols and datasets are the other levels of variation, and the $1.8$M fitted INRs do not make
them independent replications; a hierarchical resampling that reports the variance decomposition
across those levels would be the right analysis and is not what we ran.

\section{Conclusion}
% =====================================================================================

Parameter symmetry accounts for a substantial part of the weight-space perception gap, and we can
now say in what sense. For periodic-activation networks the relevant group is $\Dinf\wr S_n$ ---
prior work uses two of its generators as augmentations; what is new here is the group they generate,
the affine phase component that no monomial-matrix classification contains, and the fact that at one
hidden layer the group exhausts generic functional equivalence. Exact orbit-valued reframing
recovers $63\%$, $66\%$ and $32\%$ of the shared-versus-random accuracy gap on MNIST, FashionMNIST
and CIFAR-10. That fraction is relative to the learning algorithm rather than a causal
decomposition, which is why we also measure the effect of the group directly, holding each network
and its function fixed (\S\ref{sec:orbit}): randomizing the group over a corpus with no
initialization nuisance costs $79.1$ points, against an $80.4$-point observed gap, and every
treatment recovers more of that scatter than of the observed gap. Within the group, per-neuron sign
flips account for about $63$ of those points, neuron relabelling for about $15$, and windings up to
$|j|=10$ for about one --- so the affine phase component is mathematically necessary for the
identifiability statement and empirically minor as a source of scatter, and we report it as both.

The intervention also settles, in one direction, a question the ladder could not. A reader that is
$G$-invariant --- and whose invariance we \emph{measure}, at $0.59$ points under the same
randomization --- scores $84.81\%$ on the shared-initialization corpus and $56.24\%$ on the
independently initialized one. That $28.6$-point loss cannot be group scatter, and it is not lost
signal, since function-query accuracy moves only $5.4$ points between the same two corpora. The
weight-space perception gap is therefore not reducible to parameter symmetry. What the residual
\emph{is} --- genuinely different orbits, or an incomplete invariant family that reads more from a
shared chart --- an incomplete invariant cannot decide, and we do not claim it does.

A matched control settles the other open attribution. An encoding with the same monomials,
trigonometric orders, pooling and dimension as our invariant one, differing only in whether $\Dinf$
is quotiented out, recovers $0.216$ of the CIFAR-10 gap against the invariant encoding's $0.534$
(\S\ref{sec:control}). Roughly $0.32$ of that encoding's recovery is invariance and $0.22$ is what
the nonlinearity buys on its own --- enough that the encoding's advantage over template-free
canonicalization is not a clean symmetry comparison, and we say which comparison is.
The treatments most often applied to this nuisance --- bounded group augmentation, $K$-view
training, unweighted frame averaging --- recover at most $13\%$ under our implementations and
budgets.

Two results were not anticipated. The recoverable fraction is not a constant across corpora:
alignment recovers about two thirds on the grayscale corpora and one third on natural images, and
a luminance-CIFAR arm rules out output-channel count as the explanation. And the ordering of the
two exact treatments reverses between those regimes. Within weight space, the choice of orbit
representative matters more than the reader architecture we tested: a permutation-equivariant
reader matched to the decoder's parameter count recovers $0.265$ where reframing recovers $0.628$,
and replacing an invariant encoding's fixed spectral pooling with a learned equivariant reader
raises it from $0.269$ to $0.526$ --- though that comparison is confounded by reader architecture
and capacity and we do not decompose it further.

The practical conclusion points away from weight space at this scale. Priced against function
access on a FLOPs-matched frontier (\S\ref{sec:s5}), every weight-space pipeline we tested is
dominated on both accuracy and inference cost, and amortizing the preprocessing over many
downstream tasks does not close the gap, because the weight reader's per-task cost already exceeds
function-query's entire per-task cost. The condition is $Kc \ll P$: it concerns how many probes a
task needs relative to the parameter count, not the size of the INR. Where that condition fails ---
representations expensive to evaluate, or targets not identifiable from the realised function ---
the comparison would have to be remade, and that is a different experiment from the ones here.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} Corpora, canonicalizers, the invariant encoder, all ladder cells, the
frozen pre-registrations with their hashes, the adversarial review notes and the full prediction
ledger --- hits and misses --- are released with the code. Every figure and table in this paper is
regenerated by a numbered script from committed artifacts.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:po2}}\label{app:po2}
% =====================================================================================

Throughout, $\theta = (W,b,u,\beta)$ denotes a one-hidden-layer sine network of width $n$,
\begin{equation}
f_\theta(x) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{n} u_i \sin\!\big(\langle w_i, x\rangle + b_i\big) \;+\; \beta,
\qquad x \in \R^m,
\label{eq:app-l1}
\end{equation}
with $w_i \in \R^m$, $b_i,\beta \in \R$ and $u_i \in \R^c$; for readability we take $c=1$, the
general case being coordinatewise in the output index. Recall
$\Thgen = \{\theta : w_i \neq 0,\ u_i \neq 0\ \forall i,\ w_j \neq \pm w_i\ \forall j \neq i\}$ and
the normal form $g_{d,j}(w,b,u) = ((-1)^d w,\ (-1)^d b + \pi j,\ (-1)^{d+j}u)$.

\begin{lemma}[Analytic continuation]\label{lem:app-analytic}
If $f_\theta = f_{\theta'}$ on a nonempty open $U \subseteq \R^m$, then $f_\theta = f_{\theta'}$
on all of $\R^m$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each summand of \eqref{eq:app-l1} extends to an entire function of $z \in \C^m$, so
$h = f_\theta - f_{\theta'}$ is entire, in particular real-analytic on the connected set $\R^m$. A
real-analytic function on a connected open set vanishing on a nonempty open subset vanishes
identically: the set where all derivatives vanish is open by the local power-series expansion,
closed by continuity of the derivatives, and nonempty.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fourier atoms]\label{lem:app-atoms}
$f_\theta$ is a tempered distribution and, with the convention
$\mathcal F[e^{\mathrm i\langle w,\cdot\rangle}] = (2\pi)^m \delta_w$ and the factor $(2\pi)^m$
suppressed,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{f_\theta} \;=\; \beta\,\delta_0 \;+\; \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{u_i}{2\mathrm i}
\Big( e^{\mathrm i b_i}\,\delta_{w_i} \;-\; e^{-\mathrm i b_i}\,\delta_{-w_i} \Big).
\label{eq:app-atoms}
\end{equation}
For $\theta \in \Thgen$ the $2n+1$ points $\{0\} \cup \{\pm w_i\}_{i \le n}$ are pairwise distinct
and every coefficient in \eqref{eq:app-atoms} except possibly $\beta$ is nonzero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$f_\theta$ is bounded, hence tempered. Write
$\sin(\langle w,x\rangle + b) = \frac{1}{2\mathrm i}(e^{\mathrm i b}e^{\mathrm i\langle w,x\rangle}
- e^{-\mathrm i b}e^{-\mathrm i\langle w,x\rangle})$ and apply the transform termwise, which is
legitimate because the sum is finite. Distinctness: $w_i \neq 0$ gives $w_i \neq -w_i$ and
$\pm w_i \neq 0$; $w_j \neq \pm w_i$ for $j\neq i$ gives distinctness across neurons. The
coefficient of $\delta_{\pm w_i}$ has modulus $|u_i|/2 \neq 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:po2}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:app-analytic}, $f_\theta = f_{\theta'}$ on $\R^m$, hence
$\widehat{f_\theta} = \widehat{f_{\theta'}}$ in $\mathcal S'(\R^m)$. Dirac masses at distinct
points are linearly independent in $\mathcal S'$, so two finite atomic distributions are equal iff
their supports and their coefficients agree.

\emph{(i) The constant.} Since $0$ lies in neither $\{\pm w_i\}$ nor $\{\pm w'_k\}$, the atom at
the origin is matched only by itself: $\beta = \beta'$.

\emph{(ii) Width and permutation.} The punctured supports agree:
$\{\pm w_i\}_{i\le n} = \{\pm w'_k\}_{k \le n'}$. The left side has exactly $2n$ elements and the
right exactly $2n'$, so $n = n'$. Both sets are closed under $v \mapsto -v$ and are partitioned
into $n$ unordered pairs $\{w_i,-w_i\}$, respectively $\{w'_k,-w'_k\}$; since the pairs are
disjoint, there is a unique bijection $\pi \in S_n$ with
$\{w'_{\pi(i)}, -w'_{\pi(i)}\} = \{w_i, -w_i\}$, i.e.\ $w'_{\pi(i)} = (-1)^{d_i} w_i$ for a unique
$d_i \in \{0,1\}$ (unique because $w_i \neq 0$).

\emph{(iii) Coefficients, case $d_i = 0$.} Matching the atoms at $w_i$ and at $-w_i$ gives
\[
\frac{u'_{\pi(i)}}{2\mathrm i}e^{\mathrm i b'_{\pi(i)}} = \frac{u_i}{2\mathrm i}e^{\mathrm i b_i},
\qquad
-\frac{u'_{\pi(i)}}{2\mathrm i}e^{-\mathrm i b'_{\pi(i)}} = -\frac{u_i}{2\mathrm i}e^{-\mathrm i b_i},
\]
the second being the complex conjugate of the first, so the content is
$u'_{\pi(i)} e^{\mathrm i b'_{\pi(i)}} = u_i e^{\mathrm i b_i}$ with both $u$'s real and nonzero.
Taking moduli gives $|u'_{\pi(i)}| = |u_i|$, so $u'_{\pi(i)} = \pm u_i$. If $u'_{\pi(i)} = u_i$
then $e^{\mathrm i b'_{\pi(i)}} = e^{\mathrm i b_i}$, i.e.\ $b'_{\pi(i)} = b_i + 2\pi k$; if
$u'_{\pi(i)} = -u_i$ then $e^{\mathrm i b'_{\pi(i)}} = e^{\mathrm i (b_i + \pi)}$, i.e.\
$b'_{\pi(i)} = b_i + \pi + 2\pi k$. These are exactly $g_{0,j}$ with $j = 2k$ and $j = 2k+1$
respectively, since $g_{0,j}(w,b,u) = (w,\, b + \pi j,\, (-1)^j u)$.

\emph{(iv) Coefficients, case $d_i = 1$.} Here $w'_{\pi(i)} = -w_i$, so the atom of $\theta'$ at
$w'_{\pi(i)}$ must match the atom of $\theta$ at $-w_i$:
$u'_{\pi(i)}e^{\mathrm i b'_{\pi(i)}} = -u_i e^{-\mathrm i b_i} = u_i e^{\mathrm i(\pi - b_i)}$.
Taking moduli again gives $u'_{\pi(i)} = \pm u_i$; if $u'_{\pi(i)} = u_i$ then
$b'_{\pi(i)} = -b_i + \pi + 2\pi k$, and if $u'_{\pi(i)} = -u_i$ then
$b'_{\pi(i)} = -b_i + 2\pi k$. These are exactly $g_{1,j}$ with $j$ odd and $j$ even, since
$g_{1,j}(w,b,u) = (-w,\, -b + \pi j,\, (-1)^{1+j}u)$.

Assembling the per-neuron elements with the permutation $\pi$ gives $g \in \Dinf \wr S_n$ with
$\theta' = g\theta$.

\emph{(v) Uniqueness.} It suffices that the stabilizer of any $\theta \in \Thgen$ is trivial, since
$g_1\theta = g_2\theta$ then forces $g_2^{-1}g_1 = \mathrm{id}$. Let $g = (\pi, (g_{d_i,j_i})_i)$
fix $\theta$. Reading the first-layer rows, $(-1)^{d_i} w_{\pi(i)} = w_i$ for every $i$. If
$\pi(i) \neq i$ this contradicts $w_{\pi(i)} \neq \pm w_i$, so $\pi = \mathrm{id}$; then
$(-1)^{d_i}w_i = w_i$ with $w_i \neq 0$ forces $d_i = 0$; then the bias reads
$b_i + \pi j_i = b_i$, so $j_i = 0$. Hence $g = \mathrm{id}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Where each hypothesis is used]
$w_i \neq 0$ is used for distinctness of $\pm w_i$, for uniqueness of $d_i$, and in (v);
$u_i \neq 0$ is used to know every atom is present, which is what makes the support carry the
width; and $w_j \neq \pm w_i$ is used for distinctness across neurons and in (v). On the parallel
stratum $w_j = \pm w_i$ the two atoms coincide and add as phasors,
$u_i\sin(z+b_i) + u_j\sin(z+b_j) = \operatorname{Im}\big((u_ie^{\mathrm i b_i}
+ u_je^{\mathrm i b_j})e^{\mathrm i z}\big)$, so only the complex sum is observable and the
conclusion genuinely fails --- the exclusion is not an artifact of the proof.
\end{remark}

\section{The depth-two invariant encoding, in full}\label{app:w10}
% =====================================================================================

This appendix specifies rung W10 coordinate by coordinate, proves invariance of every emitted
coordinate, and states what is \emph{not} claimed.

\paragraph{Notation.} $L=2$, widths $n$ (layer 1) and $p$ (layer 2), input dimension $m$, output
dimension $c$: $W^1 \in \R^{n\times m}$, $b^1 \in \R^n$, $W^2 \in \R^{p\times n}$, $b^2 \in \R^p$,
$W^3 \in \R^{c\times p}$. Layer 1 acts on the rows of $W^1$, the entries of $b^1$ and the
\emph{columns} of $W^2$; layer 2 acts on the rows of $W^2$, the entries of $b^2$ and the columns of
$W^3$. Write $\varepsilon_i = (-1)^{d_i + j_i}$ for the sign a layer-1 neuron's outgoing column
picks up, and $\varepsilon'_k = (-1)^{e_k + l_k}$ for the layer-2 analogue. Let
$G = (W^2)^\top W^2 \in \R^{n \times n}$.

\paragraph{Transformation rules.} Under a layer-1 element,
\[
w^1_i \mapsto (-1)^{d_i} w^1_i, \quad
\sin b^1_i \mapsto \varepsilon_i \sin b^1_i, \quad
\cos b^1_i \mapsto (-1)^{j_i}\cos b^1_i, \quad
\sin 2b^1_i \mapsto (-1)^{d_i}\sin 2b^1_i,
\]
and $\cos 2b^1_i \mapsto \cos 2b^1_i$, $\|w^1_i\|^2 \mapsto \|w^1_i\|^2$. Under a layer-2 element
$W^2$ has its rows sign-flipped and permuted, so $G \mapsto G$; layer-2 phase shifts touch $b^2$
and $W^3$ only. Under layer 1, $G_{il} \mapsto \varepsilon_i\varepsilon_l G_{il}$ and
$G \mapsto PGP^\top$ for the layer-1 permutation matrix $P$.

\paragraph{The emitted vector.} With $\lambda^{\downarrow}(M) \in \R^n$ the eigenvalues of a
symmetric $M$ in decreasing order, and $\mathrm{sort}_{\kappa}$ the gather of a vector by the
descending order of the key $\kappa$, W10 emits the concatenation
\[
\underbrace{\lambda^{\downarrow}(A),\ \lambda^{\downarrow}(B),\ \lambda^{\downarrow}(C)}_{3n},\quad
\underbrace{\mathrm{sort}_{\kappa_1}\big(\|w^1_\bullet\|^2\big),\
\mathrm{sort}_{\kappa_1}\big(\cos 2b^1_\bullet\big),\
\mathrm{sort}_{\kappa_1}\big(G_{\bullet\bullet}\big)}_{3n},
\]
\[
\underbrace{\mathrm{sort}_{\kappa_2}\big(\|w^2_\bullet\|^2\big),\
\mathrm{sort}_{\kappa_2}\big(\cos 2b^2_\bullet\big),\
\mathrm{sort}_{\kappa_2}\big(\|u_\bullet\|^2\big),\
\mathrm{sort}_{\kappa_2}\big(\sin(b^2_\bullet)\,u_\bullet\big)}_{(3+c)p},
\]
where $A_{il} = (\sin b^1_i \sin b^1_l)G_{il}$,
$B_{il} = (\cos b^1_i w^1_i \cdot \cos b^1_l w^1_l)G_{il}$,
$C_{il} = (\sin 2b^1_i w^1_i \cdot \sin 2b^1_l w^1_l)$, $u_k = W^3[:,k] \in \R^c$,
$\kappa_1 = \|w^1_\bullet\|^2$ and $\kappa_2 = \|w^2_\bullet\|^2$. The total dimension is
$6n + (3+c)p$, which is $320$ at $n=p=32$, $c=1$ and $384$ at $c=3$.

\begin{proposition}[Invariance, coordinate by coordinate]
Every coordinate above is invariant under $G_{\mathrm{grp}} = (\Dinf \wr S_n)\times(\Dinf \wr S_p)$,
provided the sort keys $\kappa_1,\kappa_2$ have no ties.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{The matrices.} $A_{il} \mapsto (\varepsilon_i\sin b^1_i)(\varepsilon_l \sin b^1_l)\cdot
\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_l G_{il} = A_{il}$, using $\varepsilon_i^2 = 1$. For $B$, each factor
$\cos b^1_i w^1_i \mapsto (-1)^{j_i}(-1)^{d_i}\cos b^1_i w^1_i = \varepsilon_i \cos b^1_i w^1_i$,
so the inner product contributes $\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_l$, which cancels $G$'s. For $C$, each
factor $\sin 2b^1_i w^1_i \mapsto (-1)^{d_i}(-1)^{d_i}\sin 2b^1_i w^1_i$ is already invariant, and
no Gram coupling is needed. All three depend on $W^2$ only through $G$, which is layer-2 invariant.
Under the layer-1 permutation each transforms as $M \mapsto PMP^\top$, an orthogonal conjugation,
which leaves the spectrum unchanged; each is symmetric by construction, so
$\lambda^{\downarrow}$ is well defined and invariant.

\emph{The layer-1 order statistics.} $\|w^1_i\|^2$, $\cos 2b^1_i$ and $G_{ii}$ are individually
$\Dinf$-invariant per neuron ($\varepsilon_i^2 = 1$ for the last), and all four vectors --- the
three payloads and the key $\kappa_1$ --- are permuted by the \emph{same} $P$. Gathering by the
descending order of $\kappa_1$ therefore yields the same vector before and after.

\emph{Layer 2.} $\|w^2_k\|^2 = \sum_i (W^2_{ki})^2$ is invariant under layer-1 column signs
($\varepsilon_i^2=1$) and layer-1 permutation (it is a sum over $i$), and under layer-2 row signs
($(-1)^{2e_k}=1$); $\cos 2b^2_k$ is invariant; $\|u_k\|^2$ picks up $(\varepsilon'_k)^2 = 1$; and
$\sin(b^2_k)u_k \mapsto \varepsilon'_k\varepsilon'_k \sin(b^2_k)u_k$. All four are permuted by the
same layer-2 permutation as the key $\kappa_2$, so the gather is invariant.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is not claimed]\label{rem:w10-scope}
Three qualifications, all of which matter for how the W10 rows are read.

\emph{Invariant, not proven complete.} We prove every coordinate is constant on orbits. We do
\emph{not} claim the family separates distinct orbits, and the empirical shortfall against
$\calign$ bounds --- without identifying --- what it discards. In particular the eigenvalue pooling
is deliberately lossy: it retains a symmetric matrix only through its spectrum.

\emph{Stability.} The spectral block is $1$-Lipschitz in the spectral norm by Weyl's inequality,
$|\lambda_i(M) - \lambda_i(M')| \le \|M - M'\|_2$, so repeated eigenvalues are harmless here ---
it is eigen\emph{vectors} that are unstable at a repeated eigenvalue, and we do not use them. The
order-statistic blocks are the fragile part: gathering three payloads by a shared key is
discontinuous where the key ties, i.e.\ where two neurons have equal $\|w^1_i\|^2$. This is the
sine-network instance of the obstruction of Proposition~\ref{prop:po5}, it has Lebesgue measure
zero, and the implementation uses a stable sort so the map is at least deterministic; the audit in
\S\ref{sec:bench} reports how close the production corpora come to it.

\emph{An encoding, not a reframing.} W10 returns features, not a parameter vector realizing the
same function, and its coordinates are nonlinear in the parameters. Its gain is therefore
separated from generic nonlinear feature engineering only by the matched control of
\S\ref{sec:control}, not by its invariance.
\end{remark}

\section{Calibration of the program's own forecasts}\label{sec:calibration}
% =====================================================================================

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig3_calibration.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Scoring our own predictions.} (a) Every registered interval, rescaled to its own
$[\text{lo80},\text{hi80}]$; blue is a hit, red a miss, arrows are off-scale. (b) Realized
coverage against nominal. The intervals are too \emph{narrow}, not too wide.}
\label{fig:calibration}
\end{figure}

Because every prediction was registered with an interval, the program can be scored as a
forecaster \citep{gneiting2007scoring}. Through the two grayscale arms, realized coverage was
$9/14 = 64\%$ against a nominal $80\%$: the intervals were too narrow. More useful than the number
is that the failures fall into exactly two modes.

\emph{Mode 1 --- hedging a registered mechanism toward priors from a different setting.} Three of
the five early misses (the anchor gap, predicted $30\,[12,45]$, observed $80.4$; the CIFAR render
fidelity, predicted $27\,[22,32]$\,dB, observed $40.1$; the alignment recovery, predicted
$0.10\,[0.02,0.30]$, observed $0.628$) err in the same direction: our own mechanism predicted an
extreme and we hedged toward the middle because a neighbouring literature, working in a different
regime, reported milder effects. Where we trusted the mechanism instead --- the $80.4$-point gap,
predicted to the decimal --- the intervals hit.

\emph{Mode 2 --- registering a contrast that could not exist.} Two misses (optimization noise,
predicted $+2.0$; marginalization-beats-augmentation, predicted $+15$) assumed a nuisance was
present and then registered a difference between two ways of handling it. Both nuisances turned
out to be null, so neither contrast \emph{could} have appeared at any magnitude.

\paragraph{The CIFAR-10 arm was registered with those lessons applied, and is the cleaner test.}
Seventeen intervals were frozen against a corpus that did not yet have a single decoded cell,
after an explicit decision to register \emph{its own} magnitudes rather than inherit the MNIST
ones --- because scoring the FashionMNIST arm against MNIST magnitudes had already produced one
spurious ``miss'' that only careful write-up caught. (That failure mode is now blocked by the
analysis script rather than by prose: an arm with no registration of its own prints
\emph{not scored}.) \input{tables/cifar_scoreline}

The two misses are the informative ones and they are the paper's finding, not a footnote to it:
$f(\mathrm{W5})$ came in at half its registered value, and the bracket claim ``W10 lands between
W4 and W5'' broke \emph{upward} because W10 overtook W5. Both are the crossover of
\S\ref{sec:crossover}, seen from the registration's point of view. Meanwhile the probability call
that ran on the encoding's algebra rather than on extrapolation --- $f(\mathrm{W10})$ would rise
with the output-channel count --- resolved correctly.

\paragraph{S4e, and a failure mode neither mode covers.} The identifiability hunt registered nine
intervals and scored \textbf{7/9}. Its two misses are one event: the best functional residual at
$n=2$ came in at $5.9\times10^{-8}$ against a registered $3\times10^{-4}\,[10^{-6}, 3\times10^{-3}]$,
and the orbit residual there at $1.2\times10^{-7}$ against $0.35\,[0.05, 0.65]$ --- because the
pilot that informed both predictions used $n=32$ restarts and never sampled the global basin, while
the confirmatory run at $n=128$ did. Those rows were flagged \emph{pilot-informed} in the ledger
before the run for exactly this reason, and they are the argument for that flag: an under-powered
pilot mispredicts the powered run, and in this case in the direction that made the result better.

S4e also exposed a third failure mode, which is about \emph{criteria} rather than point
predictions: a pre-registered threshold can be under-specified in a way only data reveals
(\S\ref{sec:s4e}, the missing absolute floor). The lesson we take is that a registered criterion
should be checked against its instrument's resolution at registration time, and that is now part of
the template.

The luminance-CIFAR arm (\S\ref{sec:crossover}) scored $9/10$ and both of its probability calls
resolved against a mechanism this paper had previously proposed, which is why that mechanism is
withdrawn in \S\ref{sec:crossover} rather than qualified.
