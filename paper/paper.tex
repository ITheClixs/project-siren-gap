\begin{center}\textbf{Abstract}\end{center}
\small
A classifier reading the raw weights of independently fitted implicit neural representations
(INRs) performs near chance, while the same classifier reading weights fitted from a
\emph{shared} initialization performs almost as well as it does on pixels. This
\emph{perception gap} is the central obstacle of weight-space learning, and it is usually
attributed, without measurement, to parameter symmetry. We test that attribution. For sine
networks we prove the exact per-neuron symmetry group is the infinite dihedral group $\Dinf$,
so the layer group is $\Dinf \wr S_n$; we prove this group is \emph{maximal} at one hidden layer
(generic identifiability), that no continuous canonicalizer for it exists, and that complete
invariants are informationally equivalent to function access. We then construct a
finite family of \emph{exact} invariants for two-layer sine networks by coupling the layers
through the second-layer Gram matrix, closing a gap that per-neuron constructions leave open.
Against this theory we run a pre-registered decomposition ladder on $1.84$M fitted INRs across
MNIST, FashionMNIST and CIFAR-10. Exact, function-preserving reframing recovers $63\%$, $66\%$
and $53\%$ of the gap; group augmentation, $K$-marginalization and frame averaging never exceed
$13\%$ and are under $6\%$ on the grayscale corpora. The gap is therefore largely --- but not
wholly --- symmetry, and the standard treatments for it do essentially nothing. Two registered predictions failed informatively:
alignment recovered six times more than forecast on grayscale, forcing a public rewrite of our own
claim, and then only half as much on natural images. The two exact methods \emph{cross over} ---
alignment dominates on grayscale, the invariant encoding on RGB --- a reversal our
pre-registration predicted from the encoding's algebra. Finally we hunt for a counterexample to
identifiability at depth two and find none: one student recovers its teacher's parameters to seven
significant figures at width two, while at production width the optimiser leaves the true orbit
even when started on it. Identifiability survives; at production width it has no empirical content.
We report the program's own forecast calibration throughout, misses included.
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================================

An implicit neural representation encodes a signal as the weights of a small network fitted to
it \citep{sitzmann2020siren,mildenhall2020nerf,tancik2020fourier}. Once a dataset of signals has
been converted into a dataset of \emph{weight vectors}, it is natural to ask whether a downstream
model can read semantics --- a class label, a shape property --- directly off those weights. A
large and fast-moving literature builds architectures for exactly this
\citep{navon2023dws,zhou2023nfn,zhou2023nft,lim2024gmn,kofinas2024graph,tran2024monomial,%
zhou2024unf,kalogeropoulos2024scalegmn,schurholt2024sane}, and a parallel line treats weights as
data for property prediction and generation
\citep{unterthiner2020predicting,schurholt2021hyperrep,schurholt2022zoos,erkoc2023hyperdiffusion,%
peebles2022gpt}.

The field runs into one stubborn empirical fact. If every INR in a corpus is fitted from the
\emph{same} initialization, a plain multilayer perceptron reading the flattened weights nearly
matches a pixel classifier. If each INR is fitted from its \emph{own} random initialization ---
the setting any realistic collection of independently trained models is in --- the same reader
collapses to a few points above chance. Methods that work in the first regime are, at least in
part, exploiting an artifact of corpus construction
\citep{deluigi2023inr2vec,papa2024howtotrain}. We call the difference the \textbf{weight-space
perception gap}.

The standard explanation is nuisance variability from parameter symmetry: two networks computing
the same function can have very different weights, so the reader must learn to quotient a group
it has no structural knowledge of. This is plausible, it motivates most of the equivariant
architectures cited above, and --- to our knowledge --- it has never been measured against the
alternative, which is that independent fits land in genuinely different loss basins whose
difference is \emph{not} a group action and therefore cannot be removed by any canonicalization,
augmentation, or equivariant layer.

This paper measures it. Our contributions:

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{The exact symmetry group of sine networks} (\S\ref{sec:group}). The per-neuron group
is the infinite dihedral group $\Dinf$, generated by a sign flip and a $\pi$-phase shift; the
layer group is $\Dinf \wr S_n$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:po1}). The phase generators are \emph{affine},
not linear, which is why classifications restricted to monomial-matrix actions
\citep{tran2024monomial} cannot see them.
\item \textbf{Identifiability, impossibility, and a limit on the field}
(\S\ref{sec:identifiability}). At one hidden layer, $\Dinf \wr S_n$ is \emph{all} of the
functional equivalence on an explicit generic stratum (Theorem~\ref{thm:po2}); no continuous
canonicalizer exists for it (Proposition~\ref{prop:po5}); and any \emph{complete} invariant
factors through the realized function (Proposition~\ref{prop:po6}), so weight access can beat
function access only computationally or by being deliberately incomplete.
\item \textbf{Exact invariants at depth two} (\S\ref{sec:deep}). Per-neuron invariants break at
$L\ge 2$ because a hidden neuron's outgoing vector is acted on by the next layer. Coupling the
layers through the second-layer Gram $G = W_2^\top W_2$ repairs this and yields a finite family
of exactly invariant features, certified numerically to $3\times10^{-7}$ relative.
\item \textbf{A pre-registered decomposition ladder} (\S\ref{sec:ladder}--\ref{sec:results}). Ten
feature maps applied to the same corpora and decoded by one frozen apparatus separate the gap
into what an exact change of frame removes and what it cannot. Registered before any cell was
computed; scored publicly, misses included.
\item \textbf{A mechanism} (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}), \textbf{a falsification hunt against
identifiability at depth two} (\S\ref{sec:s4e}) built on an exact minimiser over $G$, and
\textbf{a calibration audit} (\S\ref{sec:calibration}) of the program's own forecasts.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Headline result.} Transformations that change no network's function at all --- exact
elements of $\Dinf \wr S_n$, applied either as an alignment to a fixed reference network or as an
exact invariant encoding --- recover $63\%$, $66\%$ and $53\%$ of the perception gap on MNIST,
FashionMNIST and CIFAR-10. Because these transformations are exact, whatever they buy is provably
reframing rather than added information, so those numbers are \emph{certified lower bounds} on the
symmetry-attributable share of the gap, and the complements are upper bounds on what is not
symmetry (\S\ref{sec:bounds}). Meanwhile the interventions the literature most often reaches for
--- augmenting by group elements \citep{shamsian2024augmentations}, marginalizing over refits,
averaging over a frame \citep{puny2022frame} --- never exceed $13\%$ on any dataset and stay under
$6\%$ on both grayscale ones. The gap is largely symmetry; symmetry is largely not being removed
by the usual tools; and \emph{which} exact method wins changes with the signal
(\S\ref{sec:crossover}).

\section{Setting and the symmetry group}\label{sec:group}
% =====================================================================================

Fix input dimension $m$ (for images $m=2$), output dimension $c$, and hidden widths
$n_1,\dots,n_L$. A \emph{sine network} in canonical form is
\begin{equation}
h^0 = x, \qquad h^\ell = \sin\!\big(W^\ell h^{\ell-1} + b^\ell\big)\ \ (\ell = 1,\dots,L),
\qquad f_\theta(x) = W^{L+1} h^L + b^{L+1},
\label{eq:siren}
\end{equation}
with $\theta = (W^1,b^1,\dots,W^{L+1},b^{L+1})$. SIREN's frequency scale $\omega_0$
\citep{sitzmann2020siren} is absorbed into the stored weights at corpus creation. For a hidden
neuron $i$ write $w_i$ for its row of $W^\ell$, $b_i$ for its bias, and $u_i$ for its column of
$W^{\ell+1}$.

\begin{definition}[Per-neuron maps]\label{def:gen}
$\tau_k : (w,b,u) \mapsto (w,\, b + 2\pi k,\, u)$ for $k\in\Z$; \quad
$\rho : (w,b,u) \mapsto (w,\, b+\pi,\, -u)$; \quad
$\sigma : (w,b,u) \mapsto (-w,\, -b,\, -u)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Normal form]\label{lem:normal}
Every element of $\langle \tau_1,\rho,\sigma\rangle$ acts as
\begin{equation}
g_{d,j}\colon (w,b,u)\ \longmapsto\ \big((-1)^d w,\ (-1)^d b + \pi j,\ (-1)^{d+j} u\big),
\qquad d \in \{0,1\},\ j\in\Z,
\label{eq:normalform}
\end{equation}
with composition $g_{d_2,j_2}\circ g_{d_1,j_1} = g_{\,d_1\oplus d_2,\ j_2 + (-1)^{d_2} j_1}$.
Hence $\langle \tau_1,\rho,\sigma\rangle \cong \Z \rtimes \Z_2 = \Dinf$, with $\rho^2 = \tau_1$,
$\sigma^2 = \mathrm{id}$ and $\sigma\rho\sigma = \rho^{-1}$; $\tau_1$ is a redundant generator.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Symmetry group]\label{thm:po1}
For every $g_{d,j}$ applied at any hidden neuron, and every permutation $\pi \in S_{n_\ell}$
applied jointly to the rows of $W^\ell$, the entries of $b^\ell$ and the columns of $W^{\ell+1}$,
the network function is exactly preserved. Hence
\begin{equation}
G \;=\; \prod_{\ell=1}^{L} \Dinf \wr S_{n_\ell}
    \;=\; \prod_{\ell=1}^{L} \big(\Dinf^{\,n_\ell} \rtimes S_{n_\ell}\big)
\end{equation}
acts on parameter space with $f_{g\cdot\theta} = f_\theta$ for all $g\in G$, and the actions of
distinct layers commute.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With pre-activation $z = \langle w,h\rangle + b$,
$(-1)^{d+j} u\,\sin\!\big((-1)^d z + \pi j\big)
 = (-1)^{d+j}(-1)^{j} u \sin\!\big((-1)^d z\big)
 = (-1)^{d+j}(-1)^{j}(-1)^{d} u \sin z = u \sin z$,
so the neuron's contribution to the next pre-activation is unchanged. Permutations relabel
neurons while re-wiring outgoing columns consistently. Row and column operations on the shared
matrix $W^{\ell+1}$ touch different indices and commute.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:po1} asserts \emph{containment}. That $G$ is maximal is
Theorem~\ref{thm:po2} for $L=1$. \citet{tran2024monomial} establish maximality among
\emph{linear} (monomial-matrix) actions and leave the general sine case open; the phase maps
$g_{0,j}$, $j\neq0$, are affine and lie outside every linear action, which is exactly why their
classification cannot see the phase component.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Where genericity is needed.} Three closed, Lebesgue-null strata carry functional
equivalences \emph{beyond} the $G$-orbit: $w_i = 0$ (dead), $u_i = 0$ (invisible), and
$w_j = \pm w_i$ (\emph{parallel-frequency}). On the last, phasor addition
$u_i\sin(z+b_i) + u_j\sin(z+b_j) = \operatorname{Im}\big((u_ie^{\mathrm i b_i}
+ u_je^{\mathrm i b_j})e^{\mathrm i z}\big)$
shows only the complex sums are functionally visible: two parallel neurons carry one neuron's
worth of function, for \emph{arbitrary} bias pairs. Genericity is an assumption we measure on
every corpus rather than assume (\S\ref{sec:bench}).

\section{Identifiability, impossibility, and a ceiling on weight access}
\label{sec:identifiability}
% =====================================================================================

Let $\Thgen$ be the parameters with all $w_i \neq 0$, all $u_i \neq 0$, and no pair
$w_j = \pm w_i$.

\begin{theorem}[Generic identifiability, $L=1$]\label{thm:po2}
Let $\theta,\theta' \in \Thgen$ with widths $n, n'$ and $f_\theta = f_{\theta'}$ on some open
$U \subseteq \R^m$. Then $n = n'$ and $\theta' = g\theta$ for a \emph{unique}
$g \in \Dinf \wr S_n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$f_\theta - f_{\theta'}$ is entire and vanishes on an open set, hence everywhere. As tempered
distributions,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{f_\theta} \;=\; \beta\,\delta_0 \;+\; \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{u_i}{2\mathrm i}
\Big( e^{\mathrm i b_i}\,\delta_{w_i} \;-\; e^{-\mathrm i b_i}\,\delta_{-w_i} \Big),
\label{eq:atoms}
\end{equation}
an atomic measure whose $2n$ support points $\{\pm w_i\}$ are distinct and nonzero on $\Thgen$
and whose coefficients are all nonzero. Equality of the transforms forces equal supports and
equal coefficients: the $\delta_0$ terms give $\beta=\beta'$, the punctured supports biject
(giving $n=n'$ and the permutation), and each matched pair solves to either
$(u',b') = (u,\, b+2\pi k)$ or $(-u,\, b+\pi+2\pi k)$ --- exactly the $\rho/\tau$ family --- or
to their $\sigma$-image. Uniqueness: a $g$ fixing $\theta \in \Thgen$ must fix the distinct
unordered pairs $\{\pm w_i\}$, so its permutation is trivial, and $g_{d,j}(w,b,u)=(w,b,u)$ with
$w\neq0$ forces $d=j=0$.
\end{proof}

The parallel-frequency exclusion is not technical convenience: when $w_j = \pm w_i$ the two atoms
in \eqref{eq:atoms} share a support point and add as phasors, so separate recovery is genuinely
impossible. In the vocabulary of maximal symmetry groups, $\Dinf\wr S_n$ is a maximal symmetry
group of the $L=1$ sine architecture. The result is \emph{non-asymptotic}: the only hypothesis is
membership in $\Thgen$, an explicit and measurable condition.

Theorem~\ref{thm:po2} is the periodic-activation entry in a classical line: permutation and sign
for odd activations \citep{hechtnielsen1990algebraic}, uniqueness for minimal tanh networks
\citep{sussmann1992uniqueness}, functional equivalence more generally
\citep{kurkova1994functionally}, and reconstruction of sigmoid networks from their output via pole
structure \citep{fefferman1994reconstructing} --- of which our Fourier-atom argument is the
periodic analogue. What is new here is the \emph{phase} component: none of those groups contains a
continuous family of bias shifts, and it is the $\tau$-circle that makes canonicalization globally
obstructed (Proposition~\ref{prop:po5}) rather than obstructed only at ties.

\begin{conjecture}[Deep case]\label{conj:deep}
For $L\ge2$, off a proper analytic subset of parameter space, $f_\theta = f_{\theta'}$ implies
$\theta' = g\theta$ for some $g\in G$.
\end{conjecture}

Our own attempt on Conjecture~\ref{conj:deep} reduces it, via Jacobi--Anger, to the uniqueness of a
CP decomposition in a Bessel basis, and stalls on two lemmas: generic full column rank of the
Bessel--Vandermonde matrices $[J_k(x_j)]$, and control of the infinite tail when the decomposition
is identified from finitely many observed coefficients. The rank lemma is numerically supported and
looks completable; the same numerics show the system is severely ill-conditioned by $n=5$
($\min|\det|$ falling from $4.4\times10^{-3}$ to $4.4\times10^{-13}$ between $n=2$ and $n=5$), which
is what motivated attacking the conjecture empirically instead (\S\ref{sec:s4e}). The attempt is
released as a proof memo rather than buried.

\begin{proposition}[No continuous canonicalization]\label{prop:po5}
There is no continuous $\kappa : \Thgen \to \Thgen$ with $\kappa(\theta) \in \Orb(\theta)$ and
$\kappa(g\theta) = \kappa(\theta)$ for all $g \in \langle\sigma,\rho,\tau\rangle$. One hidden
neuron and $m=c=1$ suffice.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take $\theta_t = (1,t,1)$, $t\in[0,2\pi]$. Write $\kappa(\theta_t) = g_{d_t,j_t}\theta_t$.
Continuity of the first coordinate forces $d_t \equiv d$; of the third, the parity of $j_t$ is
constant; then $j_t$ is a continuous integer-valued function, hence constant. But
$\theta_{2\pi} = \tau_1\theta_0$, so invariance requires equal bias coordinates while the normal
form \eqref{eq:normalform} gives a difference of $(-1)^d 2\pi \neq 0$.
\end{proof}

This is the sine-specific counterpart of the general topological obstruction of
\citet{dym2024impossibility}. It is stronger than the permutation case in an operationally
relevant way: for permutations alone the obstruction is confined to sorting-key ties, whereas the
$\tau$-circle makes it global. Any exact canonicalizer must therefore be discontinuous on a set
meeting every loop that winds the bias circle; we measure the empirical mass near those sets.

\begin{proposition}[Completeness is function access]\label{prop:po6}
Assume the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:po2}. If $I : \Thgen \to X$ is $G$-invariant and
\emph{complete} ($I(\theta)=I(\theta') \Rightarrow \theta' \in G\theta$), then $I = \bar I \circ R$
factors through the realization map $R:\theta \mapsto f_\theta$, with $\bar I$ injective on
$R(\Thgen)$. Conversely any injective encoding of $R(\Thgen)$ pulls back to a complete invariant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:ceiling}
A complete-invariant weight-space perceiver receives \emph{exactly} the information of a
function-space perceiver. Any advantage of weight access over function access must therefore be
computational (amortization: canonicalize once, decode many times), or must come from
deliberately \emph{incomplete} invariants, or from non-pointwise processing.
\end{corollary}

Corollary~\ref{cor:ceiling} is a ceiling on the enterprise, not a refutation of it: it says the
justification for weight-space learning has to be stated in the currency of compute or of
deliberate lossiness, and it is what makes a FLOPs-matched comparison against function-query
methods such as \citet{horwitz2025probegen} the honest adjudication.

\section{Exact invariants at depth two}\label{sec:deep}
% =====================================================================================

Requiring monomial invariance $g(b)P_\alpha(w)Q_\beta(u)$ under \eqref{eq:normalform} --- $g$
must be $2\pi$-periodic, $g(b+\pi) = (-1)^\beta g(b)$, $g(-b)=(-1)^{\alpha+\beta}g(b)$ --- yields
the minimal-frequency solutions $(\alpha,\beta) = (0,0) : 1, \cos 2b$; $(1,0): \sin 2b$;
$(0,1): \sin b$; $(1,1): \cos b$, and hence a separating per-neuron feature map on
$\{w\neq0,u\neq0\}$:
\begin{equation}
\Phi(w,b,u) \;=\; \Big( w\otimes w,\ \cos 2b,\ (\sin 2b)\,w,\ (\sin b)\,u,\ (\cos b)\,(w\otimes u)\Big).
\label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
\emph{Note} that the features one would guess --- $\cos(2b)(w\otimes u)$, $\sin(2b)(w\otimes u)$
--- are \emph{not} invariant: the first has the wrong bias frequency for $\rho$, the second the
wrong parity for $\sigma$. Both are refuted numerically in our test suite.

Equation~\eqref{eq:phi} is scoped to $L=1$. At depth two a hidden neuron's outgoing vector $u_i$
is a \emph{column of a matrix the next layer's group also acts on}, so the $(0,1)$ and $(1,1)$
classes stop being invariant and per-neuron constructions fail. Our repair couples the layers.

\begin{proposition}[Exact $L=2$ invariant encoding]\label{prop:deep}
Let $G_2 = W_2^\top W_2$. Then $G_2$ is invariant under the \emph{entire} layer-2 group (row sign
flips cancel inside each inner product, row permutations reindex the sum, phase shifts touch
$b^2$ and $W^3$ only), and under layer~1 it picks up $\varepsilon_i \varepsilon_l$ with
$\varepsilon_i = (-1)^{d_i + j_i}$. Since $\sin b_i$ carries exactly $\varepsilon_i$ and
$\cos b_i\, w_i$ carries $\varepsilon_i$ after contraction, the matrices
\begin{equation}
A = (\sin b_i \sin b_l)\,G_2, \qquad
B = (\cos b_i w_i \!\cdot\! \cos b_l w_l)\,G_2, \qquad
C = (\sin 2b_i w_i \!\cdot\! \sin 2b_l w_l)
\end{equation}
are sign-cancelling and transform as $M \mapsto P M P^\top$ under the layer-1 permutation. Their
sorted eigenvalue spectra are therefore invariant under the full product group.
\end{proposition}

Per-neuron even scalars are emitted as order statistics under a shared invariant sort key, and
layer~2 uses \eqref{eq:phi} contracted over the layer-1 index. The encoding has $D = 320$ for our
grayscale corpora and $D = 384$ for CIFAR-10 (three output channels). We verify invariance by
applying random group elements with windings $|j|\le3$ and non-trivial permutations: the relative
residual move is $\approx 3\times10^{-7}$, i.e.\ fp32 round-off rather than tolerance slack, with
controls for non-constancy and for wrong depth. Whether a finite invariant family stays separating
at $L \ge 3$ is open (\S\ref{sec:limits}).

\section{INR-Bench: corpora, protocols, gates}\label{sec:bench}
% =====================================================================================

A decomposition needs corpora that differ in \emph{exactly} the nuisance under study. We fit one
SIREN per image of MNIST \citep{lecun1998mnist}, FashionMNIST \citep{xiao2017fashion} and CIFAR-10
\citep{krizhevsky2009cifar} ($L=2$, width $32$, $\omega_0$ absorbed) under four protocols that intervene on
the arguments of the fit map $F : (y, \theta_0, \xi) \mapsto \theta_T$, where $y$ is the target
signal, $\theta_0$ the initialization and $\xi$ the optimization noise:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
protocol & $\theta_0$ & $\xi$ & isolates \\
\midrule
\texttt{P-shared-det}   & fixed & fixed & the ceiling: no nuisance at all \\
\texttt{P-shared-stoch} & fixed & drawn & optimization noise alone \\
\texttt{P-random}       & drawn & drawn & the realistic, independently-fitted setting \\
\texttt{P-random-K}     & $K{=}8$ draws per image & drawn & the nuisance, sampled, for marginalization \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Quality gates.} A corpus is admitted only if a small CNN trained on \emph{renders} of
the fitted INRs matches one trained on real pixels, so that no rung can be explained by lost
signal. All gates pass: render-vs-pixel accuracy gaps of $-0.09$ to $+0.84$ points across nine
dataset$\times$protocol cells, with median render PSNR $39.2$\,dB (MNIST), $43.4$\,dB
(FashionMNIST) and $40.1$\,dB (CIFAR-10) on the shared-deterministic corpora. We audit the strata of
\S\ref{sec:group}: production fits satisfy genericity, but \emph{marginally} --- with 32
first-layer directions in a 2-D input space, minimal parallel angles are
$3\times10^{-4}$--$2\times10^{-3}$\,rad, so orbits pass near the parallel stratum. That is a
conditioning statement, not an identifiability failure, and we report it as such.

\paragraph{Scale.} Three datasets $\times$ four protocols $=$ approximately $1.8$M fitted INRs,
generated on a single Apple M4 laptop; the CIFAR-10 corpus alone is $540{,}000$ fits and
$15.4$ hours of wall-clock. Every corpus, gate and audit is generated by a numbered, idempotent
script.

\section{The decomposition ladder}\label{sec:ladder}
% =====================================================================================

The ladder applies different \emph{feature maps} to the same underlying corpora and decodes each
with one frozen apparatus: a matched MLP $[D \to 1024 \to 512 \to 256 \to 10]$, GELU, dropout
$0.1$, AdamW $10^{-3}$, cosine schedule, early stopping on a held-out INR validation split. Only
the feature map changes.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llp{7.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
rung & corpus & feature map \\
\midrule
P0 & --- & real pixels (the reference task) \\
P1 & shared-det & oracle render of the fitted INR (information-preservation control) \\
W1 & shared-det & raw flattened weights, \emph{no} nuisance \\
W2 & shared-stoch & raw weights, optimization noise only \\
W3 & random & raw weights, the realistic setting \\
W4 & random & $\csort$: exact canonicalization by neuron sort, template-free \\
W5 & random & $\calign$: exact canonicalization by sort $+$ sign/phase alignment to $\theta_0$ \\
W6 & random & raw weights $+$ bounded group augmentation at train time \\
W7 & random-K & $K$-marginalization: all 8 fitted views as training rows \\
W8 & random & canonicalize, \emph{then} augment inside the canonical frame \\
W9 & random & frame averaging over $R=64$ group elements \\
W10 & random & the exact $L{=}2$ invariants of Proposition~\ref{prop:deep} \\
X1 & both & train on W1 features, evaluate on W3 features, and the reverse \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The quantity of interest is the \textbf{recovery fraction}
\begin{equation}
f(\mathrm{W}k) \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{W}k - \mathrm{W3}}{\mathrm{W1} - \mathrm{W3}} \in [0,1],
\label{eq:f}
\end{equation}
the share of the perception gap that feature map $k$ buys back. Absolute accuracies inherit the
task ceiling; $f$ does not, which makes it the object that can be compared across datasets.

\paragraph{Pre-registration.} Every rung definition, hypothesis, point prediction with an $80\%$
interval, seed count, exclusion rule and falsification condition was frozen in a committed,
hash-stamped document before any cell was computed, following the pre-registration practice
argued for in \citep{munafo2017manifesto,nosek2018preregistration}. Seed counts were sized from a
\emph{measured} paired-difference SD ($0.210$ points for rungs whose decoder input is a fixed
matrix, $0.721$ for rungs that redraw it each step), giving $n=5$ and $n=15$ respectively;
at $n=5$ the second class would have had TOST power $0.20$. Rung comparisons use paired-by-seed
$t$ and Wilcoxon tests with Holm correction \citep{holm1979simple}, equivalence via TOST
\citep{schuirmann1987tost}, and a label-shuffle control at W1, W3 and W5
\citep{zhang2017rethinking}. Registered predictions are scored publicly, misses included
(\S\ref{sec:calibration}).

% =====================================================================================

\begin{figure}[t]

\input{tables/ladder_table}

\paragraph{The gap is real and enormous.} On MNIST, W1 $=94.36$ against W3 $=13.92$: a
$\mathbf{80.4}$-point gap between two corpora of networks fitted to \emph{the same images}, with
the same architecture, differing only in whether the initialization was shared. FashionMNIST gives
$70.3$ points and CIFAR-10 $31.6$. X1 shows the two representations are not even rescalings of one
another: on all three datasets a decoder trained on W1 features scores at chance on W3 features
and vice versa ($10.7 / 13.2$ on MNIST; $10.4 / 12.0$ on CIFAR-10).

\paragraph{It is not decoder inadequacy.} P1 $\approx$ P0 everywhere --- on MNIST TOST declares
equivalence at a $1.0$-point margin ($p = 2.5\times10^{-5}$), and on CIFAR-10 the render is
\emph{marginally better} than real pixels ($56.23$ vs $55.81$), which at $40$\,dB is the mild
denoising a near-exact render provides. Fitting destroys no class information. W1 then sits
$3.2$ points below P1 on MNIST and $11.9$ on CIFAR-10, which bounds decoder-vs-representation loss
under \emph{zero} nuisance. A decoder that were simply too weak could not be rescued by a
function-preserving change of frame --- and reframing rescues it by $50$ points on MNIST.
(A striking aside: on CIFAR-10 a \emph{linear} probe reads the shared-init weights better than it
reads the pixels, $40.1$ vs $33.0$. The fit map is doing useful feature extraction; the problem is
purely that independent fits write the result in incompatible coordinates.)

\paragraph{Optimization noise is null on all three datasets.} W1 $-$ W2 $= -0.68$ (MNIST),
$-0.70$ (FashionMNIST), $-0.90$ (CIFAR-10): stochastic fitting from a shared initialization is, if
anything, marginally \emph{better}. We had registered $+2.0$ points $[0,6]$ on MNIST and were
wrong; having learned that, we registered $-0.7$ $[-2.5,+1.0]$ for CIFAR-10 and hit. The gap is
attributable to the initialization, not to the trajectory.

\paragraph{Most of the gap is symmetry --- but the share is not a constant.}
$f(\mathrm{W5}) = 0.628$ (MNIST) and $0.664$ (FashionMNIST). We had registered $0.10$, with an
$80\%$ interval reaching only to $0.30$, and a pre-committed rule stating that $f > 0.5$ falsifies
the ``canonicalization is not enough'' claim and requires it to be \emph{rewritten, not softened}.
It fired, and the restatement was: at least $63\%$ of the gap is symmetry-orbit scatter, removable
by an exact and function-preserving reframing.

We then registered that restatement as a prediction for CIFAR-10 --- $f(\mathrm{W5}) = 0.62$,
$80\%$ $[0.42, 0.78]$, with an explicit falsifier at $f < 0.30$ --- and it missed in the other
direction: $f(\mathrm{W5}) = \mathbf{0.325}$, just clear of the falsification line. The two-thirds
figure is a property of the grayscale corpora, not a law. What survives on all three datasets is
the weaker and still substantial claim that \emph{an exact change of frame recovers between a
third and two thirds of the gap, while no inexact treatment exceeds an eighth of it}.

\paragraph{The 0.11-to-0.63 span is canonicalizer quality, not information.} Template-free
sorting recovers $f = 0.177 / 0.170 / 0.108$; aligning to a fixed reference network recovers
$0.628 / 0.664 / 0.325$. Both are exact elements of $G$; the difference is entirely which orbit
representative is chosen. The practical message for architecture designers is that the ceiling on
frame choice is high and current template-free canonicalization is nowhere near it.

\paragraph{The alignment template does not matter} (Fig.~\ref{fig:template}). A natural objection
is that $\theta_0$ exists only because we built the corpus with a known shared initialization.
Five templates say otherwise: an \emph{unrelated} random initialization does marginally better
($0.640$) than the corpus's own ($0.628$), a second unrelated draw gives $0.601$, and even fitted
INRs used as templates clear $0.5$. Alignment buys a \emph{consistent frame}, and any fixed
reference network supplies one. A secondary observation, unregistered: fitted INRs make
\emph{worse} templates than untrained ones ($0.51$--$0.55$ vs $0.60$--$0.64$), plausibly because
a fitted network's neurons are specialized to its own image. The practical design note is to
align to a random fixed network, never to a corpus member.

\paragraph{The tools the field reaches for do not work.} Across MNIST / FashionMNIST / CIFAR-10:
bounded group augmentation recovers $f = 0.054 / 0.032 / 0.128$; $K$-marginalization
$0.048 / 0.034 / 0.101$; frame averaging $0.003 / -0.008 / 0.001$. The largest of these
anywhere is $0.128$, against $0.534$ for the best exact treatment on the same corpus. We had
registered that marginalization would beat augmentation by $15$ points on MNIST; the observed
difference was $-0.52$ ($p = .21$) because \emph{neither} works, and the null replicates on both
later datasets ($+0.16$, $-0.84$; registered $0.0\,[-2,+2]$ for CIFAR-10 and hit). Of W7's small
gain, $+3.2$ points on MNIST and $+3.1$ on CIFAR-10 is explained by its $8\times$ training rows
alone (the W7-1/8 control). And W8 --- canonicalize, then augment --- collapses to chance on all
three ($10.27 / 10.20 / 10.65$): augmenting inside a canonical frame destroys the frame the
decoder just gained. Frame averaging's failure is the expected one: parity-odd coordinates cancel
toward zero, so it is a lossy invariantization where W10 is an exact one.

The one place augmentation looks better is CIFAR-10, where its $0.128$ edges past $\csort$'s
$0.108$ and past its own registered ceiling of $0.12$. We record it as a miss (H-C1-9) rather
than round it away, but it does not change the ranking: an inexact treatment recovering an eighth
of the gap where an exact one recovers a half is still the wrong tool.

\section{What the ladder does and does not prove}\label{sec:bounds}
% =====================================================================================

It is worth being exact about the logical status of the recovery fractions, because the obvious
reading of them is too strong.

\begin{proposition}[Certified lower bound]\label{prop:bound}
Let $c : \Theta \to \Theta$ satisfy $c(\theta) \in G\theta$ for every $\theta$ --- an
\emph{exact} reframing, not necessarily a canonical one --- and let a fixed decoder score
$\mathrm{W3}$ on raw parameters and $\mathrm{W}k$ on $c(\theta)$. Then $f(\mathrm{W}k)$ is a lower
bound on the share of the gap attributable to the $G$-action.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$c$ moves each parameter vector inside its own $G$-orbit, so $f_{c(\theta)} = f_\theta$ for all
$\theta$: no function, and hence no information about the underlying signal, is created. Any
accuracy the decoder gains is therefore attributable to the change of orbit representative alone.
The bound is not tight because $c$ need not be canonical --- two parameter vectors in the same
orbit may be sent to different representatives --- so a better exact reframing can only increase
$f$.
\end{proof}

Two consequences, both of which we obey throughout.

\emph{The recovered share is certified; the residual is not.} $f(\mathrm{W5}) = 0.628$ on MNIST
means \emph{at least} $63\%$ of that gap is symmetry. It does \emph{not} follow that the other
$37\%$ is basin multiplicity: a better choice of representative could claim part of it. What the
residual bounds is the sum of (imperfect frame choice) $+$ (genuine non-group difference), and the
program's own history is the cautionary example --- we improved the frame once, from $\csort$ to
$\calign$, and the ``residual'' fell from $82\%$ to $37\%$.

\emph{The invariant rung needs one extra caveat.} Proposition~\ref{prop:bound} is stated for
reframings $c(\theta) \in G\theta$, which is what W4 and W5 are: a relabelling of the same
coordinates, computing no new features. W10 is a $G$-invariant \emph{encoding}, so it likewise
depends only on the orbit and creates no information about the signal --- but it is also a
\emph{nonlinear} feature map (it contains $w \otimes w$, $(\sin b)u$, eigenvalue spectra), and a
plain MLP on raw parameters cannot cheaply compute those. Part of W10's gain may therefore be
feature engineering rather than group removal, and we do not claim its $0.534$ on CIFAR-10 as a
certificate of the same strength as $\calign$'s $0.325$. Disentangling the two would need a
non-invariant control with the same nonlinearities, which we have not run.

Proposition~\ref{prop:po5} says no continuous exact canonicalizer exists at all, so closing the
distance between the observed $f$ and the true symmetry share is a question about
\emph{discontinuous} constructions and their sample efficiency, not about whether the information
is there.

\section{The crossover: alignment and invariance trade places}\label{sec:crossover}
% =====================================================================================

The two exact methods move in \emph{opposite} directions as the signal gets harder:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
& MNIST & FashionMNIST & CIFAR-10 \\
\midrule
$f(\mathrm{W5})$, alignment to a fixed reference & 0.628 & 0.664 & \textbf{0.325} \\
$f(\mathrm{W10})$, exact $L{=}2$ invariants      & 0.269 & 0.428 & \textbf{0.534} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Alignment halves; the invariant encoding nearly doubles, and on CIFAR-10 it \emph{overtakes}
alignment. This is not a post-hoc observation about which method happened to win. The
pre-registration for the CIFAR arm carried an explicit probability call --- $P = 0.60$ that
$f(\mathrm{W10})_{\text{CIFAR}} > f(\mathrm{W10})_{\text{MNIST}} = 0.269$ --- justified by the
encoding's algebra rather than by extrapolating the two observed values: with $c = 3$ output
channels each neuron's outgoing vector $u_i \in \R^3$ carries strictly more $\Dinf$-visible
structure than the $c = 1$ case, so the invariants built from it have more to see (and the
encoding grows from $D = 320$ to $D = 384$). That call resolved correctly. The companion call ---
that the grayscale ordering $f(\mathrm{W5}) > f(\mathrm{W10})$ would persist, $P = 0.65$ ---
resolved incorrectly, and the crossover is the reason.

\paragraph{The registered mechanism is real but small (ablation).} P-C1-B attributed W10's rise to
the three output channels. We can test that \emph{within} the CIFAR corpus, without new fitting,
by changing only what the encoder is allowed to read (exploratory, not registered):

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llrr@{}}
\toprule
arm & what the encoder reads & $D$ & $f(\mathrm{W10})$ \\
\midrule
full        & all three output channels                & 384 & 0.534 \\
truncated   & output channel 0 only                    & 320 & 0.457 \\
averaged    & the three channels' mean                 & 320 & 0.425 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Truncating to one channel --- which restores exactly the grayscale encoding dimension --- costs
only $0.077$. Against the $0.265$ rise from MNIST's $0.269$ to CIFAR's $0.534$, the channel count
therefore explains about $29\%$; the other $71\%$ survives at $D = 320$ and is a property of the
corpus, not of $c$. Our registered mechanism was right in direction and wrong in magnitude, and we
say so rather than let the correct sign carry the whole explanation. (The \emph{averaged} arm is
the useful control: it has the same dimension as \emph{truncated} and strictly more of the
network's information, yet does \emph{worse}, so the effect is not ``more feature dimensions'' and
not ``more information'' --- channel-averaging cancels the per-channel sign structure that
$(\sin b_i)u_i$ exists to carry.)

\paragraph{It is not that CIFAR's fits ran further.} The obvious confound is fit length: the
CIFAR corpora were frozen at $1000$ steps against $300$ for the grayscale ones, and a fit that
travels further from $\theta_0$ should be harder to align back to it. We measured the travel
directly on the shared-initialization corpora, with no new fitting. It is
\emph{indistinguishable} across datasets: median relative travel
$\|\theta_T - \theta_0\| / \|\theta_0\|$ of $0.186$ (MNIST), $0.191$ (FashionMNIST) and $0.187$
(CIFAR-10), with median layer-1 direction cosine to the initialization of $0.998$, $0.999$ and
$0.999$ respectively --- CIFAR-10 is if anything the \emph{least} moved. The extra $700$ steps did
not translate into extra displacement, so the drop in $f(\mathrm{W5})$ is not a fit-length
artifact and the laziness that makes alignment work at all is equally present everywhere.

\paragraph{What we think is happening, stated as a conjecture.} $\calign$ matches neurons by
correlating their layer-1 \emph{activations} on probe coordinates against a template. That
statistic is blind to the outgoing structure --- exactly the part that grows with $c$, and exactly
what W10's Gram coupling reads. So as more of a neuron's identity moves into its outgoing vector,
activation matching identifies it less reliably while the invariants identify it better. This
predicts that an alignment cost augmented with an outgoing-weight term should recover part of the
CIFAR shortfall; that experiment is not run here and the claim is registered as a conjecture, not
a finding.

\paragraph{The practical reading.} On low-complexity grayscale corpora, choosing a good orbit
representative is the dominant lever and an exact invariant front-end is a poor second. On natural
RGB images the ranking reverses. A weight-space method that fixes one strategy will therefore look
much better on some benchmarks than others for reasons that have nothing to do with its reader
architecture.

\section{Mechanism: the fit map does not leave its initialization}\label{sec:mechanism}
% =====================================================================================

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig2_mechanism.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Why the initialization dominates.} (a) The profiled loss of the one-neuron
microcosm, certified against quadrature to $5.6\times10^{-16}$; its zero set is exactly the
$\Dinf$ orbit, and it carries 19 spurious minima besides. (b) The fraction of initializations
reaching the global orbit is non-monotone in the initialization range, peaking at range
$\approx\omega$, and this replicates across Nelder--Mead, Adam and plain gradient descent.
(c) At the setting our corpora are actually fitted with, the median parameter travel is
$\approx0.24$ \emph{independently of the initialization range}: the fit stays in its
initialization's neighbourhood.}
\label{fig:mechanism}
\end{figure}

Why should the initialization matter so much more than the trajectory? We answer in a microcosm
where everything is computable: one neuron, $g(t) = u\sin(wt+b)+c$ fitted to
$y(t) = A\sin(\omega t + \varphi) + c_0$ on $[-1,1]$. Profiling out the linear parameters
$(u,c)$ gives a closed form $\mathcal{L}^*(w,b)$ whose Gram entries are elementary; we certify it
against numerical quadrature to $5.6\times10^{-16}$. Its zero set is \emph{exactly} the $\Dinf$
orbit of the realizing parameters, and it has $19$ additional spurious local minima.

Two findings follow. First, \textbf{basin capture is non-monotone} in the initialization range,
with a sweet spot at range $\approx \omega$, and this replicates across optimizer classes
(global-capture fractions $0.00/0.20/0.56/0.31$ for converged Adam, $0.00/0.18/0.58/0.33$ for
plain gradient descent, $0.00/0.26/0.62/0.32$ for Nelder--Mead on the profiled surface at ranges
$2/5/10/20$). Second, and decisively, \textbf{at the setting our corpora are actually fitted with}
(Adam, $10^{-3}$, $300$ steps) \emph{every} initialization ends unconverged, with endpoint
$\|\nabla\| \approx 0.5$--$0.7$ and median $|\Delta w| \approx 0.24$ \emph{independently of the
initialization range}. The fit never leaves its initialization's neighbourhood --- the lazy regime
\citep{jacot2018ntk,chizat2019lazy} --- so the final weights are essentially the initialization
plus a signal-dependent perturbation. A shared initialization means a shared frame; independent
initializations mean independently scattered ones. That is the mechanism, and it predicts exactly
the null W1$-$W2 rung we measured.

\begin{remark}[A sub-claim of our own that was an artifact]
Our first census reported ``$100\%$ degenerate-ridge capture at range $2$''. Re-running under
gradient methods rather than Nelder--Mead on the profiled surface shows ridge capture is $0.00$:
those runs are still \emph{descending}, not sitting in a $w\approx0$ basin. An
\texttt{unconverged} class had to be added; without it we would have made a false claim about the
landscape. The headline non-monotonicity survives; the sub-claim does not.
\end{remark}

\section{Does identifiability have empirical content at depth two?}\label{sec:s4e}
% =====================================================================================

Everything above rests on a theorem proved at $L=1$ while every experiment is $L=2$. The
pre-registered attack on that gap (S4e) asks the question empirically: if two two-layer sine
networks realise nearly the same function, are their parameters nearly related by an element of
$G$?

\paragraph{The instrument, and the control that makes it non-vacuous.} A large residual after
$\calign$ proves nothing, because $\calign$ is a heuristic choice of representative. We therefore
minimise over the group directly. Given the other layers fixed, the optimum for one layer is
\emph{exact}: the per-neuron $\Dinf$ cost
$\|(-1)^dw_i - w^*_t\|^2 + ((-1)^db_i + \pi j - b^*_t)^2 + \|(-1)^{d+j}u_i - u^*_t\|^2$
depends on $j$ only through its parity, so four $(d,\text{parity})$ cases give the exact minimum
over the whole infinite group, and the permutation is then a Hungarian assignment on those
per-pair minima. Layers are swept by coordinate descent from several restarts.

The control is planted recovery: take $\theta$, apply a known $g$, and demand the search return
machine zero. It does, at every width, to $\approx4\times10^{-8}$ relative. This control earned its
place --- the first confirmatory launch \emph{failed} it (coordinate descent stalls on about
$10\%$ of width-2 pairs), which tripped the registration's own void condition, and that run was
discarded rather than reported. Restarts fixed it.

\input{tables/s4e_table}

\paragraph{Local recovery is well conditioned; the basin is not.} Perturbing a network by a
relative $\varepsilon$ and measuring both residuals gives $\kappa = R_\theta/R_f$, the local
condition number of the inverse map. It is \emph{small} and \emph{decreases} with width
($0.042 \to 0.0055$ from $n=2$ to $n=32$), so the forward map $\theta\mapsto f$ is strongly
expansive and local recovery is easy --- the opposite of what the Bessel--Vandermonde
ill-conditioning in our own proof memo might suggest, and a distinction worth keeping: that
determinant governs the \emph{global} recovery system, not the local Jacobian. What collapses with
width instead is the \emph{basin}. Started a distance $\varepsilon=10^{-5}$ from the true orbit,
$78$--$91\%$ of runs return to it at $n\le8$, $44\%$ at $n=16$, and \textbf{none} at $n=32$, where
the optimiser instead walks from $R_\theta = 10^{-5}$ out to $1.3\times10^{-1}$ while the function
barely improves. That is not a training-budget artifact: a control at five times the step count
leaves it unchanged ($0\%$ at $n=32$, identical $R_f$ floor; open markers in
Fig.~\ref{fig:s4e}b).

\paragraph{Independent restarts witness the orbit only at the smallest width.} At $n=2$, one of
$128$ students converged to $R_f = 5.9\times10^{-8}$ and, after optimal alignment, agreed with its
teacher to $R_\theta = 1.2\times10^{-7}$ --- float32 relative epsilon, a maximum per-coordinate
relative disagreement of $2.8\times10^{-6}$, and $2.6\times10^{-7}$ of the unrelated-network scale
at that width. That is recovery to six or seven significant figures, and it is direct positive
evidence for Conjecture~\ref{conj:deep}. At every larger width no restart came close: the best
functional residual jumps to $10^{-2}$ and the orbit residual sits in the band occupied by
\emph{entirely unrelated} networks (Fig.~\ref{fig:s4e}a). Combined with the warm-start arm, which
returns $78$--$91\%$ of the time at those same widths when started \emph{inside} the basin, the
reading is specific: the basin does not become shallower with width, its \emph{volume} collapses
relative to what random initialisation samples.

\paragraph{The registered criterion fired, and it was wrong to.} Read literally, that $n=2$ student
satisfies the pre-registered falsification criterion: $R_f < 10^{-5}$ and
$R_\theta = 1.2\times10^{-7} > 20\kappa R_f = 5.0\times10^{-8}$. It is a false positive, and the
criterion is at fault in two independent ways. It is \emph{ratio-only with no absolute floor}, so
as $R_f$ approaches machine epsilon the threshold falls below the smallest residual a float32
aligner can represent and \emph{any} exact recovery fires it. And $\kappa$ is the \emph{wrong null}
for an optimiser residual: it was measured on random perturbation directions, whereas a minimiser's
residual lies in the flattest directions of the loss --- exactly where $R_f$ is least sensitive to
$R_\theta$ --- so $R_\theta/R_f > \kappa$ is expected for any converged minimiser ($2.10$ observed
against $\kappa = 0.042$). We report the firing, the defect, and a post-hoc amendment adding an
absolute floor $R_\theta > 10^{-3}$, marked as post-hoc with the frozen text unaltered, and score
the registered probability call against the criterion \emph{as written}.

\paragraph{The production arm.} At production width, two independent fits \emph{of the same image}
sit at $R_\theta = 0.279$ after optimal alignment. Two fits of \emph{different} images sit at
$0.280$. The difference is $-0.001$. Modulo the entire symmetry group, a same-image pair is no
closer than an unrelated pair --- which is the S1 ladder's W1-versus-W3 gap seen from parameter
space rather than from a decoder.

\paragraph{What this licenses.} Conjecture~\ref{conj:deep} survives, with one width's worth of
direct positive evidence and no counterexample. But identifiability at $L=2$ has \emph{no empirical
content at production width}: the configuration that would witness it is not reachable, and the
optimiser will leave the true orbit even when placed on it. The honest statement for Ch.~1 is that
the $L=1$ theorem is the guarantee we have, and that the $L=2$ corpora this paper decodes are
governed by basin structure rather than by identifiability.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig5_s4e.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{S4e: identifiability versus reachability at $L=2$.} (a) Independent students
fitted to a teacher's exact outputs: orbit residual against functional residual, with the local
conditioning line $R_\theta = \kappa R_f$ and the band occupied by unrelated networks. (b) The
fraction of runs that return to the true orbit when started a relative distance $\varepsilon$ from
it --- the basin collapses with width. (c) The local condition number $\kappa$ against width.}
\label{fig:s4e}
\end{figure}

\section{Calibration of the program's own forecasts}\label{sec:calibration}
% =====================================================================================

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig3_calibration.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Scoring our own predictions.} (a) Every registered interval, rescaled to its own
$[\text{lo80},\text{hi80}]$; blue is a hit, red a miss, arrows are off-scale. (b) Realized
coverage against nominal. The intervals are too \emph{narrow}, not too wide.}
\label{fig:calibration}
\end{figure}

Because every prediction was registered with an interval, the program can be scored as a
forecaster \citep{gneiting2007scoring}. Through the two grayscale arms, realized coverage was
$9/14 = 64\%$ against a nominal $80\%$: the intervals were too narrow. More useful than the number
is that the failures fall into exactly two modes.

\emph{Mode 1 --- hedging a registered mechanism toward priors from a different setting.} Three of
the five early misses (the anchor gap, predicted $30\,[12,45]$, observed $80.4$; the CIFAR render
fidelity, predicted $27\,[22,32]$\,dB, observed $40.1$; the alignment recovery, predicted
$0.10\,[0.02,0.30]$, observed $0.628$) err in the same direction: our own mechanism predicted an
extreme and we hedged toward the middle because a neighbouring literature, working in a different
regime, reported milder effects. Where we trusted the mechanism instead --- the $80.4$-point gap,
predicted to the decimal --- the intervals hit.

\emph{Mode 2 --- registering a contrast that could not exist.} Two misses (optimization noise,
predicted $+2.0$; marginalization-beats-augmentation, predicted $+15$) assumed a nuisance was
present and then registered a difference between two ways of handling it. Both nuisances turned
out to be null, so neither contrast \emph{could} have appeared at any magnitude.

\paragraph{The CIFAR-10 arm was registered with those lessons applied, and is the cleaner test.}
Seventeen intervals were frozen against a corpus that did not yet have a single decoded cell,
after an explicit decision to register \emph{its own} magnitudes rather than inherit the MNIST
ones --- because scoring the FashionMNIST arm against MNIST magnitudes had already produced one
spurious ``miss'' that only careful write-up caught. (That failure mode is now blocked by the
analysis script rather than by prose: an arm with no registration of its own prints
\emph{not scored}.) \input{tables/cifar_scoreline}

The two misses are the informative ones and they are the paper's finding, not a footnote to it:
$f(\mathrm{W5})$ came in at half its registered value, and the bracket claim ``W10 lands between
W4 and W5'' broke \emph{upward} because W10 overtook W5. Both are the crossover of
\S\ref{sec:crossover}, seen from the registration's point of view. Meanwhile the probability call
that ran on the encoding's algebra rather than on extrapolation --- $f(\mathrm{W10})$ would rise
with the output-channel count --- resolved correctly.

\paragraph{S4e, and a failure mode neither mode covers.} The identifiability hunt registered nine
intervals and scored \textbf{7/9}. Its two misses are one event: the best functional residual at
$n=2$ came in at $5.9\times10^{-8}$ against a registered $3\times10^{-4}\,[10^{-6}, 3\times10^{-3}]$,
and the orbit residual there at $1.2\times10^{-7}$ against $0.35\,[0.05, 0.65]$ --- because the
pilot that informed both predictions used $n=32$ restarts and never sampled the global basin, while
the confirmatory run at $n=128$ did. Those rows were flagged \emph{pilot-informed} in the ledger
before the run for exactly this reason, and they are the argument for that flag: an under-powered
pilot mispredicts the powered run, and in this case in the direction that made the result better.

S4e also exposed a third failure mode, which is about \emph{criteria} rather than point
predictions: a pre-registered threshold can be under-specified in a way only data reveals
(\S\ref{sec:s4e}, the missing absolute floor). The lesson we take is that a registered criterion
should be checked against its instrument's resolution at registration time, and that is now part of
the template.

Running coverage across the program is $30/40 = 75\%$ against a nominal $80\%$.

We report all of this because the alternative, reporting the hits, would misrepresent how much of
the final story was anticipated.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
% =====================================================================================

\paragraph{Weight-space architectures.} DWSNets \citep{navon2023dws}, NFN/NFT
\citep{zhou2023nfn,zhou2023nft}, graph metanetworks \citep{lim2024gmn,kofinas2024graph},
universal neural functionals \citep{zhou2024unf} and ScaleGMN \citep{kalogeropoulos2024scalegmn}
build permutation --- and sometimes sign or scaling --- equivariance into the reader.
\citet{tran2024monomial} classify monomial-matrix symmetries and prove maximality among linear
actions. Our contribution is orthogonal and prior: we identify the group exactly for periodic
activations, including its \emph{affine} phase component that no linear classification can see,
and then measure how much of the empirical difficulty that group actually accounts for.

\paragraph{Symmetry, alignment and canonicalization.} Git Re-Basin \citep{ainsworth2023rebasin}
and the linear-mode-connectivity line \citep{entezari2022lmc} align networks by permutation for
merging; our $\calign$ generalizes the assignment step to $\Dinf \wr S_n$ using the Hungarian
algorithm \citep{kuhn1955hungarian}. Learned canonicalization \citep{kaba2023canonicalization} and
frame averaging \citep{puny2022frame} are the standard principled alternatives; W9 is frame
averaging measured in this setting, and it recovers nothing. \citet{dym2024impossibility} give the
general impossibility of continuous canonicalization, of which Proposition~\ref{prop:po5} is the
sine instance. \citet{lim2024empirical} remove symmetries by \emph{architecture}; we remove them
by canonicalization on a fixed architecture, which is the complementary intervention and the one
that leaves the function untouched.

\paragraph{Concurrent 2026 work.} Three preprints bear directly on results here and are cited by
identifier, since our engagement with them is through the close-read memos released with this code
rather than through published versions: arXiv:2602.01083 proves approximation-theoretic
universality for permutation-invariant weight-space networks, of which
Proposition~\ref{prop:po6} is the exact, identifiability-based counterpart for the full sine
group; arXiv:2604.23720 supplies the maximal-symmetry-group vocabulary in which
Theorem~\ref{thm:po2} is stated; and arXiv:2605.08281 reports that in a meta-learned regime the
bias column is a causal readout route while weight-space geometry does not predict reader accuracy
--- a dissociation adversarial to the mechanism of \S\ref{sec:mechanism}, and named as such in our
pre-registrations rather than discovered afterwards.

\paragraph{INR corpora and the shared-initialization question.} inr2vec \citep{deluigi2023inr2vec}
relies on shared-initialization corpora; \citet{papa2024howtotrain} intervene on fit
hyperparameters and observe downstream deltas across a large benchmark. Their shared-versus-random
comparison is one rung-pair of our ladder (W1 vs W3), and we replicate it seed-for-seed as a
pre-registered anchor before decomposing it. \citet{shamsian2024augmentations} treat the nuisance
by augmentation; W6 measures that treatment against an exact alternative and finds it recovers
$5\%$ where reframing recovers $63\%$. Approaches that avoid the nuisance by construction ---
functa \citep{dupont2022functa}, hypernetwork and meta-learned regimes --- change the
data-generating process, and are reference points rather than rungs.

\section{Limitations and open problems}\label{sec:limits}
% =====================================================================================

\textbf{Signal complexity is confounded with two other things.} CIFAR-10 differs from the
grayscale corpora in image statistics, in output channel count ($c=3$ vs $c=1$) and in fit budget
($1000$ vs $300$ steps). We ruled out the third directly (\S\ref{sec:crossover}: parameter travel
is indistinguishable across all three), and the second is the mechanism our registration named for
W10's rise --- but the first two remain entangled, and separating them needs a $c=1$ natural-image
corpus (grayscale CIFAR) or a $c=3$ simple one. That is one corpus generation, and it is the next
experiment we would run.

\textbf{Identifiability is proved only at $L=1$, and every experiment here is $L=2$.} This remains
the program's weakest theoretical link: Theorem~\ref{thm:po2}'s guarantee and our corpora do not
meet. S4e (\S\ref{sec:s4e}) closed the empirical half of the question --- the conjecture survives
with positive evidence at $n=2$ and no counterexample --- but not the proof: the deep case still
reduces to a Bessel--CP tensor decomposition with two open lemmas. S4e's own contribution to the
limitation is that it shows the question cannot be settled empirically at production width, so the
remaining route is analytic. \textbf{Depth $\ge 3$ invariants} need a Gram per successive layer and the
parity bookkeeping compounds; whether a finite family stays separating is unknown.
\textbf{Eigenvalue pooling in W10 is deliberately lossy}, and the $0.269$-versus-$0.628$ span
against $\calign$ bounds --- but does not identify --- what it discards. \textbf{The reader is a
plain MLP with no permutation structure}, so W3 is floor-level partly by construction; this is the
intended reading of the rung, and X1 plus the canonicalization rungs are what separate ``the
reader is weak'' from ``the representation is scrambled''. \textbf{Genericity holds marginally}:
minimal parallel angles of $3\times10^{-4}$\,rad mean orbits pass close to the stratum where
identifiability genuinely fails. \textbf{All results are single-device (Apple MPS)}; a CUDA
replication of one headline table is owed. \textbf{Widths are 32--64}; the Hungarian assignment is
$O(n^3)$ per layer and an amortized or Sinkhorn path is required before this scales to width 1024.

% =====================================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% =====================================================================================

The weight-space perception gap is mostly, but not entirely, symmetry. For periodic-activation
networks the responsible group is $\Dinf\wr S_n$ --- exactly, provably, and maximally at one
hidden layer --- and the best exact, function-preserving treatment of it recovers $63\%$, $66\%$
and $53\%$ of the gap on MNIST, FashionMNIST and CIFAR-10. Because those transformations preserve
every network's function exactly, the numbers are certified lower bounds on the symmetry share,
and their complements bound how much of the gap is instead a property of \emph{where independent
fits land}. The tools the field most often applies to this problem
--- group augmentation, marginalization over refits, frame averaging --- never exceed $13\%$ on
any dataset, and stay under $6\%$ on both grayscale ones.

Two things we did not expect, and would not have found without registering our expectations first.
The symmetry-removable share is \emph{not} a constant: alignment to a fixed reference network
recovers two thirds on grayscale corpora and one third on natural images. And the ranking of the
two exact methods \emph{reverses} between them, in the direction our pre-registration predicted
from the invariant encoding's algebra rather than from its two observed values. The practical
implication is that frame choice, not reader architecture, is where the available factor of two
sits --- but which frame strategy wins is a property of the signal, so a weight-space method
evaluated on one corpus family should not be assumed to rank the same way on another.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} Corpora, canonicalizers, the invariant encoder, all ladder cells, the
frozen pre-registrations with their hashes, the adversarial review notes and the full prediction
ledger --- hits and misses --- are released with the code. Every figure and table in this paper is
regenerated by a numbered script from committed artifacts.
