# Close-read: How to Train Neural Field Representations (Papa et al., arXiv:2312.10531)

**Read:** full text (targeted at RQ structure + Table 1 + Fig 4), 2026-07-17.
**Role:** anchor A1 source; C-11 prior-art delimitation; fitter-design reference.

## What they do

JAX library (fit-a-nef) for parallel NeF fitting; hyperparameter study on downstream
classification; Neural Field Arena benchmark. Their research questions: RQ1 shared vs random
initialization; RQ2 overtraining ("NeFs that achieve excellent reconstruction quality whose
parameters, however, form poor representations"); RQ3 architecture expressivity vs representation
quality. Downstream models: Kofinas-style neural-graph MPNN variant (permutation-respecting).

## Findings relevant to us

- **Table 1 (A1 calibration):** shared init beats random init across 110 neural datasets; e.g.
  MicroImageNet 0.14→0.20 (+40% rel.), ShapeNet 0.32→0.71 (+120% rel.). Direction robust; absolute
  values depend on their graph-net reader. Our A1 anchor (MNIST, matched MLP, >10 pts) is a
  different reader/dataset — deltas will be documented, direction is the binding part.
- **Fig 4:** pairwise distances of NeF representations grow with fit steps; shared init keeps
  representations grouped. This is dispersion-vs-steps — *consistent with* C-11's premise.
- **RQ2 (overtraining):** they hypothesize and test that overtraining degrades representation
  quality despite better reconstruction. **C-11's delta must therefore be stated as mechanism, not
  phenomenon:** we add (i) chaos-rate curves (divergence of δ₀-separated fits vs steps), (ii)
  post-alignment dispersion (is the growth symmetry/basin or genuine?), (iii) basin counts vs
  steps, (iv) the canonicalization interaction (does c_align undo overtraining damage?). The bare
  "more steps can hurt downstream accuracy" claim is theirs.
- Fitter design: their library validates large-batch parallel fitting; our A.1 batched-einsum
  fitter is the torch/MPS analogue (T4/T9 green; throughput profiled at G1).
