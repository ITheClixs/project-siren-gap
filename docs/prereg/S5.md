## 1. What is being adjudicated

PO-6 proves that a **complete** $G$-invariant of the weights factors through the realised function.
Its corollary is the uncomfortable one:

> A complete-invariant weight-space perceiver receives exactly the information of a function-space
> perceiver. Any advantage of weight access must be **computational** — amortization: canonicalize
> once, decode many times — or must come from deliberately **incomplete** invariants.

That is a claim about the whole field's justification, and this program has been asserting it since
G2 on the strength of a proof alone. S5 measures it. Three access models, one corpus, one accounting:

| access | mechanism | nuisance exposure |
|---|---|---|
| **function-query** | learned probe coordinates; classify $f_\theta$ evaluated there | none: T14 certifies exact $G$-invariance |
| **render** | the same at every grid point (rung P1's information, priced) | none |
| **weight** | the ladder's rungs W3/W4/W5/W10/W11 | the whole perception gap |

FLOPs are **analytic**, not wall-clock (`sirengap.eval.flops`; one MAC $=2$ FLOPs, transcendentals
at 1, per-INR at inference), so the frontier reproduces on any machine and can be audited line by
line. Wall-clock on one throttling laptop would not survive review and is not used.

## 2. The two regimes PO-6 names, priced separately

* **Single task.** Total per INR = preprocessing + reader.
* **Amortized over $T$ tasks.** Weight access may canonicalize once and reuse: $C + T\cdot D_w$.
  Function-query with *learned* probes cannot, because the probes are task-specific:
  $T\cdot(K\cdot\text{siren} + D_f)$. This is the field's actual case and it is priced, not waved at.

An analytic observation, registered here because it is a prediction and not an afterthought: with a
first-layer decoder width $W$ and output channels $c$, weight access reads a $P$-dimensional vector
at cost $\approx 2PW$ while function-query reads $Kc$ dimensions at cost
$\approx 2KcW + K\cdot\text{siren}$. **Function access should therefore be cheaper whenever
$Kc \ll P$**, largely independently of INR scale. We expect this to be the dominant effect and it
is what H-S5-6 tests.

effect. It is registered because the comparison that matters is against weight access's **80.4-point**
swing between the same two protocols.

## 4. Probability calls

- **P-S5-A = 0.85.** Function-query at some $K \le 64$ beats the best weight-access rung on
  `P-random` (W5, $64.41$) **at strictly lower FLOPs per INR** — i.e. weight access is dominated on
  both axes in the single-task regime. If this holds, PO-6's corollary is not just proved but
  *binding in practice*, and the paper must say so plainly.
- **P-S5-B = 0.30.** There exists $T \le 100$ at which amortized weight access (W5) overtakes
  function-query on total FLOPs. Registered low: the per-task decoder cost of weight access is
  itself larger, so amortizing the preprocessing may never be enough.
- **P-S5-C = 0.60.** Learned probes beat frozen probes at $K=16$ by more than 2 points.

## 5. What each outcome licenses (pre-committed wording)

- **P-S5-A holds.** The honest statement is that on these corpora, at this scale, **weight access is
  dominated by function access on both accuracy and compute in the single-task regime.** The
  program's own weight-space contributions (the canonicalizers, the invariant encoding, W11b) are
  then to be presented as what they are: the best available treatment of a representation one
  should think twice before choosing — plus the amortized regime, if P-S5-B rescues it.
- **P-S5-A fails.** Weight access is competitive; PO-6's corollary is proved but not binding at this
  scale, and the paper says why (probes need more queries than the theory suggests).
- **Either way**, the frontier figure ships with both regimes and with the analytic accounting
  released, and no comparison is made without stating the decoder input dimension that drives it.

**This registration is adversarial to the program's own subject matter.** The prediction is that
the thing this thesis studies is, at this scale, the wrong tool. That is registered in advance
precisely so the result cannot be softened afterwards.

## 6. Exclusions, controls, compute

Frozen decoder shape for every access model ($\cdot \to 1024 \to 512 \to 256 \to 10$), so the reader
is matched and only the *input* differs. Same splits, same optimiser schedule, 5 seeds for the
headline $K$ sweep. Controls: the protocol comparison (H-S5-7) and the frozen-probe ablation
(H-S5-8), which separates "querying works" from "learning *where* to query works". The INR
parameters are frozen throughout and T14 asserts they receive no gradient.

**Budget:** a 3-epoch probe measured $3$–$5$\,s per configuration; the full sweep at $\le100$ epochs
with patience 10 is estimated $\le30$ min. **Stopping rule:** if the sweep exceeds 3 h, $K=256$ drops
to 3 seeds and the reduced $n$ is reported.

**Pre-freeze exposure — declared.** A 3-epoch smoke run was executed and seen: $K=4$ gave $16.54$,
$K=16$ gave $48.87$, the protocol control gave $+6.20$ points, and the frozen ablation gave
$+8.67$. All are far from converged (3 of $\le100$ epochs) but they anchor the predictions above,
which are flagged `pilot-informed`.
