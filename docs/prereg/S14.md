# S14 Pre-registration — does the output-channel count explain W12's crossover? — FROZEN

**Study:** S14. Ledger rows `H-S14-*`, `P-S14-*`. Written before either ablation arm is run.
This is the seed of a follow-up rather than a section of the present paper: the paper reports the
observation and this registration, and the mechanism is settled properly at a scale where the
answer is worth its own study.

---

## 1. The observation

W12 trails ScaleGMN on both grayscale benchmarks and beats it on the only RGB one:

| corpus | $c$ | W12 | ScaleGMN | gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MNIST-INR | 1 | 93.08 | 96.57 | $-3.49$ |
| FashionMNIST-INR | 1 | 75.15 | 80.46 | $-5.31$ |
| CIFAR-10-INR | 3 | 38.16 | 36.43 | $\mathbf{+1.73}$ |

Our own luminance arm isolates $c$ directly, with the same images, architecture and fit budget on
both sides: W12 scores $0.965$ at $c=3$ against $0.826$ at $c=1$, a difference of $+0.139$ in
recovery from the channel count alone.

## 2. The candidate mechanism, and why it is not yet established

The $(1,1)$ block of the feature map is $\mathrm{cat}[\sin b_2,\ u]$, of width $1+c$. It is the
only block whose width grows with the output dimension, and under the grading a non-neutral block
reaches the head only through the even products the bilinear rounds form. So a wider $(1,1)$ gives
the graded reader more to route, and $c$ controls that width: two channels at $c=1$, four at $c=3$.

What the observation cannot distinguish is **block width** from **colour information**. RGB INRs
carry more information about the image than luminance ones, quite apart from where that
information sits in the grading.

## 3. Design: three arms, one corpus

All on our own `cifar10` corpus ($c=3$), frozen W12 configuration, $n=5$ seeds, nothing else
changed.

| arm | $u$ into the $(1,1)$ block | width | colour information |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** (baseline, already run) | all three channels | 4 | full |
| **B** collapse | channel mean, one channel | 2 | reduced |
| **C** pad | three copies of the channel mean | 4 | reduced |

**B versus C** varies width at fixed information. **A versus C** varies information at fixed
width. T20 asserts the arms differ in exactly the intended way and that every other block,
including the $\|u\|^2$ energy in the neutral block, is byte-identical across them.

## 4. Registered quantities

Written as recovery fractions against the `cifar10` anchors, where arm A scores $0.965$.

| id | quantity | point | 80% interval | reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H-S14-1 | $f(\mathrm{B})$, collapse | 0.86 | [0.72, 0.95] | should fall toward the luminance arm's 0.826 if either factor matters |
| H-S14-2 | $f(\mathrm{C})$, pad | 0.89 | [0.75, 0.97] | keeps width, loses colour |
| H-S14-3 | $f(\mathrm{C}) - f(\mathrm{B})$, the **width** effect at fixed information | 0.03 | [$-0.02$, $+0.10$] | we expect width to matter less than information |
| H-S14-4 | $f(\mathrm{A}) - f(\mathrm{C})$, the **information** effect at fixed width | 0.07 | [$0.00$, $+0.16$] | the residual of the $+0.139$ luminance difference |

**Probability calls.**
- **P-S14-A = 0.70.** The information effect (H-S14-4) exceeds the width effect (H-S14-3).
- **P-S14-B = 0.35.** The width effect is at least $0.05$, i.e. the grading's block structure is
  materially responsible rather than incidental.
- **P-S14-C = 0.60.** Arm B lands within $0.05$ of the luminance arm's $0.826$, i.e. collapsing the
  channels reproduces what actually fitting grayscale images does.

## 5. What the outcomes license (pre-committed)

- **If the width effect dominates**, the crossover is a property of *our grading* rather than of
  the data, and the paper's suggestion that W12 suits multi-channel signals is restated as a
  statement about the construction's block widths. That is the interesting outcome and the one
  that would justify the follow-up.
- **If the information effect dominates**, RGB corpora simply carry more for any reader to use,
  the crossover is not evidence about the grading, and we say so plainly. W12's CIFAR win over
  ScaleGMN then needs a different explanation, which we would not have.
- **If both are within noise of zero**, the channel count is not the mechanism at all and the
  luminance difference has some other cause. The observation stays in the paper as unexplained.
- **In every branch** this is reported as a single-corpus, single-dataset probe. It cannot settle
  why ScaleGMN wins on grayscale, only whether channel count plausibly explains our side of it.
