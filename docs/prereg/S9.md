## 1. Why

The review asked for a reader that handles the phase symmetry explicitly, and named the route:

> A promising route is to replace raw bias $b$ by the phasor $(\cos b, \sin b)$, which removes the
> integer $2\pi$ translation. The remaining sign and reflection actions can then be represented by
> finite-dimensional linear transformations.

This program has two readers already and neither is that object. **W11a** is permutation-equivariant
and deliberately blind to $D_\infty$. **W11b** is $G$-invariant, but only because it consumes W10's
fixed invariant family — it inherits whatever that family discards, which is exactly the limitation
we report. W12 is the third thing: invariance obtained on the **raw parameters**.

## 2. The construction (`src/sirengap/models/phasor.py`)

Under $g_{d,j}$ the phasor coordinates of the bias transform with $j$ only through its **parity**,
so replacing $b$ by $(\cos b, \sin b)$ turns $\Z \rtimes \Z_2$ into a finite $\Z_2\times\Z_2$ acting
by signs. Writing a *character* $\chi = (a,c)$ for a feature picking up $(-1)^{ad+cj}$:

| $\chi$ | layer-1 features | layer-2 features |
|---|---|---|
| $(0,0)$ | $\|w_i\|^2$, $\cos 2b_i$, $\sum_k (W^2_{ki})^2$, $1$ | $\|w^2_k\|^2$, $\|u_k\|^2$, $\cos 2b^2_k$, $1$ |
| $(1,0)$ | $w_i$, $\sin 2b_i$ | $\sin 2b^2_k$ |
| $(0,1)$ | $\cos b_i$ | $\cos b^2_k$ |
| $(1,1)$ | $\sin b_i$ | $\sin b^2_k$, $u_k$ |

Every layer preserves the grading: linear maps act within a character, products add characters, the
nonlinearity is odd off the neutral block, and only $(0,0)$ channels reach the head. $W^2$ couples
the layers with character $(1,1)$ on the layer-1 side and $(1,0)$ on the layer-2 side, which leaves
exactly two legitimate message channels per direction — the Gram coupling of Prop.~10 rediscovered
as a message rule, but neither pooled nor fixed.

**Capacity by rule**: the width minimising $|\text{params} - 1{,}873{,}162|$, the frozen decoder's
count, as `S1-w11.md` fixed for W11a/W11b. On MNIST that is width 186, $1{,}874{,}898$ parameters,
$+0.09\%$.

**Verified, not asserted** (T16): logits move by $<10^{-4}$ relative under random group elements
including windings up to $|j| = 40$ — far outside anything a fitted network occupies, because the
phasor quotients the translation exactly rather than approximately. The feature scaling is
scale-only and is itself $G$-invariant, since a shift would destroy every character.

## 4. What the outcomes license (pre-committed)

- **If P-S9-C resolves TRUE**, this paper's practical claim — that within weight space the choice of
  orbit representative matters more than the reader architecture — is **withdrawn**, not qualified.
  It is stated here in advance so that outcome cannot be absorbed as a footnote.
- **If P-S9-A resolves FALSE**, the $\Dinf$ component has no architectural payoff at this capacity
  and the paper must say that the group's mathematical necessity does not translate into reader
  design, alongside the S6 finding that its affine part is empirically minor.
- **If W12 lands between W11a and W5**, the reported statement is the ordered one: permutation-only
  $<$ $G$-aware $<$ frame choice, at matched capacity on this corpus, with the ordering and not the
  levels as the claim.
- **Void condition.** If T16's invariance check fails at the production width (relative logit move
  $> 10^{-4}$ under $|j|\le 3$ elements), the run is discarded rather than reported, because a reader
  that is only approximately invariant is not the object this study is about.
