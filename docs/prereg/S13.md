## 1. Why this is the deciding experiment

The published weight-space literature measures INR classification on the corpus of
\citet{navon2023dws} — the same corpus DWSNets, NFN, NG-GNN and ScaleGMN all report on. Its INRs
have the architecture our readers already take: two hidden layers of width 32, 2-D input, 1-D
output, sine. So W12 can be run on it unchanged, and the result is directly comparable to numbers
other groups have published rather than to baselines we chose.

**Published test accuracies, MNIST-INR** (quoted from ScaleGMN, Table 1):

| method | MNIST | F-MNIST | CIFAR-10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| DWS | 85.71 ± 0.57 | 67.06 ± 0.29 | 34.45 ± 0.42 |
| NFN$_{NP}$ | 78.50 ± 0.23 | 68.19 ± 0.28 | 33.41 ± 0.01 |

| ScaleGMN-B | 96.59 ± 0.24 | 80.78 ± 0.16 | 38.82 ± 0.10 |

## 2. A fact established on import, before any prediction below

The benchmark corpus is **independently initialized**. Median pairwise relative parameter distance
over a random sample of 64 of its INRs is **1.419** (p10 1.339, p90 1.487), against **1.40** for
our own `P-random` and **0.19** for our shared-initialization corpus.

This matters for how this paper reads its own results. We have described the shared-versus-random
contrast as exposing an artifact of corpus construction, and it does; but the field's main
benchmark is already in the hard regime, so the published leaderboard is not measured on the easy
one. Our raw-weight rung reaches $13.9\%$ there while these methods reach $78$–$97\%$, which means
their architectures, not their corpora, are doing that work.

## 3. Deviations from the published protocol, declared

- The official `mnist_splits.json` is not in the distributed archive. We use the train/test halves
  encoded in the directory names and carve validation deterministically out of the training half
  (54k/6k/10k). This is our split, not theirs, and small differences are expected from it.
- Our decoder training schedule is the one frozen for W12 in S9, not any schedule tuned here.
- We report five seeds, as everywhere else in this program.

## 5. What the outcomes license (pre-committed)

- **If W12 beats ScaleGMN**, the paper has a competitive architecture contribution and should be
  submitted as one, with this table in the abstract.
- **If W12 lands between NG-GNN and ScaleGMN**, the architecture is respectable but not leading.
  The paper is a measurement paper with a good reader, the abstract says so, and the reader is
  reported as competitive-but-behind with the gap quoted.
- **If W12 lands below DWS**, the architectural claim is withdrawn from the abstract entirely. W12
  is then reported as an instrument built to test what quotienting the group buys, not as a
  contribution to the leaderboard, and the paper's contribution is the measurement programme and
  the group characterization alone.
- **In every branch** the published numbers are quoted in the paper next to ours, and the split
  deviation of §3 is stated wherever the comparison appears. We do not tune W12 against this
  benchmark: one run, the frozen configuration, whatever it gives.
