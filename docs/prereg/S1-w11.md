## 1. Why this rung exists

Every rung of the ladder so far changes the **feature map** and holds the **reader** fixed at the
matched MLP. That is what makes the decomposition interpretable — and it is also the paper's
biggest exposed flank, because the field does not read weights with a plain MLP. It builds
permutation-equivariant architectures (DWSNets, NFN/NFT, graph metanetworks, ScaleGMN,
Monomial-NFN). The paper's central practical claim is:

> frame choice, not reader architecture, is where the available factor of two sits.

That claim has never been tested against a reader architecture. W11 tests it.

It also closes the open half of OPEN_PROBLEMS #4. W10 is exactly $G$-invariant but pools its
invariant matrices into **sorted eigenvalue spectra**, which is deliberately lossy; the note there
asks whether that pooling, rather than the invariants, is what limits W10 to $f = 0.269$–$0.534$.

## 2. Two variants, answering two questions

| variant | reader | equivariance | question |
|---|---|---|---|
| **W11a** `RawGraphReader` | bipartite message passing on raw $(W^1,b^1)$, $W^2$ as edges, $(b^2, u)$ | $S_{n_1}\!\times\!S_{n_2}$ only; **not** $\Dinf$ | does reader architecture substitute for frame choice? |
| **W11b** `InvariantGraphReader` | W10's node scalars and the matrices $A,B,C$ as edges, with **learned** equivariant pooling | full product group | is W10's eigenvalue pooling the bottleneck, or the invariants? |

W11a's *lack* of $\Dinf$ invariance is the point, not an oversight: it is the coverage the cited
family actually has for sine networks, whose phase generators are affine and outside every
monomial-matrix action. T13 asserts both properties, including a negative control that W11a must
**not** be $\Dinf$-invariant.

**Corpus and protocol.** `P-random` (the realistic setting), same splits, same 5 seeds, same
optimiser schedule as the frozen decoder (AdamW $10^{-3}$, cosine, $\le100$ epochs, batch 512,
early stop on the INR validation split, patience 10). Reported on MNIST first; extension to the
other corpora is a separate decision and is **not** registered here.

**Capacity is matched, by rule.** W11 breaks the matched-reader control by construction, so the
comparison would be worthless if the readers were smaller. Widths are chosen by the rule *"closest
parameter count to the frozen decoder's 1{,}873{,}162"*, giving width 424 for W11a
(1{,}879{,}874, $+0.4\%$) and width 288 for W11b (1{,}845{,}210, $-1.5\%$). Neither width is a free
parameter and neither was tuned on accuracy.

**Pre-freeze exposure — declared.** A smoke run at width 32, one seed, was executed and seen:
W11a $30.91$ ($f = 0.210$), W11b $44.96$ ($f = 0.385$). That is a $40$–$60\times$ smaller reader
than the registered configuration, so it bounds nothing from above, but it is what the predictions
below are anchored on and the rows are flagged `pilot-informed`.

## 4. The claims this can break, stated in advance

- **P-W11-A = 0.75.** $f(\mathrm{W11a}) < f(\mathrm{W5})$ — an equivariant reader on raw weights
  does **not** reach what an exact change of frame reaches. If this fails, the paper's central
  practical claim is wrong and §12's "frame choice, not reader architecture" must be withdrawn.
- **P-W11-B = 0.70.** $f(\mathrm{W11b}) > f(\mathrm{W10})$ — learned equivariant pooling beats
  sorted eigenvalue spectra on the same invariants, i.e. the pooling is *part* of the bottleneck.
- **P-W11-C = 0.30.** $f(\mathrm{W11b}) > f(\mathrm{W5})$ — the invariant reader overtakes
  alignment. Registered low: if it holds, the practical recommendation inverts to "build the
  equivariant invariant reader, do not align", and the crossover story of §9 gains a third regime.

## 5. What each outcome licenses (pre-committed wording)

- **W11a well below W5 (the registered expectation).** The paper's claim stands and gains its first
  test against a reader architecture. The honest statement is bounded: *one* equivariant family, at
  matched capacity, on one corpus — not "equivariance cannot work".
- **W11a at or above W5.** The claim is **withdrawn**, not softened, exactly as the §9 conjecture
  was on the luminance arm. The decomposition results are unaffected (they are statements about
  feature maps); what dies is the practical recommendation drawn from them.
- **W11b above W10 but below W5.** OPEN_PROBLEMS #4 is answered with a split: pooling accounts for
  the difference $f(\mathrm{W11b}) - f(\mathrm{W10})$, and the residual against W5 is attributable
  to the invariants' incompleteness. Both numbers are reported, neither is rounded into a story.
- **W11b at or below W10.** Learned pooling buys nothing; the invariants themselves are the limit,
  and OPEN_PROBLEMS #4 closes in the other direction.

**In every case** the reader parameter counts and per-seed wallclock are reported beside the
accuracies, and no comparison is made to a rung whose reader capacity differs by more than $10\%$
without saying so in the same sentence.

## 6. Exclusions and compute

No row-wise INR exclusion. A seed whose validation accuracy fails to exceed 15\% is re-run at
seed$+1000$ and flagged; more than one such collapse invalidates the cell.

**Budget, measured before freezing** (three-epoch probe at the registered widths, then discarded):
$19$\,s/epoch for W11a and $26$\,s/epoch for W11b, so five seeds at a typical $\approx35$ epochs is
$\approx55$ min and $\approx76$ min respectively, $\approx2.2$ h in total. **Stopping rule:** if a
single variant exceeds 3 h for 5 seeds, that variant drops to 3 seeds and the reduced $n$ is
reported in the table rather than hidden. The probe's accuracies (W11a $31.17$, W11b $46.87$ after
three epochs, both far from converged) are declared here alongside the width-32 pilot of §2.
